# Sulforaphane deep dive

Sulforaphane is the best-studied food-derived activator of the body's own antioxidant defences. This page goes deeper than the summary in `diet.md` and `foundations.md`. It covers how sulforaphane works, how much of it actually reaches your blood, whether stacking sources makes sense, every human trial we could verify (positive, null and negative), fertility, safety and practical preparation. Every citation was checked against its PubMed record in this session, and none of the cited papers is retracted.

## Bottom line

- **Mechanism is solid.** Sulforaphane switches on the KEAP1-Nrf2 pathway, which raises glutathione and a battery of detoxifying and antioxidant enzymes. In humans this has been shown directly: airway cells make more of these enzymes after broccoli sprouts, and people excrete more detoxified air pollutants [sfn-riedl2009, sfn-egner2014, sfn-chen2019].
- **Delivery decides everything.** Sulforaphane is made only when the plant enzyme myrosinase meets glucoraphanin. Raw sprouts or seed powder with active enzyme convert roughly 30-70% of their glucoraphanin. Cooked broccoli, or glucoraphanin pills without enzyme, often convert under 10%, and the figure varies 40-fold between people [sfn-fahey2012, sfn-egner2011, sfn-vermeulen2008, sfn-mastaloudis2026].
- **Clinical evidence is mixed.** The clearest human effects are on detox biomarkers in polluted Qidong, China. Smaller trials show modest benefits in obese people with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes, H. pylori load, liver enzymes, some autism and schizophrenia symptoms, and a prostate cancer marker. Many other trials are null: COPD, asthma, prediabetes (primary endpoint), young children with autism, schizophrenia cognition, and several cancer biomarker studies [sfn-axelsson2017, sfn-wise2016, sfn-dwibedi2025, sfn-magner2023, sfn-kassar2025]. About half of registered trials have never been published [sfn-saito2025].
- **Saturation.** Absorption does not saturate across food-relevant doses, and there is no tolerance over 12 weeks. The biological response, however, appears to have a threshold at low doses and is capped at high doses. If you already take an effective sulforaphane dose, cooked broccoli adds little extra Nrf2 activation; raw broccoli adds a meaningful second dose; and broccoli's fibre and other nutrients are never redundant. Details below.
- **Fertility.** There are no human trials. All the evidence is from animals and lab dishes, so we grade it mechanistic.
- **Safety at food and trial doses is good.** The main problem is stomach upset. There is no thyroid signal in trials. The theoretical concerns are high-dose, long-term Nrf2 activation (especially with cancer) and enzyme induction affecting some drugs.

## 1. How sulforaphane works

### The KEAP1 switch

Sulforaphane was isolated from broccoli in 1992 as the most potent inducer of "phase 2" detoxification enzymes in the vegetable. Unlike many carcinogens, it does not induce the phase 1 cytochrome P450 enzymes that can activate toxins [sfn-zhang1992]. Three-day-old sprouts contain 10-100 times more of its precursor, glucoraphanin, than mature broccoli does [sfn-fahey1997].

Nrf2 is a transcription factor. Under normal conditions it is made constantly and destroyed constantly: its partner KEAP1 acts as an adaptor for a ubiquitin ligase, so Nrf2 has a half-life of under 20 minutes [sfn-kobayashi2004]. KEAP1 carries reactive cysteine residues that act as sensors. Sulforaphane is a mild electrophile (an isothiocyanate). It forms reversible adducts with these cysteines, and the speed with which an inducer reacts with thiols tracks its potency [sfn-dinkovakostova2002]. Cysteine 151 is among the residues sulforaphane modifies most readily; an early mass-spectrometry study that missed it had stripped the reversible adduct off during sample preparation [sfn-hu2011]. Once KEAP1 is modified, newly made Nrf2 escapes destruction, enters the nucleus and binds antioxidant response elements (AREs).

### What gets switched on

The Nrf2 target genes include NQO1, heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), multiple glutathione S-transferases (GSTs), the two subunits of glutamate-cysteine ligase (the rate-limiting enzyme for glutathione), and thioredoxin-system enzymes. In human nasal cells, GSTM1, GSTP1, NQO1 and HO-1 rose with increasing doses of broccoli sprout homogenate, and alfalfa sprouts did nothing [sfn-riedl2009]. This is why sulforaphane is called an "indirect antioxidant". It scavenges almost no radicals itself; it raises long-lived enzymes that work catalytically. Of the many targets proposed for sulforaphane, only KEAP1-Nrf2 is considered validated [sfn-dinkovakostova2017]. Compared with curcumin, silymarin or resveratrol, sulforaphane is both a more potent Nrf2 inducer and far more bioavailable [sfn-houghton2016].

### Secondary actions: NF-kB and HDAC

- **NF-kB.** In macrophages, sulforaphane cut the inflammatory mediators NO, PGE2 and TNF-alpha. It did this by blocking NF-kB binding to DNA, not by stopping NF-kB from reaching the nucleus. Thiol modification is the likely mechanism [sfn-heiss2001]. This is a cell-culture finding; human inflammation results are mixed (section 4).
- **HDAC inhibition.** A single 68 g serving of broccoli sprouts inhibited histone deacetylase (HDAC) activity in human blood cells at 3 and 6 hours [sfn-myzak2007]. Sprouts, but not an enzyme-free supplement, lowered blood-cell HDAC activity [sfn-clarke2011b]. A glucoraphanin supplement lowered blood-cell HDAC activity in women before breast biopsy [sfn-atwell2015b]. But 200 µmol/day did not change HDAC activity in men before prostate biopsy [sfn-zhang2020], and doubling or splitting a 200 µmol dose showed no dose response [sfn-atwell2015a]. HDAC inhibition is real but small and inconsistent in people.

### Why "more" is not simply "better"

Nrf2 responses are built to be modest and self-limiting. Calabrese and Kozumbo describe Nrf2 as the mediator of hormetic responses, with a quantitatively constrained, modest maximal stimulation [sfn-calabrese2021]. In cell culture, sulforaphane at 1-5 µM *increased* proliferation and migration of several human cell lines, including cancer cells, while 10-40 µM inhibited them [sfn-bao2014]. Biomarkers that capture the human pharmacodynamic response remain poorly validated [sfn-yagishita2020], and drug developers still face open questions about specificity and safety [sfn-cuadrado2019].

## 2. Pharmacokinetics

### Making sulforaphane: plant enzyme vs gut bacteria

Glucoraphanin is inert. It must be cut by myrosinase, which sits in separate compartments in the plant and meets its substrate only when tissue is crushed (chewing, chopping, blending). If myrosinase has been destroyed by cooking or processing, bacteria in the colon can do the job. That route has two problems:

- **It is inefficient and wildly variable.** Single doses of glucoraphanin converted from about 1% to over 40% in different people, in both rural Chinese and Baltimore volunteers. Each person's rate was fairly consistent on repeat testing. Antibiotics and bowel cleansing abolish it. Preformed sulforaphane converted uniformly at 70-90% [sfn-fahey2012].
- **It depends on specific microbes.** A gene cluster (BT2159-BT2156) lets the common gut bacterium *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* activate glucosinolates [sfn-liou2020]. In the 2025 prediabetes trial, people who responded to a glucoraphanin-based extract had more of a Bacteroides regulator gene needed for conversion, and that gene's abundance tracked blood sulforaphane [sfn-dwibedi2025]. In a 2026 crossover, four bacterial conversion genes correlated with each person's conversion [sfn-mastaloudis2026].

Direct comparisons show the size of the gap:

| Form | Approximate fraction appearing as sulforaphane metabolites | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Preformed sulforaphane (beverage or extract) | ~70% (70-90%) | [sfn-egner2011, sfn-fahey2012, sfn-shapiro2006] |
| Fresh sprouts, chewed (active myrosinase) | ~60% | [sfn-cramer2012] |
| Broccoli seed extract + mustard-seed myrosinase | ~40% | [sfn-mastaloudis2026] |
| Raw broccoli | 32-37% | [sfn-conaway2000, sfn-vermeulen2008] |
| Glucoraphanin extract or seed extract, no added enzyme | ~5-20% | [sfn-egner2011, sfn-shapiro2006, sfn-mastaloudis2026] |
| Steamed/cooked broccoli | 3.4-10% | [sfn-conaway2000, sfn-vermeulen2008] |
| Myrosinase-free glucoraphanin powder | ~24% in 4 men | [sfn-cramer2012] |
| High-glucoraphanin broccoli soup (enzyme denatured) | 2-15% | [sfn-sivapalan2018] |

Sprouts and seeds given with their own active myrosinase were 3-4 times more bioavailable than glucoraphanin without it, and a commercial glucoraphanin supplement performed like a boiled sprout extract [sfn-fahey2015]. A myrosinase-free broccoli supplement gave dramatically lower and later blood and urine isothiocyanates than 40 g of fresh sprouts [sfn-clarke2011a]. Even a myrosinase-treated extract gave about one-third the metabolites of fresh sprouts at 200 µmol/day [sfn-atwell2015a]. Chewing matters: thoroughly chewed sprouts gave 42 µmol of excreted metabolites versus 29 µmol when swallowed whole [sfn-shapiro2001].

Stomach acid also matters. In a pilot with a seed-and-sprout extract containing active myrosinase, conversion went up after volunteers started omeprazole (which reduces acid), and enteric coating also helped, suggesting acid partly inactivates plant myrosinase. Greater body mass predicted lower conversion [sfn-fahey2019].

### Timing: a short pulse

- After about 200 µmol of sprout isothiocyanates, blood levels peaked at about 1-2 µM within 1 hour and fell with a half-life of 1.8 hours; 58% of the dose was in urine within 8 hours, reflecting active kidney secretion [sfn-ye2002].
- From raw broccoli the plasma peak came at 1.6 hours; from cooked broccoli at 6 hours, because colonic bacteria do the work later. Excretion half-life was about 2.5 hours either way [sfn-vermeulen2008].
- Glucoraphanin without enzyme gives a slower, lower, steadier exposure; preformed sulforaphane gives a bolus [sfn-egner2011].
- Splitting 200 µmol into two 100 µmol doses 12 hours apart kept plasma levels higher later in the day [sfn-atwell2015a].
- Oral extract at 50, 100 and 200 µmol/day gave dose-dependent levels in plasma and skin; at 50 µmol, sulforaphane was not detectable in skin [sfn-tahata2018].

### Heat

Heating florets or sprouts to about 60 °C before crushing actually *raised* sulforaphane. It inactivates epithiospecifier protein, which otherwise diverts glucoraphanin to an inactive nitrile. At 70 °C and above, floret myrosinase is lost [sfn-matusheski2004]. That is why raw broccoli beats steamed broccoli about 3- to 10-fold [sfn-conaway2000, sfn-vermeulen2008].

### Genotype: GSTM1 and GSTT1

Sulforaphane is eliminated by conjugation with glutathione, partly via GSTs, and many people carry a deletion of the GSTM1 gene. The effect is not what you might expect:

- GSTM1-null people had slightly *higher* plasma sulforaphane and faster, larger urinary excretion [sfn-gasper2005].
- In 114 people given a standard broccoli meal, GST genotypes made little difference, and more GSTM1-null people were high excreters (62% vs 39%) [sfn-steck2007].
- In a Shanghai cohort, men with detectable urinary isothiocyanates had lower lung cancer risk (RR 0.65), most strongly if GSTM1-null (RR 0.36) or GSTM1/GSTT1 double-null (RR 0.28) [sfn-london2000]. This is observational.
- In Qidong, benzene detoxification was higher in GSTT1-positive people regardless of treatment arm [sfn-egner2014].

Bottom line: genotype modestly changes handling. It is not a reason to avoid or prefer sulforaphane, and no trial has shown genotype-specific clinical benefit.

### Repeated dosing: no tolerance

- With repeated 50 µmol doses of isothiocyanates, excretion varied by only 9% within and between people, and excretion was linear from 25 to 200 µmol [sfn-shapiro2001].
- Over 12 weeks of daily beverage, sulforaphane bioavailability did not decline and the detox effect held [sfn-egner2014].
- Fifteen days of daily broccoli did not uniformly change absorption: it rose in leaner people (BMI below 26) and fell in heavier people [sfn-charron2018].

## 3. The saturation question

*If someone already takes broccoli seed powder or a sulforaphane supplement, does eating broccoli or sprouts add anything?*

No trial has tested a supplement versus supplement-plus-broccoli on any outcome. A review of more than 50 sulforaphane trials stresses that dose-response shapes and a maximally effective dose are not established [sfn-yagishita2019]. So the answer has to be assembled from the following.

### Established facts

1. **Absorption does not saturate in the food range.** Excretion rises linearly from 25 to 200 µmol [sfn-shapiro2001]; plasma and skin levels climb from 50 to 200 µmol/day [sfn-tahata2018]. More intake means more sulforaphane in the body.
2. **Low doses can fall below a threshold.** In Qidong, a 10-day beverage producing a median 24.6 µmol/day of urinary sulforaphane metabolites raised benzene detoxification by 63%. Half that dose (10.3 µmol/day) gave +11% and one-fifth (4.3 µmol/day) gave -6%, neither different from placebo [sfn-chen2019].
3. **Some responses keep rising to high doses.** Nasal phase 2 enzymes rose dose-dependently up to the top dose, 200 g of sprouts [sfn-riedl2009].
4. **Others plateau or never move.** At 200 µmol/day, single versus split dosing and sprouts versus extract made no difference to HO-1, HDAC activity or p21 in blood cells [sfn-atwell2015a]. In COPD, neither 25 nor 150 µmol/day induced Nrf2 target genes in lung cells [sfn-wise2016]. Three days of 100 g sprouts did not induce antioxidant genes in nasal or blood cells of asthmatics [sfn-sudini2016].
5. **No tolerance over weeks** [sfn-egner2014, sfn-shapiro2001].
6. **Each dose is a separate pulse.** With a half-life of about 2 hours [sfn-ye2002, sfn-vermeulen2008] and Nrf2 itself turning over in minutes once the inducer is gone [sfn-kobayashi2004], a morning supplement and an evening broccoli meal give two pulses, not one bigger one.
7. **Sources add up in the gut.** Fresh sprouts plus a myrosinase-free glucoraphanin powder recovered 65% of the combined dose versus 24% for the powder alone, because the sprouts' enzyme converted the powder's glucoraphanin too [sfn-cramer2012].
8. **The response has a biological ceiling.** Nrf2 is constrained by design [sfn-calabrese2021], cell responses are biphasic [sfn-bao2014], and permanent maximal activation is harmful (section 6) [sfn-wakabayashi2003].

### What a serving of broccoli actually adds (arithmetic, not trial data)

Supermarket heads averaged 0.38 µmol glucoraphanin per gram, ranging from under 0.005 to 1.13 [sfn-yagishita2019]. A 200 g serving therefore averages about 76 µmol glucoraphanin. At 37% (raw) that is about 28 µmol absorbed sulforaphane, roughly the Qidong threshold; at 3.4% (cooked) it is about 3 µmol [sfn-vermeulen2008]; cooked with 1 g mustard powder, about 4.5 times more than cooked alone [sfn-okunade2018]. For comparison, clinical trials use 50-200 µmol/day [sfn-singh2014, sfn-axelsson2017, sfn-alumkal2015].

### Inference (reasonable but untested)

- **On top of a small dose** (a few tens of µmol, or a glucoraphanin-only pill in a poor converter): extra sprouts or raw broccoli probably push you further up the rising part of the curve, toward or past the Qidong threshold. Not wasted.
- **On top of a large dose** (100-200 µmol/day of active product): the extra Nrf2 effect of cooked broccoli is probably negligible, and raw broccoli adds a modest second pulse with uncertain marginal value, given that blood-cell markers already plateaued at 100-200 µmol [sfn-atwell2015a].
- **Broccoli is not a sulforaphane pill.** High-glucoraphanin broccoli lowered LDL cholesterol by 5-7% over 12 weeks, more than standard broccoli [sfn-armah2015]. Crucifer intake tracks lower mortality in cohorts [sfn-zhang2011, sfn-aune2017]. Mature broccoli also supplies fibre, folate and indole glucosinolates that sprouts largely lack [sfn-fahey1997], and indoles plus isothiocyanates induced ARE genes additively or synergistically in cells [sfn-saw2011]. None of that is replaced by a supplement.

### Other Nrf2 activators: additive or redundant?

- **Curcumin.** Much weaker and far less bioavailable as an Nrf2 inducer than sulforaphane [sfn-houghton2016]. In a schizophrenia meta-analysis of Nrf2 activators, neither curcumin nor sulforaphane had robust symptom effects [sfn-okazaki2026]. No human trial has combined them.
- **Coffee.** In a 29-person pilot, 750 mL/day of a specially roasted coffee for 4 weeks raised Nrf2 transcription in blood lymphocytes on average, with large individual variation [sfn-volz2012]. Coffee works through many other routes, so overlap with sulforaphane is partial at most.
- **Exercise.** Exercise is a strong physiological Nrf2 activator, but the response weakens with age. A 2026 review found the case for combining exercise with sulforaphane or curcumin promising in cells and rodents but unproven in older people [sfn-traustadottir2026]. In small trials, a sulforaphane supplement reduced muscle soreness and post-exercise MDA [sfn-komine2021], while a hot-water-reconstituted high-glucoraphanin powder (myrosinase likely inactivated) did nothing for recovery [sfn-cesanelli2026].
- **Combinations in general.** Additivity of different Nrf2 inducers has been shown only in cell culture [sfn-saw2011]. One human factorial trial of sulforaphane plus brain training found each helped processing speed alone, but the combination added nothing [sfn-nouchi2021]. That is a cognitive outcome, not Nrf2, but it is a reminder that combinations often fail to add up.

**Answer:** Sulforaphane sources are partly redundant for the Nrf2 effect once you are at an effective dose, but not fully: absorption keeps rising, each meal is its own pulse, and broccoli brings non-Nrf2 benefits. Coffee, exercise and a Mediterranean-style diet are not made redundant by sulforaphane, because they act through many other pathways. Chasing maximal Nrf2 activation with several concentrated products is not supported and may be counterproductive.

### Reading the table honestly

- **Strongest:** pollutant detoxification in Qidong. These are large, randomized, placebo-controlled trials with mass-spectrometry endpoints and a dose-finding study [sfn-egner2014, sfn-chen2019]. The endpoint is a validated exposure biomarker, not disease.
- **Promising but unreplicated or subgroup-only:** glucose control (positive only in obese poorly controlled diabetes; the prediabetes follow-up missed its target) [sfn-axelsson2017, sfn-dwibedi2025]; liver enzymes [sfn-kikuchi2015, sfn-satomi2022]; H. pylori load [sfn-yanaka2009].
- **Mixed:** autism (positive in adolescents and young men, null in young children, small pooled effect) [sfn-singh2014, sfn-magner2023, sfn-wang2025]; schizophrenia (negative symptoms in two Chinese trials, null elsewhere) [sfn-kassar2025]; cancer biomarkers [sfn-cipolla2015, sfn-zhang2020].
- **Null:** lung and airway diseases (COPD, asthma, ozone) [sfn-wise2016, sfn-sudini2016, sfn-duran2016]; blood pressure [sfn-christiansen2010]; H. pylori eradication [sfn-chang2020].

## 5. Fertility

**There are no human trials of sulforaphane, broccoli sprouts or crucifer intake on sperm quality, sperm DNA fragmentation, egg quality, ovarian reserve, PCOS outcomes or IVF results.** We found only animal and lab-dish data.

**Sperm (mechanistic only):**
- Adding 5 µM sulforaphane to human semen before freezing improved post-thaw viability, motility and morphology and lowered ROS and lipid peroxidation [sfn-valipour2021]. This applies to sperm-banking media, not to eating broccoli.
- In mice on a high-fat diet, sulforaphane injections restored spermatogenesis and fertility; in the lab it protected mouse and human sperm motility from fatty acid stress through AMPK and reduced ER stress [sfn-mu2019].
- In diabetic mice, 4 months of sulforaphane prevented testicular cell death and oxidative damage while raising testicular Nrf2 [sfn-wang2014]. In pubertal mice exposed to a phthalate, it protected sperm count, motility and testosterone [sfn-jiang2017].

**Eggs and ovaries (mechanistic only):**
- Sulforaphane reduced ROS and apoptosis in granulosa cells taken from women with PCOS during egg retrieval [sfn-taheri2021] and protected granulosa cells from healthy women against hydrogen peroxide [sfn-esfandyari2021].
- 1 µM during in vitro maturation improved bovine oocyte maturation and embryo yield under paraquat stress [sfn-feng2023], and it reduced toxin-induced spindle and mitochondrial defects in mouse oocytes [sfn-li2023].
- But bovine granulosa cells died at concentrations above about 15 µM [sfn-sohel2017], echoing the biphasic pattern seen in other cells [sfn-bao2014].

**Grade:** mechanistic, impact 1 of 5 for both sperm and egg. The only human result with indirect relevance to PCOS is the glucose benefit in obese people with poorly controlled diabetes [sfn-axelsson2017], which has not been tested in PCOS. Eating broccoli is reasonable as part of a healthy diet when trying to conceive; high-dose supplements around conception have no supporting data.

## 6. Safety

### Gastrointestinal effects

Stomach upset is the main side effect. In the 2025 prediabetes trial (150 µmol/day), loose stools, nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and reflux were more common on extract than placebo and drove most of the drop-outs [sfn-dwibedi2025]. With one stabilised sulforaphane preparation, 6 of 10 volunteers reported mild stomach upset [sfn-fahey2017]. In the pancreatic cancer pilot, 15 capsules a day were hard to swallow and sometimes increased nausea and digestive problems [sfn-lozanovski2020]. Most other trials up to 200 µmol/day reported good tolerance and no excess adverse events [sfn-tahata2018, sfn-alumkal2015, sfn-wang2025, sfn-elkhalifa2023].

### Thyroid ("goitrogen") concerns

- A 12-week trial of a sulforaphane/glucoraphanin beverage did not change TSH, free T4, thyroglobulin or thyroid autoantibodies in 45 women [sfn-chartoumpekis2019].
- A 7-day inpatient phase I study found no change in TSH, T3, T4 or liver enzymes [sfn-shapiro2006].
- A 2024 systematic review of 123 studies concluded that Brassica vegetables do not harm thyroid function in humans, especially with adequate iodine intake [sfn-galanty2024].
- The real goitrogen is goitrin, from progoitrin. It inhibits iodine uptake at 194 µmol but not 77 µmol. Collards, Brussels sprouts and some Russian kale can contain this much, but commercial broccoli and kale contain under 10 µmol per 100 g [sfn-felker2016].

### Product quality

Many products contain glucoraphanin without active myrosinase and deliver far less than sprouts [sfn-clarke2011a, sfn-fahey2015]. Free sulforaphane is unstable and must be stabilised; stabilised forms were as bioavailable as pure sulforaphane [sfn-fahey2017]. Supermarket broccoli varies more than 100-fold in glucoraphanin [sfn-yagishita2019]. Commercial label claims are rarely independently assayed, and publication bias affects the trial literature [sfn-saito2025].

### Drug interactions (mostly theoretical)

- In human liver cells, sulforaphane induced GSTs and markedly reduced expression and activity of CYP3A4, a major drug-metabolising enzyme; CYP1A2 was unaffected [sfn-maheo1997]. No human interaction study exists.
- Crucifer diets lowered serum bilirubin in a controlled feeding trial, showing induction of UGT1A1, which also conjugates some drugs [sfn-navarro2009].
- Acid-suppressing drugs increased conversion of a myrosinase-containing product [sfn-fahey2019]; that is an interaction in sulforaphane's favour.
- In people receiving chemotherapy: Nrf2 overexpression made cancer cells resistant to cisplatin, doxorubicin and etoposide [sfn-wang2008]. Discuss supplements with the oncologist.

### Chronic Nrf2 activation

- Mice that lack KEAP1 entirely, and so have permanently maximal Nrf2, die after birth from hyperkeratosis of the oesophagus and forestomach [sfn-wakabayashi2003].
- Cancer-driving genes (Kras, Braf, Myc) raise Nrf2 to protect tumour cells from ROS, and Nrf2 was needed for Kras-driven tumours in mice [sfn-denicola2011].
- The synthetic Nrf2 activator bardoxolone methyl increased heart-failure events (HR 1.83) in diabetic kidney disease, and the trial was stopped [sfn-dezeeuw2013]. Bardoxolone is far more potent and has other actions; this is not evidence against broccoli.
- Food-level sulforaphane produces transient, modest activation [sfn-kobayashi2004, sfn-calabrese2021], and in cohorts crucifer intake goes with lower, not higher, mortality and cancer risk [sfn-zhang2011, sfn-li2022]. The concern applies to long-term high-dose concentrates, particularly in people with active cancer.

## 7. Practical guide

**Fresh broccoli sprouts.** About 30-70 g a day (a loose handful up to roughly a cup) matches amounts used in trials [sfn-lopezchillon2019, sfn-clarke2011a, sfn-yanaka2009]. Eat raw and chew thoroughly [sfn-shapiro2001]. Add to food after cooking, not during. Do not blend into hot soups. Sprout glucoraphanin varies by seed source; the sprouts in one trial held about 6 µmol per gram [sfn-yanaka2009]. Raw sprouts carry a bacterial food-safety risk, so buy fresh from a reputable source or grow them hygienically.

**Broccoli seed powder.** Choose a product that states active myrosinase or an assayed glucoraphanin content. Stir into cool or room-temperature water, juice or yogurt, wait a few minutes, then consume. Aim for roughly 100-200 µmol glucoraphanin with active enzyme, which at about 30-40% conversion yields about 30-80 µmol sulforaphane [sfn-mastaloudis2026, sfn-fahey2015]. Never cook it.

**Supplements.** Prefer products with active myrosinase or stabilised sulforaphane [sfn-fahey2017, sfn-egner2011]. Trial doses were 40-200 µmol (7-35 mg) sulforaphane-equivalent per day [sfn-singh2014, sfn-axelsson2017, sfn-alumkal2015]. Splitting the dose keeps levels up longer [sfn-atwell2015a]. If you get stomach upset, lower the dose [sfn-dwibedi2025]. A glucoraphanin-only pill converts poorly and unpredictably [sfn-clarke2011a, sfn-fahey2012]; pairing it with fresh sprouts or mustard powder boosts conversion [sfn-cramer2012, sfn-mastaloudis2026].

**Mature broccoli.**
- Raw or very lightly steamed is best [sfn-vermeulen2008, sfn-conaway2000].
- Chop and wait: crushing starts conversion. Gentle warming to about 60 °C helps; boiling or prolonged cooking destroys the enzyme [sfn-matusheski2004].
- On cooked broccoli, stir in about 1 g (half a teaspoon) of brown mustard powder after cooking [sfn-okunade2018]. A little seed powder or raw daikon radish works on the same principle [sfn-egner2011].
- High-glucoraphanin varieties deliver more, even when cooked [sfn-sivapalan2018, sfn-armah2015].

**Other crucifers.** One or two servings most days, some raw [sfn-zhang2011, sfn-navarro2014]. Cabbage, kale and Brussels sprouts contribute other isothiocyanates and indoles. Very large intakes of collards and Brussels sprouts are the only plausible thyroid concern, and mainly with low iodine intake [sfn-felker2016, sfn-galanty2024].

**Who should be cautious.** People on chemotherapy or with active cancer (Nrf2 can protect tumour cells) [sfn-wang2008, sfn-denicola2011]; people on narrow-margin drugs cleared by CYP3A4 or UGT1A1 who want high-dose supplements [sfn-maheo1997, sfn-navarro2009]; anyone with GI sensitivity [sfn-dwibedi2025].

## 8. What we don't know

- **Dose-response shape.** The threshold near 25 µmol/day excreted is for one biomarker in one population [sfn-chen2019]. Where benefits plateau for other outcomes is unknown [sfn-yagishita2019].
- **Stacking.** No trial has compared a supplement alone with a supplement plus sprouts or broccoli, or tested sulforaphane combined with curcumin, coffee or exercise on any clinical outcome [sfn-traustadottir2026].
- **Hard outcomes.** No trial has tested sulforaphane on cancer incidence, cardiovascular events or mortality. Crucifer-mortality links are observational [sfn-zhang2011, sfn-li2022].
- **Validated oxidative-damage markers.** Few trials measured F2-isoprostanes or 8-oxo-dG; those that measured MDA or 8-OHdG were small and in at-risk groups [sfn-bahadoran2011, sfn-kikuchi2015], and a larger healthy-aging trial found no change [sfn-nouchi2022]. Pharmacodynamic biomarkers remain unvalidated [sfn-yagishita2020].
- **Who responds.** Gut microbes, body mass and stomach acid all change conversion [sfn-dwibedi2025, sfn-fahey2019, sfn-charron2018]. Whether stratifying by these improves results is untested.
- **Fertility.** No human data at all [sfn-valipour2021, sfn-taheri2021].
- **Long-term high dose.** Trials last weeks to a year (one small pilot 42 months [sfn-shimizu2026]). The safety of years of 100-200 µmol/day is unknown [sfn-cuadrado2019].
- **Unpublished trials.** About half of registered trials have no published results [sfn-saito2025].
