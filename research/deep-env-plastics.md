# Phthalates and bisphenols: what plastic chemicals do, and what you can do about them

The site used to treat phthalates and BPA as one factor. They are different chemicals with different evidence. **Phthalates** are plasticizers that make PVC soft and carry fragrances. Several of them block male hormones during fetal development. Their best human evidence is about pregnancy (preterm birth) and baby boys' genital development. **Bisphenols** (BPA and its replacements BPS, BPF and BPAF) are building blocks of polycarbonate and of the epoxy linings of cans, and act mainly like weak estrogens. Their best evidence is about exposure: what raises urine levels, what lowers them, and why regulators disagree by up to 20,000-fold on a safe dose.

Both groups have one thing in common. The body clears them in hours, so your urine shows roughly what you ate and touched yesterday. That makes exposure easy to change: in a 2026 randomized trial, a week of low-plastic food cut urinary BPA by 60% [ept-harray2026]. It also makes health studies hard, because one urine sample is a poor guide to anyone's usual exposure. This page replaces the phthalates/BPA section of the environment deep dive with six factors: [phthalates], [phthalates-sperm], [phthalates-egg], [bisphenols], [bisphenols-sperm] and [bisphenols-egg]. It also audits the existing [microplastics] and [microplastics-sperm] entries.

## The core science

**Phthalates** split into two families. High-molecular-weight ones (DEHP, DiNP, DiDP) soften PVC in food tubing, gloves, flooring, cables and medical devices. Low-molecular-weight ones (DEP, DBP, DiBP, BBzP) turn up in fragrances, cosmetics, some medicine coatings and adhesives. In rats, DBP, DEHP and BBzP given during a fetal window lower testosterone and INSL3 from the fetal testis. That causes the "phthalate syndrome": hypospadias, undescended testes, malformed accessory glands and a shorter anogenital distance (AGD) [ept-foster2006]. EFSA's group safe dose for DBP, BBzP, DEHP and DiNP is built on that fetal-testosterone effect [ept-efsa2019]. DEP is much weaker as an anti-androgen [ept-radke2018].

**Bisphenols** act on estrogen signalling and also show anti-androgenic activity in lab systems. BPS and BPF have hormonal potency of the same order as BPA [ept-rochester2015]. EFSA's 2023 safe dose for BPA was set on a different endpoint: in mice, BPA expanded the pro-inflammatory Th17 cells of the immune system [ept-efsa2023].

**Oxidative stress.** Oxidative stress is the best-documented step between exposure and harm in people, mostly in pregnancy studies with repeated samples:

- In 482 pregnant Boston women (LIFECODES) with up to four urine samples each, phthalate metabolites tracked urinary 8-OHdG (a DNA-oxidation marker) and 8-isoprostane (a lipid-peroxidation marker). The links were strongest for MBzP, MBP and MiBP [ept-ferguson2015].
- In the same women, 8-isoprostane statistically explained an estimated 47-60% of the link between the DEHP metabolite MECPP and preterm birth [ept-ferguson2017].
- In Puerto Rico, an IQR rise in MBP or MiBP went with about 50% more 8-isoprostane [ept-ferguson2014].
- For BPA, an IQR rise went with 5-9% more of both markers and 9% more IL-6 [ept-ferguson2016].
- In 1,034 Chinese men, every phthalate metabolite tracked 8-OHdG, 8-isoPGF2α and HNE-MA. Oxidative stress suggestively explained about 12% of the link between MBP and lower sperm concentration [ept-liu2019].

These are consistent associations, not proof of cause. The same urine sample measures both the chemical and the marker, so anything that concentrates urine or changes metabolism can link the two.

## 1. Phthalates

### Where exposure comes from

| Source | Evidence | Size of effect |
|---|---|---|
| Processed, packaged and fast food (DEHP, DiNP) | NHANES 2003-2010, 8,877 people [ept-zota2016]; ultra-processed food quintiles [ept-martinezsteele2020] | ≥35% of calories from fast food: DEHP metabolites +24%, DiNP +39% |
| Food-processing contamination | Catered "low-exposure" diet [ept-sathyanarayana2013] | Ground coriander and milk raised children's estimated DEHP intake above EPA and EFSA limits |
| Fragrance and personal care (DEP) | 406 men [ept-duty2005]; HERMOSA [ept-harley2016] | Cologne or aftershave: urinary MEP about double (265 vs 108 ng/mL); +33% per extra product |
| Enteric-coated medicines (DBP, DEP) | NHANES [ept-hernandezdiaz2009]; case report [ept-hauser2004] | Mesalamine users: MBP 50× non-users; one Asacol patient over 100× the 95th percentile |
| Medical PVC tubing and bags (DEHP) | NICU infants [ept-calafat2004] | MEHP geometric mean 100 vs 3.4 ng/mL in the general population |
| Vinyl flooring and dust (BBzP) | 83 infants [ept-carlstedt2013] | Higher MBzP with PVC bedroom flooring (disputed [ept-blakey2013]) |

Typical German intakes are about 2.7 µg/kg/day of DEHP, 2.1 of DnBP, 1.5 of DiBP and 0.6 of DiNP. Children are more exposed than adults [ept-wittassek2008]. US and German exposure to DEP, DBP, BBzP and DEHP has fallen since about 2001 [ept-johns2015, ept-oskar2024]. Replacements have risen fast:

- **DEHTP** went from under 10% detection to 100% in German 24-hour urine between 2009 and 2017, though intakes are still far below its safe dose [ept-lessmann2019]. It is in 96-99.9% of Americans [ept-silva2019].
- In Europe, health-based biomonitoring guidance values for phthalates were exceeded in up to 5% of children and teens. For about 17% of them, a combined risk from five reprotoxic phthalates could not be ruled out [ept-gerofke2024, ept-lange2021].

### Pregnancy and baby boys

Pregnancy outcomes are the most consistent human evidence for phthalates:

- **Preterm birth.** A pooled analysis of 16 US cohorts (6,045 pregnancies) found 12-16% higher odds of preterm birth per interquartile rise in MBP, MiBP, MECPP and MCPP. Modelled 30% and 50% cuts in exposure would prevent about 6 and 11 preterm births per 1,000 [ept-welch2022]. The 2026 ECHO cohort (5,318 pairs) found DiNP linked to 0.6 days shorter gestation (preterm OR 1.16) [ept-buckley2026]. An EPA systematic review rates the preterm evidence as moderate [ept-radke2019].
- **Anogenital distance.** Boys' AGD was shorter with prenatal phthalates in the first US study [ept-swan2005] and with first-trimester DEHP in 366 boys from the TIDES cohort [ept-swan2015]. In Sweden, the replacement DiNP was linked to a small (about 4%) reduction [ept-bornehag2015]. A low-exposed Danish cohort found no consistent association [ept-jensen2016].

### Sperm ([phthalates-sperm])

- **Semen quality.** A meta-analysis found that men with high urinary MBP or MBzP have about twice the odds of low sperm concentration (pooled OR 2.19 and 1.88). It found no link with motility or morphology [ept-wangh2023]. An EPA review called the evidence "robust" for DEHP and DBP across AGD, semen and testosterone [ept-radke2018]. A 2026 systematic review graded certainty low to moderate [ept-dhar2026].
- **Sperm DNA damage.** In Boston fertility-clinic men, MEP and MEHP were linked to more Comet-assay DNA damage. The MEHP link (about 17% per IQR) appeared only after statistically adjusting for its own oxidative metabolite [ept-duty2003, ept-hauser2007].
- **Mixtures.** In 420 EARTH men, a 24-chemical mixture analysis found the consistent signals were mercury and triclosan, not phthalates [ept-genardwalton2025].
- **The couple's IVF outcome.** Most paternal phthalate metabolites did not predict it [ept-dodge2015]. A paternal DEHP factor did (HR 1.41 for failing to reach live birth) [ept-minguezalarcon2021].

### Eggs and IVF ([phthalates-egg])

- **EARTH (Boston), 256 IVF patients.** Women in the top quartile of DEHP metabolites had fewer eggs and 19 percentage points lower clinical pregnancy and live birth per cycle [ept-hauser2016]. They also showed an imprecise rise in early pregnancy loss [ept-messerlian2016].
- **Israeli IVF cohort, 136 women.** DEHP was again linked to fewer eggs and good embryos, but **not** to implantation, pregnancy or live birth [ept-machtinger2018].
- **Ovarian reserve.** In 657 Chinese women, a phthalate-plus-DINCH mixture went with 8.6% lower antral follicle count [ept-jiang2024].
- **Time to pregnancy.** Two cohorts conflict: one found phthalates lengthened it [ept-charifson2024], the other that several phthalates shortened it [ept-blaauwendraad2025].

### The mortality estimates

In 2022 Trasande and colleagues analysed 5,303 NHANES adults. They linked DEHP and other high-molecular-weight phthalates to higher all-cause mortality (top tertile HR about 1.4-1.5) and extrapolated 90,761-107,283 US deaths a year at ages 55-64 [ept-trasande2022]. Later burden models applied that hazard ratio worldwide: 356,238 cardiovascular deaths [ept-hyman2025] and 1.97 million preterm births [ept-hyman2026] attributed to DEHP.

Critics point to the same database giving different answers:

- An earlier NHANES analysis found no link with cardiovascular death [ept-sturgeon2016].
- A larger one implicated MBzP and MnBP, not DEHP [ept-beyer2022].
- A 2025 mixture analysis found the link only in people with low vitamin D or folate [ept-zhang2025].

Each study rests on one spot urine, so the measured "exposure" changes from day to day. Attributable-death numbers assume the association is causal. Reverse causation is also possible: sick people get more medical PVC and coated drugs.

## 2. Bisphenols

### Sources and levels

In NHANES 2013-14, BPA was found in 95.7% of Americans, BPS in 89.4% and BPF in 66.5%. Median BPA was 1.24 µg/L [ept-lehmler2018]. After California listed BPA under Proposition 65, US BPA fell 15% while unlisted BPS rose 20%, a textbook regrettable substitution [ept-knox2024].

| Source | Evidence | Size of effect |
|---|---|---|
| Canned food | Crossover RCT, 75 adults [ept-carwile2011]; 20 volunteers [ept-peng2019] | Daily canned soup: 20.8 vs 1.1 µg/L; one canned meal +206% at 4 h, baseline by 24 h |
| Canned drinks | Crossover RCT, 103 women [ept-plachetka2025] | Cans vs PET: +22% BPA; BPS, BPF regardless of packaging |
| Polycarbonate bottles | 77 students [ept-carwile2009] | +69% after 1 week |
| Thermal receipts | Crossover, 24 adults [ept-ehrlich2014]; sanitizer experiment [ept-hormann2014] | 2 h handling: 1.8 → 5.8 µg/L, peaking at 8-12 h; gloves prevented it; sanitizer before handling: serum free BPA about 7 ng/mL within 90 min |

### Health and fertility

- **Mortality.** Top-tertile urinary BPA went with an all-cause mortality HR of 1.49 in NHANES. The estimate rests on 344 deaths and one urine sample each [ept-bao2020].
- **Sperm ([bisphenols-sperm]).** Clear harm shows mainly in BPA factory workers [ept-li2011]. At everyday levels the evidence is weak:
  - A 2022 meta-analysis found a motility link that vanished after correcting for publication bias [ept-castellini2022].
  - A 2024 meta-analysis found slight falls in concentration and count [ept-lu2024].
  - The site's claim of 10% more sperm DNA damage comes from 190 clinic men [ept-meeker2010]. In the population-based LIFE study (418 men), higher BPA went with slightly *less* DNA fragmentation [ept-goldstone2015], and young Spanish men showed no overall link [ept-kiwittcardenas2021].
  - Detectable BPS went with lower sperm concentration, mostly in overweight men [ept-ghayda2019].
- **Eggs ([bisphenols-egg]).**
  - BPA caused meiotic aneuploidy in mouse eggs [ept-hunt2003]. Another lab did not reproduce that [ept-pacchierotti2008]. BPA also disturbed spindles in human eggs matured in a dish [ept-machtinger2013].
  - In 84 Boston IVF patients, each log unit of urinary BPA meant 12% fewer eggs [ept-moklin2010]. In the same clinic's larger follow-up (256 women, 375 cycles), BPA was not linked to any IVF outcome [ept-minguezalarcon2015].
  - Meta-analyses link BPA to lower fertilization rates and embryo quality but not to pregnancy [ept-yuan2025]. A newer one reports lower AMH and pregnancy, but its confidence intervals are implausibly narrow [ept-majdi2026].
  - Replacements show similar oocyte damage in animal and lab studies [ept-peters2024].

### The safe-dose fight

| Body | Position |
|---|---|
| EFSA (2023) | TDI 0.2 ng/kg/day, cut 20,000-fold from 4 µg/kg; average diets exceed it 100-1,000-fold [ept-efsa2023] |
| German BfR | Alternative TDI 1,000-fold higher, based on sperm counts after adult exposure; rejects the Th17 endpoint [ept-kortenkamp2024] |
| European Medicines Agency | Formal disagreement with EFSA [ept-zoeller2023] |
| US FDA | Kept its higher limits [ept-vomsaal2024] |
| European Commission | Banned BPA in food contact materials, Regulation 2024/3190; most uses from 20 July 2026 [ept-eu2024] |

Behind this is a dispute about what counts as evidence. The NTP's guideline-compliant CLARITY-BPA rat study found no consistent effects below its top dose [ept-camacho2019]. Academic studies on the same animals reported effects at the lowest dose, often with non-monotonic dose-responses [ept-heindel2020]. A consultant-authored letter arguing that EFSA's evidence did not justify the cut was withdrawn by the journal [ept-prueitt2023]. Treat it as retracted.

## 3. The measurement problem

Phthalates and bisphenols have half-lives of hours: about 2-10 h for DEHP metabolites [ept-koch2004] and about 6 h for BPA and BPS [ept-thayer2015, ept-oh2018]. Every epidemiology study therefore fights noise:

- **Within-person ICC in pregnancy:** 0.08 for summed DEHP and 0.12 for BPA, versus about 0.5 for MEP and MBP [ept-braun2012]. Across phthalates, ICCs run 0.1-0.6 [ept-johns2015, ept-dewalque2015]. Variability within a single day dominates for BPA [ept-ye2011, ept-fisher2015].
- **One sample attenuated a BPA dose-response by 69%.** About 18 samples were needed to bring the bias down to 10% [ept-vernet2019].
- **Non-food exposure.** NHANES urinary BPA barely fell with longer fasting, which implies non-food sources or body storage [ept-stahlhut2009].

This error usually hides real effects. With 11 or more metabolites tested against many outcomes, it also produces chance findings. That may explain why the same metabolite looks harmful in one cohort and protective in another [ept-blaauwendraad2025].

## 4. Can you lower your levels? The intervention trials

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Rudel 2011 | 20 people, 3 days fresh food | BPA −66%, DEHP metabolites −53 to −56% [ept-rudel2011] |
| Sathyanarayana 2013 | RCT, 10 families, 5-day catered diet | DEHP rose sharply because spices and milk were contaminated [ept-sathyanarayana2013] |
| HERMOSA 2016 | 100 girls, 3 days labelled-free personal care | MEP −27%, parabens −44%; MnBP, MiBP unchanged [ept-harley2016] |
| Hutter 2016 | One family, plastic removed, 2 months | Mixed; the mother's phthalates rose [ept-hutter2016] |
| Hagobian 2017 | Pilot RCT, 24 women, 3 weeks | Urinary BPA fell vs control [ept-hagobian2017] |
| Galloway 2018 | 94 teenagers, 7-day self-run diet | No change in BPA [ept-galloway2018] |
| PERTH 2026 | RCT, 60 adults, 7 days low-plastic food, kitchenware and toiletries | MnBP −37.5%, MBzP −53.5%, BPA −59.7%; toiletries alone lowered MnBP [ept-harray2026] |

A scoping review of 26 interventions reached the same conclusion: removing sources works within days, but compliance and hidden contamination limit it, and regulation may matter more than individual effort [ept-yang2023]. No trial has measured a health or fertility outcome.

## 5. Timing

| Factor | Onset | Full effect | After stopping | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [phthalates] | Urine peaks 2-4 h after exposure; falls within 1-3 days of a change [ept-koch2004, ept-rudel2011] | 3-7 days [ept-harray2026] | Back to baseline in 1-3 days [ept-johns2015] | Daily |
| [bisphenols] | Peak 3-4 h after a meal, 8-12 h after receipts [ept-peng2019, ept-ehrlich2014] | 1-7 days | Baseline within about a day [ept-peng2019] | Daily |
| [phthalates-sperm], [bisphenols-sperm] | Not measured | About one spermatogenic cycle (64 days plus transit) [ept-heller1963] | Up to one cycle (extrapolated) | Daily, start ≥3 months ahead |
| [phthalates-egg], [bisphenols-egg] | Not measured | About 85 days of follicle growth [ept-gougeon1986] | Unknown | Daily, start ≥3 months ahead |

**Sperm.** Lowering exposure 2-4 weeks before conception still clears the chemicals within days, but it reaches only the last stages of sperm already in production.

**Eggs.** The mouse BPA data point to the final weeks of egg growth [ept-hunt2003], and the IVF studies measured exposure during the treatment cycle itself [ept-hauser2016]. So a late start is not wasted, even though the full window is about 3 months.

**Pregnancy.** The 16-cohort pool placed DEHP's preterm-birth window in the first and second trimesters [ept-friedman2025]. A fertility cohort placed it late in pregnancy [ept-yland2022].

## Where the evidence is contested

1. **Phthalate mortality.** Large attributable-death estimates [ept-trasande2022, ept-hyman2025, ept-hyman2026] versus NHANES analyses that find no cardiovascular link [ept-sturgeon2016] or blame different metabolites [ept-beyer2022, ept-zhang2025].
2. **Which pregnancy window matters for preterm birth.** Early [ept-friedman2025] versus late [ept-yland2022]. Whether the association is causal is supported by mediation through oxidative stress [ept-ferguson2017] but not proven.
3. **Anogenital distance.** Positive US and Swedish cohorts [ept-swan2005, ept-swan2015, ept-bornehag2015] versus a null Danish cohort at lower exposure [ept-jensen2016].
4. **Phthalates and semen.** "Robust" in an EPA review [ept-radke2018] versus narrow meta-analytic findings [ept-wangh2023] and mixture analyses that point elsewhere [ept-genardwalton2025].
5. **DEHP and IVF success.** −19 points of live birth [ept-hauser2016] versus no live-birth effect in a second cohort [ept-machtinger2018].
6. **BPA and sperm DNA.** Harm in a clinic sample [ept-meeker2010] versus no harm or the opposite in population samples [ept-goldstone2015, ept-kiwittcardenas2021]. BPA and semen overall: a publication-bias-adjusted null [ept-castellini2022] versus small count reductions [ept-lu2024].
7. **BPA and eggs.** An early positive cohort [ept-moklin2010] and mouse aneuploidy [ept-hunt2003] versus the null expanded cohort [ept-minguezalarcon2015] and a non-replicating mouse study [ept-pacchierotti2008].
8. **The BPA safe dose.** EFSA [ept-efsa2023] with academic support [ept-vomsaal2024, ept-zoeller2023] versus BfR, EMA and FDA [ept-kortenkamp2024]. Guideline versus academic CLARITY results [ept-camacho2019, ept-heindel2020]. A withdrawn industry-side letter [ept-prueitt2023].
9. **BPA half-life.** About 6 h in dosing studies [ept-thayer2015] versus an apparent 43 h in NHANES [ept-stahlhut2009].
10. **Whether individuals can lower exposure.** Successes [ept-rudel2011, ept-hagobian2017, ept-harray2026] versus failures and backfires [ept-sathyanarayana2013, ept-galloway2018, ept-hutter2016].
11. **Replacements.** DEHTP: no guideline developmental toxicity [ept-faber2007] versus low-dose ovarian effects in mice [ept-potts2025]. DINCH: null in IVF [ept-machtinger2018] versus single-study links with time to pregnancy, FSH and preterm birth [ept-blaauwendraad2025, ept-jiang2024, ept-yland2022]. BPS/BPF: BPA-like activity [ept-rochester2015, ept-denbraversewradj2020, ept-mhaoutykodja2024].
12. **Funding and advocacy.** Some burden estimates and reviews were funded by plastics-campaign philanthropies [ept-hyman2025, ept-symeonides2024, ept-harray2026]. A letter opposing the PVC-flooring study framed it as advocacy [ept-blakey2013]. The withdrawn letter opposing EFSA came from consultants [ept-prueitt2023].

## Practical bottom line

- **Food first.** Cook from fresh, minimally packaged ingredients. Eat less fast food and ultra-processed food. Skip canned foods and canned drinks. Store and reheat in glass or steel. These steps cut phthalate and BPA metabolites by roughly 40-65% within a week [ept-rudel2011, ept-harray2026, ept-zota2016, ept-carwile2011].
- **Personal care.** Choose fragrance-free lotions, deodorants and aftershave to cut DEP [ept-duty2005, ept-harley2016].
- **Receipts.** Decline them or hold them briefly. Don't handle them right after hand sanitizer or lotion [ept-ehrlich2014, ept-hormann2014].
- **Medicines.** If you take an enteric-coated drug long term (for example some mesalamine products), ask a pharmacist about phthalate-free versions. Don't stop the medicine [ept-hernandezdiaz2009].
- **Home.** Prefer non-PVC flooring when renovating, and wet-mop and vacuum dust [ept-carlstedt2013].
- **Keep it up.** Exposure returns within a day of going back, so the steps only work while you do them. For conception, keep them up for about 3 months beforehand and through pregnancy [ept-heller1963, ept-gougeon1986].
- **Keep expectations modest.** These steps lower urine levels. No trial has shown better sperm, eggs, IVF results or health from them. The strongest reason to act is pregnancy (preterm birth, boys' development), where the evidence is moderate [ept-welch2022, ept-radke2019].
- **"BPA-free" and "phthalate-free" are not "chemical-free".** BPS, BPF, DINCH and DEHTP have far less safety data [ept-mhaoutykodja2024, ept-potts2025].

## What we don't know

- Whether lowering phthalate or bisphenol exposure improves any health, semen, egg, IVF or pregnancy outcome. No trial has measured one.
- Whether the phthalate-preterm association is causal, and which trimester matters.
- Whether the mortality associations reflect cause, confounding or reverse causation.
- How safe DINCH, DEHTP, BPS, BPF and newer replacements are in people.
- How much non-food exposure (dust, skin, receipts, medical devices) contributes for typical people.
- Whether micro- and nanoplastics in semen and follicular fluid are measured correctly, and whether they matter.
