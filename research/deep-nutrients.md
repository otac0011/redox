# Iron, micronutrients & the 'fix deficiencies, don't megadose' rule

**In one paragraph.** Your body's strongest antioxidant defences are enzymes, not pills: superoxide dismutases, glutathione peroxidases, catalase and glutathione reductase. Each one needs a mineral or vitamin to work: copper and zinc, manganese, selenium, heme iron, riboflavin. Glutathione supply also depends on folate, B12 and B6. Running short of any of these weakens the system in ways you can measure, and fixing a real shortfall is among the most reliable "antioxidant" moves there is. Once the enzymes are saturated, though, more does nothing, and for several nutrients (iron, selenium, zinc, folic acid, vitamin A, vitamin E, iodine) high intakes cause harm. That makes a U-shaped curve. Iron is the sharpest example. It is the metal that turns hydrogen peroxide into hydroxyl radicals, yet iron deficiency is one of the world's most common and damaging nutritional problems. The rule this page defends: **test where you can, fix deficiencies, and don't megadose.**

## 1. Cofactors, not scavengers

Small antioxidant molecules (vitamin C, vitamin E) mop up radicals one at a time. Enzymes recycle themselves and handle far more. The main ones and their cofactors [nut-rayman2012, nut-iom2000, nut-iom2001]:

| Enzyme | Job | Needs | What happens when short |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOD1 (cytosol) | superoxide -> H2O2 | copper + zinc | Activity falls when copper is low; zinc excess lowers it within 6 weeks [nut-fischer1984] |
| SOD2 (mitochondria) | superoxide -> H2O2 | manganese | Lymphocyte MnSOD rises with manganese supply [nut-davis1992] |
| Glutathione peroxidases, incl. GPX4 | H2O2 and lipid peroxides -> water/alcohols | selenium + glutathione | Selenium deficiency causes Keshan cardiomyopathy [nut-zhou2018] |
| Catalase | H2O2 -> water + O2 | heme iron | (part of iron's essential role) |
| Glutathione reductase | recycles oxidised glutathione | riboflavin (FAD) + NADPH | Activity is the standard riboflavin status test [nut-mcnulty2023] |
| Glutathione synthesis | makes glutathione | cysteine, partly from homocysteine via B6; homocysteine recycling needs folate + B12 | Homocysteine rises in B12 deficiency [nut-niklewicz2024] |

The point for the rest of this page: if an enzyme is already saturated, adding more cofactor gives no further benefit. Hurst's trial shows this neatly. Selenoprotein P levelled off once total selenium intake reached about 105 ug/day, and platelet glutathione peroxidase did not change at any dose [nut-hurst2010].

## 2. Iron: the double-edged metal

### 2.1 Why iron is a pro-oxidant

Free ferrous iron reacts with hydrogen peroxide (Fenton chemistry) to make the hydroxyl radical, the most reactive oxidant in biology. It also breaks lipid hydroperoxides into chain-propagating radicals. Cells therefore lock iron inside transferrin, ferritin and heme. The danger lies in the small **labile iron pool**: loosely bound, redox-active iron whose level tracks cellular ROS production [nut-kakhlon2002]. In 2012 researchers named **ferroptosis**, a regulated form of cell death driven by iron-catalysed peroxidation of polyunsaturated membrane lipids. It happens when the selenoprotein GPX4 and its fuel, glutathione, run out [nut-dixon2012, nut-stockwell2017]. Ferroptosis connects iron, selenium and glutathione in one mechanism. Nearly all of that evidence comes from cells and animals, though.

### 2.2 Ferritin: a store and an alarm bell

Serum ferritin is the standard measure of iron stores, and a low value reliably means iron deficiency. A high value is ambiguous. Ferritin is an acute-phase protein that rises with infection, inflammation, obesity, fatty liver and alcohol. Kell and Pretorius argue that serum ferritin is mostly a leakage product from damaged cells, which would explain why it tracks oxidative stress and disease regardless of stores [nut-kell2014]. In the FeAST trial, ferritin correlated with IL-6 and CRP, and higher ferritin tracked with death [nut-depalma2010]. Any study linking "high iron stores" to disease has to separate iron from inflammation, and most cannot.

### 2.3 The iron hypothesis: heart disease, diabetes, cancer

In 1981 Sullivan proposed that women are protected from heart disease before menopause because menstruation keeps their iron stores low, and that phlebotomy could test the idea [nut-sullivan1981]. Forty years on, the evidence splits by outcome:

| Outcome | Observational data | Genetic (Mendelian randomization) | Trials | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coronary heart disease | Ferritin >=200 ug/L: RR 1.0 in 12 prospective studies [nut-danesh1999]; null in NHANES II [nut-sempos2000]; serum iron and transferrin saturation *inversely* related; heme intake positively (RR 1.57) [nut-hunnicutt2014] | Higher iron slightly *protective* (OR 0.94 per SD) [nut-gill2017]; 7-14% lower CAD per SD [nut-liu2024] | FeAST: phlebotomy in 1277 PAD patients, death HR 0.85 (NS) [nut-zacharski2007] | Not supported |
| Type 2 diabetes | +22% per 100 ug/L ferritin, stronger in women [nut-jiang2019]; top vs bottom fifth RR 1.73 [nut-kunutsor2013, nut-zhao2012] | Higher iron, ferritin and saturation raise T2D (OR 1.07-1.19) [nut-wang2021, nut-liu2024] | Phlebotomy in metabolic syndrome lowered BP and HbA1c but not insulin resistance [nut-houschyar2012] | Probably causal, modest |
| Cancer | Heme iron and colon cancer RR 1.18 [nut-bastide2011] | (not studied here) | FeAST secondary: fewer visceral cancers, HR 0.65 [nut-zacharski2008] | Suggestive only |
| Lifespan | Ferritin not linked to all-cause death in NHANES II [nut-sempos2000] | 1 SD higher serum iron = 0.7 fewer years of parental lifespan [nut-daghlas2021]; haem metabolism flagged in ageing GWAS [nut-timmers2020] | none | Weak signal of harm |
| Other | | Higher iron: less anaemia and hypercholesterolemia, more skin infections [nut-gill2019] | | Trade-offs |

The honest summary: a lifetime of genetically higher iron looks mildly bad for glucose metabolism and perhaps lifespan, and neutral to mildly good for coronary arteries. The one large phlebotomy RCT missed its primary endpoint. An accompanying editorial called the evidence far from ironclad [nut-hu2007], and another questioned the cancer result [nut-edgren2008].

### 2.4 Blood donation and the healthy donor effect

A Finnish cohort found 88% fewer heart attacks in men who had donated in the previous two years [nut-salonen1998]. A US cohort of 38,244 men found no benefit at all, even though donation clearly lowered ferritin [nut-ascherio2001]. The likely explanation is the **healthy donor effect**: only healthy people are allowed to donate, and they keep donating only while they stay healthy. Dutch donors had better self-rated health and fewer doctor visits than the general population [nut-atsma2011]. In 1.2 million Scandinavian donors, each extra annual donation was tied to 18.6% lower mortality, falling to 7.5% after adjusting for self-selection, and the authors would not call it a benefit [nut-ullum2015]. One small unblinded RCT found that removing 550-800 mL of blood lowered systolic BP by 16.6 mmHg in metabolic syndrome [nut-houschyar2012]; a commentary noted the design limits [nut-manco2012]. On the harm side, the 45,000-donor INTERVAL trial showed that shorter donation intervals cause tiredness, restless legs, lower ferritin and more deferrals for low haemoglobin [nut-diangelantonio2017, nut-kaptoge2019, nut-vandenhurk2018]. Donate because it saves lives, not as an antioxidant strategy [blood-donation].

### 2.5 Heme vs non-heme iron

Heme iron (meat, fish) is absorbed well and largely regardless of need. Non-heme iron (plants, fortificants, supplements) is tightly regulated by the hormone hepcidin and blocked by phytate, polyphenols (tea, coffee) and calcium. Vitamin C and meat enhance it. Mixed diets yield about 14-18% bioavailability and vegetarian diets 5-12% [nut-hurrell2010]. Heme iron intake, not non-heme, is linked to cardiovascular disease (+7% per mg/day) [nut-fang2015], CHD [nut-hunnicutt2014], type 2 diabetes [nut-zhao2012] and colon cancer, where heme also catalyses N-nitroso compounds and lipid oxidation products in the gut [nut-bastide2011]. See [processed-red-meat] and [unprocessed-red-meat].

### 2.6 Haemochromatosis (brief)

About 1 in 156 people of European descent are HFE C282Y homozygotes. In UK Biobank, 21.7% of homozygous men and 9.8% of homozygous women had a haemochromatosis diagnosis, along with excess liver disease, arthritis and diabetes [nut-pilling2019]. Pathological iron overload of any cause damages the gonads: a 2026 systematic review found hypogonadism in 47% of men with iron overload and amenorrhoea in many women [nut-carlomagno2026]. Genetic testing is covered elsewhere on this site.

### 2.7 Iron deficiency is also harmful

The case against excess iron must not be read as "less is better". Iron deficiency is one of the leading contributors to the global burden of disease. It impairs immunity, work capacity and brain function, and it can do so before anaemia appears [nut-pasricha2021]. In 198 non-anaemic menstruating women with fatigue and ferritin <50 ug/L, 80 mg/day iron for 12 weeks cut fatigue scores by 47.7% vs 28.8% on placebo [nut-vaucher2012]. Meta-analyses find gains in attention, and in IQ for anaemic people [nut-falkingham2010]. In non-anaemic but iron-deficient youth and menstruating adults, iron improved anxiety, fatigue and memory, with no effect in iron-replete participants [nut-fiani2025]. In pregnancy, maternal anaemia is associated with low birthweight (RR 1.31), preterm birth (RR 1.63) and perinatal death [nut-rahman2016]. Routine prenatal iron reduces anaemia and probably low birthweight [nut-finkelstein2024], even in women who start out iron-replete [nut-hansen2023]. See [iron-deficiency].

### 2.8 Iron pills when you are not deficient

Most of a supplement dose is never absorbed. In healthy adults, 19 mg/day ferrous sulfate for two weeks increased weakly bound iron in faeces five-fold and raised faecal free-radical generation by about 40% [nut-lund1999]. In Kenyan infants, iron-fortified powders shifted gut bacteria toward pathogenic E. coli and raised the gut-inflammation marker calprotectin [nut-jaeggi2015, nut-weiss2015]. Ferrous sulfate drops also changed the microbiota of iron-sufficient Swedish infants [nut-simonytesjodin2019]. Middle-aged women taking >100 mg/day had fewer butyrate producers in a small cross-sectional study [nut-shearer2024]. In a high-malaria setting, routine iron plus folic acid raised death or hospitalisation in young children by 12% [nut-sazawal2006, nut-english2006]. In pregnancy, both low and high haemoglobin are associated with adverse outcomes, high Hb with gestational diabetes and pre-eclampsia [nut-young2023]. Higher ferritin predicts gestational diabetes too [nut-khambalia2016], and Cochrane noted more women above 130 g/L on iron [nut-penarosas2015]. EFSA could not find a dose-response for chronic disease and set a safe level of 40 mg/day total iron [nut-efsa2024iron]. See [iron-excess].

Global modelling suggests that, from food alone, more than 5 billion people under-consume iodine and vitamin E, and more than 4 billion under-consume iron, riboflavin, folate and vitamin C [nut-passarelli2024]. Fortification and supplements close part of that gap.

### Selenium: the clearest U-curve

- **Deficiency:** Keshan disease, a cardiomyopathy in low-selenium regions of China; community supplementation cut it by 86% [nut-zhou2018]. Low status also weakens immunity and thyroid function and makes the sperm midpiece fragile (see section 5) [nut-rayman2012].
- **At risk:** people in low-selenium soil regions (parts of Europe, China, New Zealand), vegans there, people with malabsorption or on long-term parenteral nutrition.
- **Replete:** about 105 ug/day total intake maximises selenoprotein P [nut-hurst2010]. Plasma selenium and selenoprotein P are valid status markers [nut-ashton2009].
- **Megadoses:** 200 ug/day raised type 2 diabetes (HR 1.55) [nut-stranges2007, nut-bleys2007]. It raised high-grade prostate cancer by 91% in men with high baseline status [nut-kristal2014, nut-frankel2014]. 300 ug/day for 5 years raised 10-year mortality (HR 1.59) in moderately low-selenium Danes [nut-rayman2018]. Cochrane found no cancer prevention [nut-vinceti2018]. See [selenium-supplement].

### Zinc and copper: SOD1 partners that compete

- **Deficiency:** an estimated 17% of the world is at risk of inadequate zinc [nut-wessells2012]. Deficiency impairs immunity, growth, taste and wound healing, and lowers seminal zinc.
- **At risk:** vegans (phytate blocks absorption), older adults, alcohol use disorder, Crohn's/coeliac/bariatric surgery.
- **Replete:** RDA amounts from meat, shellfish, dairy, legumes and seeds.
- **Megadoses:** zinc induces intestinal metallothionein that traps copper. Just 50 mg/day for 6 weeks lowered red-cell SOD1 activity [nut-fischer1984]. At 100-300 mg/day, zinc causes copper-deficiency anaemia and neutropenia [nut-fosmire1990] and a myelopathy that mimics B12 deficiency [nut-jaiser2010]. A meta-analysis reporting that zinc lowers CRP and MDA [nut-hosseini2021] has been criticised for its methods [nut-jafari2021, nut-hosseini2021b]. See [zinc-status].

### Manganese: SOD2's metal

- **Deficiency:** essentially unknown on normal diets; MnSOD activity responds to manganese intake in controlled studies [nut-davis1992].
- **Megadoses:** manganese accumulates in the brain with neurotoxic effects [nut-wang2025mn]. There is no reason to supplement beyond a multivitamin. IOM UL 11 mg/day [nut-iom2001].

### Riboflavin: the glutathione recycler

- **Deficiency:** cracked lips and mouth corners and anaemia when severe. Subclinical deficiency may be common even in rich countries but is rarely measured [nut-mcnulty2023].
- **At risk:** low dairy intake, older adults, alcohol, adolescents, pregnancy.
- **Replete:** about 1.1-1.3 mg/day. The best test is the erythrocyte glutathione reductase activation coefficient.
- **Megadoses:** no known toxicity. In people with the MTHFR 677TT genotype, 1.6 mg/day lowered systolic BP by 5.6 mmHg [nut-wilson2013]. See [riboflavin].

### Magnesium

- **Deficiency:** cramps, arrhythmia and low potassium and calcium when severe. Low intake is common: 58% of older adults in one trial ate below the requirement [nut-nielsen2010].
- **At risk:** PPI users (hypomagnesaemia OR 1.71) [nut-srinutta2019], diuretic users, people with diabetes or alcohol use disorder.
- **Supplements:** a modest CRP reduction in meta-analysis [nut-veronese2022], concentrated in people who start out inflamed [nut-nielsen2010]. The claim of a hidden "magnesium crisis" [nut-dinicolantonio2018] outruns the trials. See [magnesium-status].

### Vitamin C: saturation at ~200 mg/day

- **Deficiency:** 7.1% of Americans, and smokers have one-third lower levels [nut-schleicher2009].
- **Replete:** in Levine's inpatient studies, plasma levels rose steeply between 30 and 100 mg/day and plateaued from 200 mg/day. White cells saturated at 100 mg. Doses above 400 mg had "no evident value", and oxalate excretion rose at 1000 mg [nut-levine1996]. In young women, F2-isoprostanes did not change at any dose from 30 to 2500 mg/day [nut-levine2001]. Carr and Frei argued for about 120 mg/day from epidemiology [nut-carr1999].
- **Megadoses:** limited evidence links supplements to kidney stones [nut-oconnor2022]. See [vitamin-c-status], [vitamin-c-foods], [vitamin-c-supplement].

### Vitamin E

- **Deficiency:** clinical deficiency is rare (fat malabsorption, genetic transport defects). More than 90% of Americans eat below the requirement, and plasma alpha-tocopherol <12 umol/L is linked to infection, anaemia and poor pregnancy outcomes [nut-traber2014].
- **Megadoses:** high-dose vitamin E (>=400 IU) raised all-cause mortality in a meta-analysis of 135,967 people, with risk rising above ~150 IU/day [nut-miller2005]. EFSA keeps a 300 mg/day UL because of bleeding risk [nut-efsa2024vite]. Vitamin E raised prostate cancer in men with low selenium [nut-kristal2014]. See [vitamin-e-supplement].

### B12 and folate: one-carbon metabolism and glutathione

Folate and B12 recycle homocysteine to methionine, which is needed for DNA methylation and DNA synthesis. Homocysteine can instead be routed to cysteine and glutathione.

- **B12 deficiency at risk:** vegans have lower B12 and higher homocysteine and methylmalonic acid [nut-niklewicz2024]. Two or more years of PPIs raises the odds (OR 1.65) [nut-lam2013]. Metformin lowers B12 by about 57 pmol/L [nut-chapman2016]. Older adults with atrophic gastritis are also at risk.
- **Replete vs megadose:** lowering homocysteine with B vitamins did not prevent heart attacks or death in 71,422 trial participants but reduced stroke by about 10% [nut-marticarvajal2017]. In unfortified China, folic acid cut first stroke by 21% [nut-huo2015, nut-stampfer2015]. In unfortified Norway, folic acid plus B12 raised cancer (HR 1.21) and death [nut-ebbing2009, nut-drake2009]. 1 mg/day folic acid did not prevent colorectal adenomas and may have raised advanced ones [nut-cole2007]. EFSA keeps the 1000 ug UL because folic acid can mask B12-deficiency neuropathy [nut-efsa2023folate]. See [vitamin-b12-folate-status].

### Iodine

- **Deficiency:** hypothyroidism, goitre and, in pregnancy, impaired fetal brain development. In the UK, even mild maternal deficiency was linked to lower child verbal IQ (OR 1.58) [nut-bath2013, nut-stagnarogreen2013].
- **At risk:** vegans, people avoiding dairy and iodised salt, pregnancy, countries without salt iodisation.
- **Megadoses:** excess (kelp, amiodarone, contrast media) can trigger hypo- or hyperthyroidism [nut-sohn2024]. See [iodine-status].

### Vitamin A

- **Deficiency:** a major cause of child blindness and infection deaths in low-income countries; rare in high-income ones.
- **Megadoses:** supplemental preformed vitamin A above 10,000 IU (3000 ug) per day in early pregnancy was linked to a 4.8-fold prevalence of cranial-neural-crest birth defects [nut-rothman1995]. High retinol intake is linked to hip fracture (RR 1.40) [nut-wu2014]. EFSA retained a 3000 ug/day UL based on teratogenicity [nut-efsa2024vita]. Beta-carotene pills raised lung cancer in smokers ([beta-carotene-supplement]).

## 4. Multivitamins: insurance, not therapy

| Trial | Who | Cancer | CVD | Cognition | Mortality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PHS II [nut-gaziano2012, nut-sesso2012, nut-grodstein2013] | 14,641 male physicians, 11 years | HR 0.92 (significant) | HR 1.01 | no effect | HR 0.94 (NS) |
| COSMOS [nut-sesso2022, nut-vyas2024] | 21,442 older adults, 3.6 years | HR 0.97 (NS); lung cancer HR 0.62 | HR 0.98 | +0.07 SD global cognition (pooled) | HR 0.93 (NS) |
| Three US cohorts [nut-loftfield2024] | 390,124 adults | | | | HR 1.04 |

The USPSTF review pooled a small cancer reduction (OR 0.93) but judged the evidence insufficient to recommend multivitamins [nut-oconnor2022, nut-uspstf2022]. Editorials have questioned the PHS II cancer result [nut-bach2012] and the value of multivitamins for healthy people [nut-barnard2024]. A standard, RDA-level multivitamin is reasonable insurance for restricted diets, older adults and anyone trying to conceive. "Antioxidant formula" megadoses are not ([high-dose-antioxidant-cocktail]). See [multivitamin-mineral].

## 5. Fertility: sperm and eggs

### Sperm

- **Zinc and folate.** Infertile men have lower seminal zinc, and pooled small trials suggest zinc helps [nut-zhao2016]. But FAZST, the largest male supplement trial, gave 2370 men 5 mg folic acid plus 30 mg zinc for 6 months. It found no improvement in semen or live birth (34% vs 35%), and DNA fragmentation was slightly higher (+2.4 points) [nut-schisterman2020]. Sperm DNA methylation did not change [nut-jenkins2022]. See [folate-zinc] and [zinc-status].
- **Selenium.** GPX4 becomes a structural protein forming at least half of the sperm midpiece's mitochondrial capsule, which explains midpiece fragility in deficiency [nut-ursini1999]. In low-status Scottish men, 100 ug/day improved motility [nut-scott1998], and a meta-analysis of small trials was positive [nut-buhling2019]. The largest positive trial [nut-safarinejad2009] now carries a 2023 Expression of Concern [nut-jurol2023]. Cochrane rates male antioxidant evidence low to very low certainty, with no live-birth benefit in low-bias trials [nut-smits2019, nut-deligny2022].
- **Iron.** Iron overload (thalassaemia, haemochromatosis) causes hypogonadism and sperm DNA damage [nut-carlomagno2026, nut-perera2002]. Seminal iron correlates with poorer motility [nut-ammar2019]. Nothing shows that normal dietary iron harms sperm. See [iron-overload-sperm].

### Eggs, conception and pregnancy

- **Folate.** 400-800 ug/day folic acid for anyone who could become pregnant is an A-grade USPSTF recommendation for neural tube defect prevention [nut-uspstf2023, nut-asrm2022]. Folic acid users also conceive faster (FR 1.15) [nut-cueto2016]. Multivitamin users had 41% less ovulatory infertility [nut-chavarro2008]. See [folic-acid].
- **B12.** Women with serum B12 in the top quartile before IVF had twice the live birth probability (RR 2.04) [nut-gaskins2015]. This is observational only.
- **Iodine.** Women with very low urinary iodine had 46% lower monthly chance of conceiving [nut-mills2018]. The American Thyroid Association recommends iodine supplementation before and during pregnancy [nut-alexander2017, nut-korevaar2026].
- **Iron.** In the Nurses' Health Study II, iron supplement users had 40% less ovulatory infertility (RR 0.60) [nut-chavarro2006]. Two newer cohorts of 4662 pregnancy planners found no consistent link except in women prone to deficiency [nut-hahn2019]. In mice, iron-overloaded follicular fluid from endometriosis triggered granulosa-cell ferroptosis [nut-ni2022]. See [iron-egg].

## Where the evidence is contested

1. **The iron hypothesis.** The case for harm: Sullivan [nut-sullivan1981], ferritin-diabetes meta-analyses [nut-jiang2019, nut-kunutsor2013], MR for diabetes and lifespan [nut-wang2021, nut-daghlas2021], and FeAST's cancer signal [nut-zacharski2008]. The case against: no ferritin-CHD association [nut-danesh1999, nut-sempos2000], a null primary trial [nut-zacharski2007, nut-hu2007], MR showing iron slightly *protects* coronary arteries [nut-gill2017, nut-liu2024], and the view that ferritin mostly marks inflammation [nut-kell2014]. Reviewers still call the question unresolved [nut-wood2004].
2. **Is blood donation healthy?** Kuopio says yes [nut-salonen1998]; the Health Professionals cohort says no [nut-ascherio2001]. Most of the apparent benefit is self-selection [nut-ullum2015, nut-atsma2011]. The only RCT in metabolic syndrome is small and unblinded [nut-houschyar2012, nut-manco2012].
3. **Iron for iron-replete people.** Harms show up in infants in high-infection settings [nut-sazawal2006, nut-jaeggi2015] and in gut markers in adults [nut-lund1999]. Yet iron still helped iron-replete pregnant women [nut-hansen2023], and EFSA could not define a harmful dose [nut-efsa2024iron].
4. **Selenium.** Agreed: the U-curve and harm above repletion [nut-rayman2012, nut-stranges2007, nut-rayman2018]. Disputed: the prostate analysis [nut-kristal2014, nut-frankel2014], and male fertility benefit, where the key trial has an Expression of Concern [nut-safarinejad2009, nut-jurol2023] and Cochrane certainty is very low [nut-deligny2022].
5. **Zinc and inflammation.** A positive meta-analysis [nut-hosseini2021] versus a methodological critique [nut-jafari2021]. For sperm, small positive trials [nut-zhao2016] versus the large null FAZST [nut-schisterman2020].
6. **Folic acid: benefit or risk?** Stroke benefit in unfortified populations [nut-huo2015, nut-stampfer2015] and a modest stroke effect in Cochrane [nut-marticarvajal2017], against cancer signals [nut-ebbing2009, nut-cole2007]. EFSA judged the cancer evidence insufficient [nut-efsa2023folate].
7. **Multivitamins.** PHS II cancer benefit [nut-gaziano2012] versus COSMOS null cancer result [nut-sesso2022], multiplicity concerns [nut-bach2012] and no mortality benefit in cohorts [nut-loftfield2024, nut-barnard2024]. COSMOS cognition is a new positive [nut-vyas2024] that PHS II did not find [nut-grodstein2013].
8. **Vitamin C targets.** About 200 mg/day from pharmacokinetics [nut-levine1996] versus about 120 mg/day from epidemiology [nut-carr1999] versus the 75-90 mg RDA [nut-iom2000]. Nobody's data support grams per day for replete people [nut-levine2001].
9. **Magnesium.** A "public health crisis" opinion [nut-dinicolantonio2018] versus modest RCT effects confined to depleted or inflamed people [nut-veronese2022, nut-nielsen2010].
10. **Vitamin E mortality.** The dose-response meta-analysis [nut-miller2005] drew many critical letters and relied on small trials in sick patients. EFSA nonetheless kept a conservative UL [nut-efsa2024vite].

## Practical bottom line

- **Test, then treat.** Useful tests where risk factors exist: ferritin (plus CRP and transferrin saturation when ferritin is high), B12 (plus methylmalonic acid if borderline), and selenium only if you live in a low-selenium region or eat very restrictively.
- **Iron:** treat deficiency firmly. Menstruating women, pregnant women, vegans, frequent donors and endurance athletes should check ferritin. Men and postmenopausal women should not take iron "just in case" and should choose iron-free multivitamins. High ferritin calls for a medical work-up, not home phlebotomy.
- **Selenium:** most North Americans need no supplement; never exceed about 200 ug/day from supplements.
- **Zinc:** stay at or below 25-40 mg/day total from supplements, and pair long-term zinc with copper.
- **Vitamin C:** about 200 mg/day from food saturates you. Smokers need more.
- **B12:** a reliable source is essential for vegans, people over 50, and long-term metformin or PPI users.
- **Before conception:** 400-800 ug/day folic acid, about 150 ug/day iodine, and an RDA-level prenatal multivitamin with ~27 mg iron, plus checking ferritin and B12. Men: no need for zinc or folate megadoses (FAZST). Correct genuine deficiencies through diet.
- **Avoid megadose formulas** that stack selenium, zinc, vitamin A, vitamin E and folic acid near or above ULs.

## What we don't know

- Whether lowering high-normal ferritin (by donation or diet) prevents diabetes in people without haemochromatosis. No adequately powered RCT exists.
- How to separate iron from inflammation in ferritin. Better markers of labile or "toxic" iron are missing [nut-wood2004, nut-kell2014].
- Whether ferroptosis matters for human sperm, egg or embryo quality outside rare overload states; current data are mechanistic [nut-ni2022].
- Whether correcting mild selenium, zinc, riboflavin or iodine shortfalls improves fertility. Trials have used unselected, mostly replete people.
- Who benefits from multivitamins. Effect modification by baseline status has been under-studied in trials of well-fed volunteers.
- Long-term effects of high folic acid exposure in fortified populations that also take supplements.
