# What to test (and what not to)

Oxidative stress cannot be read off a single blood test. The markers that measure it best (urinary F2-isoprostanes, 8-OHdG) are research tools without reference ranges, and the consumer panels that claim to measure it are not valid. What a person *can* test are the upstream drivers that create oxidative stress (high blood sugar, iron overload, inflammation, fatty liver, smoking-related damage) and the outcomes that matter for fertility (semen quality, sperm DNA fragmentation, ovarian reserve). This page sorts the available tests by one question: **would the result change what you do?**

A few genetic tests also pass that test. HFE (iron overload) and G6PD (oxidant sensitivity) change concrete behaviour. Most "antioxidant gene" and "detox" panels do not.

## The core idea: test the drivers, not "oxidative stress"

Oxidative damage in the body comes mostly from a handful of sources covered elsewhere on this site: excess body fat [excess-adiposity], high and swinging blood sugar [glycemic-control], smoking [tobacco-smoking], chronic inflammation [chronic-inflammation], alcohol [alcohol] and, in some people, excess iron [iron-excess]. Standard, cheap, well-validated tests exist for each of these drivers. The research biomarkers of oxidative damage are useful for showing that an intervention works in a trial, but there is no clinical cut-off and no evidence that changing them in an individual improves health [tst-frijhoff2015].

Two lessons from the research literature apply to every test below:

1. **A biomarker can predict disease without being a lever.** Homocysteine, uric acid and GGT all predict cardiovascular disease and death, but lowering homocysteine with B vitamins [tst-lonn2006, tst-bonaa2006, tst-marticarvajal2017] and lowering uric acid with allopurinol [tst-badve2020, tst-doria2020, tst-mackenzie2022] did not prevent the outcomes they predicted.
2. **Reference ranges are not decision thresholds.** WHO semen values describe fertile men, not a fertility cut-off [tst-campbell2021, tst-guzick2001]; AMH tells IVF doctors how many eggs to expect, not whether a woman can conceive naturally [tst-steiner2017].

## General health tests

| CGM (non-diabetic) | Uncertain | Healthy mean ~99 mg/dL, ~96% of time 70-140 mg/dL, CV ~17% [tst-shah2019] | Devices disagree on meal rankings [tst-howard2020] |

| Lipids, apoB | Yes for cardiovascular risk, not an oxidative-stress test | apoB <65 / <80 / <100 mg/dL by risk [tst-mach2020] | |

| GGT | Indirect: prompts look at alcohol and fatty liver | No oxidative-stress cut-off | Probably not causal [tst-nano2017] |

### Blood sugar: HbA1c, fasting glucose and CGM

High glucose is one of the clearest routes to oxidative stress, through glycation and end-products, mitochondrial superoxide and NADPH oxidase activity (see [glycemic-control]). HbA1c and fasting glucose identify prediabetes and diabetes using standard cut-offs [tst-ada2025], and a prediabetic result points straight at levers with strong evidence: [weight-loss], [aerobic-exercise-moderate] and less [refined-sugar-high-glycemic]. For women, it also matters for fertility, because diabetes lowers fecundability (see [glycemic-control], [pcos]).

Continuous glucose monitors are now sold to people without diabetes. Healthy people spend about 96% of the day between 70 and 140 mg/dL, with a median 30 minutes a day above 140 [tst-shah2019]. Enthusiasts point to hidden "glucotypes" in normoglycemic people [tst-hall2018], large person-to-person differences in meal responses [tst-zeevi2015], and a growing case for wellness use [tst-klonoff2023]. But when the same people wore two CGM brands at once, the devices ranked their meals differently (Kendall tau 0.43), so choosing meals by one device lost about half the benefit judged by the other [tst-howard2020]. Whether glucose swings drive oxidative stress independently of average glucose is itself contested: strongly correlated in type 2 diabetes [tst-monnier2006], not at all in type 1 [tst-wentholt2008].

### Inflammation and lipids

hs-CRP below 1 mg/L is low and above 3 mg/L high cardiovascular risk, and values above 10 mg/L point to acute illness [tst-pearson2003]. A persistently raised level is a reason to look for [excess-adiposity], [periodontal-disease], [sleep-apnea] or smoking. JUPITER showed that statins benefit people with normal LDL but hs-CRP of 2 mg/L or more [tst-ridker2008], but USPSTF found adding hs-CRP to standard risk scores has not been shown to improve outcomes [tst-uspstf2018], and CRP's associations depend heavily on other risk factors [tst-erfc2010].

ApoB counts atherogenic particles, and oxidation of those particles in the artery wall is a central step in atherosclerosis. Sniderman argues apoB should replace cholesterol measures [tst-sniderman2019]; the ESC/EAS guideline keeps LDL-C primary and adds apoB targets [tst-mach2020].

### Iron: ferritin, transferrin saturation and the HFE gene

Iron is the one nutrient where "more" directly fuels oxidant chemistry: free iron converts hydrogen peroxide into hydroxyl radicals (Fenton chemistry). Ferritin below 30 µg/L is a reliable sign of iron deficiency [tst-garciacasal2021]. On the other side, raised transferrin saturation with raised ferritin should lead to HFE genotyping; in C282Y homozygotes a TSAT above 45% (women) or 50% (men) with ferritin above 200 or 300 µg/L confirms hemochromatosis, and blood removal to ferritin below 50 then below 100 µg/L prevents organ damage [tst-easl2022]. See [hfe-hemochromatosis] and [iron-excess].

Ferritin alone is a poor overload test because it rises with inflammation, fatty liver and alcohol; in people who are not C282Y homozygotes, a high ferritin is usually one of those [tst-kowdley2019].

### GGT: an oxidative-stress marker hiding in the liver panel?

Lee and colleagues proposed that GGT within the normal range signals oxidative stress: it predicted F2-isoprostanes and CRP up to 15 years later [tst-lee2004] and was inversely related to blood carotenoids and vitamin C in 9,083 US adults [tst-lim2004]. GGT does track mortality, about 7% higher per 5 U/L [tst-kunutsor2014]. But genetically higher GGT did not raise diabetes risk, which points to GGT being a bystander of alcohol, fat and liver stress rather than a cause [tst-nano2017]. Treat a creeping GGT as a nudge to look at [alcohol] and [excess-adiposity].

### Uric acid and homocysteine: predictors that are not levers

Uric acid is the largest single contributor to plasma antioxidant capacity [tst-sies2007] yet predicts hypertension and heart disease, the "oxidant-antioxidant paradox" [tst-sautin2008]. An umbrella review found convincing causal evidence only for gout and kidney stones [tst-li2017], and three large allopurinol trials were null [tst-badve2020, tst-doria2020, tst-mackenzie2022].

Homocysteine follows the same pattern. Observationally 3 µmol/L lower goes with 11% less heart disease and 19% less stroke [tst-hsc2002], but B-vitamin trials did not prevent heart attacks [tst-lonn2006, tst-bonaa2006, tst-marticarvajal2017]. Stroke is the exception: it fell in HOPE-2 and in China's CSPPT, run in a population without folic acid fortification [tst-huo2015]. The useful role of homocysteine is detecting B12 or folate deficiency, which matters before conception (see [folic-acid]).

### Nutrient status: omega-3 index, vitamin D, B12, selenium and zinc

These tests answer "am I low?", which is the only situation in which supplements reliably help.

- **Omega-3 index:** 8% or more is the proposed low-risk zone, 4% or less the high-risk zone [tst-harris2004]. High-dose purified EPA cut events in REDUCE-IT [tst-bhatt2019], but 1 g/day did not in VITAL [tst-manson2019a], and in STRENGTH, people who achieved the highest levels did no better [tst-nissen2021]. See [omega3-fish].
- **Vitamin D:** 20 ng/mL covers 97.5% of people [tst-ross2011]; the older Endocrine Society guideline's 30 ng/mL target [tst-holick2011] was reversed in 2024, when the Society advised against routine testing [tst-demay2024]. USPSTF also found insufficient evidence to screen [tst-uspstf2021], and VITAL found no benefit for cancer or heart disease [tst-manson2019b]. See [vitamin-d].
- **B12:** serum B12 below about 148 pmol/L usually means deficiency; between 148 and 258 pmol/L, MMA or holotranscobalamin decides [tst-stabler2013, tst-hannibal2016]. There is no gold standard, so local ranges apply [tst-devalia2014]. Vegans, metformin users and older adults are the groups to test.
- **Selenium and zinc:** poor biomarkers in non-deficient people. Plasma selenoproteins max out at around 50-70 ng/mL selenium; above that, extra plasma selenium is mostly non-functional, and supplements showed no cancer benefit above about 120 ng/mL [tst-combs2015]. Plasma zinc moves with meals, time of day and inflammation and barely reflects intake [tst-king2015]. See [selenium-supplement] and [folate-zinc].

### Liver enzymes

Fatty liver is a major oxidant source, and high-dose supplements such as [green-tea-extract] can injure the liver. Healthy ALT limits are lower than many lab ranges, about 30 U/L in men and 19 U/L in women [tst-prati2002, tst-kwo2017].

### Research markers of oxidative damage

**F2-isoprostanes** (lipid peroxidation) and **8-OHdG** (DNA oxidation) performed best in the multi-laboratory BOSS validation [tst-kadiiska2005], and isoprostanes measured by mass spectrometry are the reference standard for lipid peroxidation [tst-milne2005]. They are **research tools, not clinical tests**. There is no individual reference range or treatment threshold. ELISA kits for urinary 8-OHdG, which most commercial labs use, cannot give absolute levels [tst-barregard2013], and measuring oxidized guanine in DNA is prone to artefacts [tst-collins2004]. No oxidative-stress biomarker has been validated as a clinical diagnostic [tst-frijhoff2015].

### Tests to skip: total antioxidant capacity, consumer oxidative-stress panels, telomere length

"Total antioxidant capacity" is a misnomer: the main antioxidant defences are enzymes, and plasma TAC mostly reflects urate, vitamin C and vitamin E [tst-sies2007]. Reading a higher TAC as healthier is wrong [tst-pompella2014]; a high uric acid raises TAC while signalling metabolic risk. Consumer telomere tests rest on qPCR methods with inter-laboratory variation above 20% [tst-martinruiz2015], the evidence that telomere length marks ageing is equivocal [tst-mather2011], and genetically longer telomeres raise the risk of several cancers [tst-tmrc2017].

## Sperm tests

### Semen analysis

Semen analysis is the cornerstone. WHO 2021 fifth-centile values from fertile men are: volume 1.4 mL, concentration 16 million/mL, total count 39 million, total motility 42%, progressive motility 30%, vitality 54% and normal forms 4% [tst-who2021, tst-campbell2021]. These are not fertility cut-offs. In a large US study fertile and infertile men overlapped heavily; concentration below 13.5 million/mL, motility below 32% and normal forms below 9% marked the subfertile range, and above 48 million/mL, 63% and 12% the fertile range [tst-guzick2001]. Guidelines ask for at least two analyses [tst-schlegel2021]. The 6th WHO manual dropped reference limits in favour of "decision limits" but did not provide any [tst-boitrelle2021].

### Sperm DNA fragmentation (SDF)

SDF tests measure the share of sperm with broken DNA, the outcome most strongly tied to oxidative stress (see [varicocele], [tobacco-smoking], [febrile-illness], [short-abstinence]). The four common assays differ: SCSA and SCD measure susceptibility to breakage indirectly, while TUNEL and Comet detect breaks directly. Cut-offs are assay-specific: SCSA DFI above about 25-30% is usually called high, and TUNEL or SCD thresholds near 20% are common [tst-agarwal2020, tst-esteves2021].

| Guideline or source | Position |
|---|---|
| AUA/ASRM 2020 | Not in the initial evaluation of the infertile couple [tst-schlegel2021] |
| ASRM 2013 | Tests do not reliably predict outcomes; not recommended routinely [tst-asrm2013] |
| EAU 2021 | SDF testing identifies infertile men and informs ART outcomes; recommended for recurrent loss and unexplained infertility [tst-minhas2021] |
| ESHRE recurrent loss 2023 | Can be considered for explanatory and diagnostic purposes (conditional) [tst-eshrerpl2023] |
| ESHRE add-ons 2023 | Not recommended for routine use; insufficient evidence to predict pregnancy or guide treatment [tst-eshre2023] |
| Expert groups | Use in varicocele with normal semen, unexplained infertility, recurrent loss, failed IUI/IVF, lifestyle risk [tst-agarwal2020, tst-esteves2021] |

The strongest evidence concerns miscarriage: high SDF doubled miscarriage risk (RR 2.16, TUNEL RR 3.94) [tst-robinson2012], and partners of women with recurrent loss average about 12 points higher SDF [tst-mcqueen2019]. For predicting IVF or ICSI pregnancy, the tests perform poorly [tst-collins2008, tst-cissen2016].

### Seminal oxidation-reduction potential (MiOXSYS)

MiOXSYS reports semen ORP divided by sperm concentration. A cut-off of 1.34 mV/10^6 sperm/mL identified abnormal semen with 98% sensitivity but only 41% specificity [tst-agarwal2019a]; pooled sensitivity and specificity for infertility were 0.81 and 0.66 [tst-tan2024]. Its proponents propose a diagnosis of "Male Oxidative Stress Infertility" with ORP guiding antioxidant use [tst-agarwal2019b]. The catch: because the index divides by sperm concentration, men with low counts automatically look "oxidized" [tst-joao2022]. ORP and direct ROS assays disagree, especially when white cells are present [tst-homa2019], and no trial shows that acting on ORP improves live birth [tst-castleton2022].

### Hormones

FSH and testosterone belong in the work-up of men with abnormal semen or symptoms [tst-schlegel2021]. Very low FSH and LH with low sperm in a man taking testosterone or steroids points to suppression (see [testosterone-anabolic-steroids]). EAU advises screening infertile men for hypogonadism [tst-minhas2021].

## Egg tests

### AMH and antral follicle count: egg number, not egg quality

AMH and AFC estimate how many eggs remain. They predict how many eggs IVF will retrieve, and ESHRE recommends them over other reserve tests for dosing stimulation [tst-eshre2020]. AMH or AFC predict poor response well but **add nothing to age for predicting pregnancy after IVF** [tst-broer2013], and AMH's link to live birth after ART is weak [tst-iliodromiti2014]. ASRM calls reserve markers poor predictors of reproductive potential independent of age [tst-asrm2020].

For natural conception, Steiner's cohort of 750 women aged 30-44 is the key study: women with low AMH (below 0.7 ng/mL) were as likely to conceive within 6 cycles as women with normal AMH (65% vs 62%) [tst-steiner2017]. A smaller Dutch study agreed [tst-depmann2017]. AMH also does not track egg quality: it was unrelated to blastocyst aneuploidy [tst-pipari2021]. The counter-evidence is a 3,150-woman cohort in which AMH below 1 ng/mL went with a modest drop in per-cycle conception (11.2% vs 14.3%) [tst-nelson2024].

### Day-3 FSH and estradiol

FSH above about 10 IU/L early in the cycle is specific but insensitive for poor IVF response, and it must be read alongside estradiol [tst-asrm2020]. It did not predict natural conception [tst-steiner2017]. A systematic review concluded no ovarian reserve test can be supported for predicting IVF pregnancy [tst-broekmans2006].

### Tests that are not supported

There is no validated test of egg quality. Home AMH kits answer an egg-count question, follicular-fluid oxidative markers are research measures, and ESHRE does not recommend antioxidant therapy in ART [tst-eshre2023]. Clinicians themselves often order AMH for reassurance or planning, uses not supported by evidence [tst-copp2024]. The levers that do matter for eggs are age ([maternal-age]), smoking ([tobacco-smoking]), body weight and [pcos] management.

## Genetics and individual variation

| Test | Changes a lever? | What to do with the result |
|---|---|---|
| HFE C282Y/H63D | **Yes** | Homozygotes with iron loading: phlebotomy, no iron or high-dose vitamin C supplements [tst-easl2022] |
| G6PD | **Yes** | Avoid fava beans and high-risk drugs; avoid megadose IV vitamin C [tst-gammal2023, tst-alangari2026] |
| GSTM1/GSTT1 | No | Eat crucifers whatever the result [tst-london2000, tst-steck2007] |
| SOD2, GPX1, CAT, PON1 | No | Ignore [tst-bag2008] |
| MTHFR C677T | No | Standard folic acid works across genotypes [tst-crider2011]; ACMG and ACOG advise against testing [tst-hickey2013, tst-acog2018] |
| APOE | Not for oxidative stress | Cell-culture antioxidant differences only [tst-miyata1996, tst-minihane2007] |
| DTC "detox" panels | No | Genotype-based advice adds nothing [tst-celismorales2017, tst-king2023] |

### HFE hemochromatosis

About 1 in 156 people of European descent in UK Biobank are C282Y homozygotes [tst-pilling2019]. In a Melbourne cohort, documented iron-overload disease developed in 28% of homozygous men but only about 1% of women [tst-allen2008]; UK Biobank found excess disease in both sexes over 7 years (men 15.7% vs 5.0%, women 10.1% vs 3.4%) [tst-pilling2019]. C282Y/H63D compound heterozygotes rarely develop disease without cofactors such as fatty liver, diabetes or alcohol [tst-walsh2006]. This is the clearest case on this page where a gene test changes an oxidative-stress lever. See [hfe-hemochromatosis].

### G6PD deficiency

G6PD makes the NADPH red cells need to keep glutathione reduced. About 400-500 million people carry a deficient variant [tst-nkhoma2009, tst-luzzatto2020]. Most never notice, but fava beans [tst-beretta2023], certain drugs or infection can trigger acute hemolysis. CPIC classes dapsone, methylene blue (sometimes sold as a "mitochondrial" supplement), pegloticase, primaquine, rasburicase, tafenoquine and toluidine blue as high risk, nitrofurantoin as medium risk, and vitamin C at standard doses as low-to-no risk [tst-gammal2023]. Very high-dose IV vitamin C is a different matter, and pre-infusion testing is advised [tst-alangari2026]. See [g6pd-deficiency].

### GSTM1/GSTT1 and isothiocyanates

GST enzymes conjugate sulforaphane and other isothiocyanates from crucifers. In Shanghai men, urinary isothiocyanates went with lower lung cancer risk, most strongly in GSTM1-null (RR 0.36) and double-null men (RR 0.28) [tst-london2000]. A crossover study found genotype changed sulforaphane metabolism [tst-gasper2005], but a 114-person feeding study found little difference [tst-steck2007]. GSTM1 null is associated with male infertility in pooled case-control data [tst-wu2013], and GSTT1 null with endometriosis [tst-mear2020]. None of this changes the advice: see [broccoli-sprouts], [cruciferous-other] and [gstm1-genotype].

### SOD2 and other antioxidant-enzyme variants

Single-population studies link SOD2 Val16Ala and PON1 variants to male infertility and sperm DNA damage [tst-ji2012], but the SOD2 variant shows no overall association with cancer in pooled data [tst-bag2008]. The associations are inconsistent and none guides treatment.

### MTHFR: the most overhyped test

MTHFR C677T TT carriers have somewhat higher homocysteine and lower folate, yet folic acid raises their folate just as it does for everyone else [tst-crider2011]. ACMG concluded MTHFR testing has minimal clinical utility [tst-hickey2013]; ACOG does not recommend MTHFR or homocysteine screening in pregnancy [tst-acog2018]; ESHRE's recurrent-loss guideline kept its thrombophilia recommendations unchanged in 2023 [tst-eshrerpl2023]. Case-control meta-analyses do link C677T with male infertility, mainly in Asian studies [tst-li2024]. Even so, no trial shows that genotype-guided folate ("methylfolate") improves fertility. Standard [folic-acid] advice applies to everyone.

### APOE

In cell culture apoE4 has the weakest antioxidant activity (E2 > E3 > E4) [tst-miyata1996]. Human diet-genotype interaction data are heterogeneous and come from small studies [tst-minihane2007]. For oxidative stress and fertility, APOE testing changes nothing.

### Sex and age differences

The textbook picture is that women are protected from oxidative stress by estrogen until menopause [tst-kander2017] and that oxidative stress rises with age [tst-liguori2018]. Population data do not cleanly agree. In Berkeley adults, sex was the strongest predictor of lipid peroxidation, with **women** running higher F2-isoprostanes than men, a stronger effect than smoking [tst-block2002]. In Framingham, female sex was likewise associated with higher urinary isoprostanes and **older** age with lower levels [tst-keaney2003]. A three-country European study found women only slightly lower than men and a moderate fall with age [tst-basu2009]. The likely explanation is that isoprostanes respond to body fat, smoking, glucose, diet (vitamin C) and creatinine indexing at least as much as to sex or age. In practice, sex and age change which tests are relevant (iron overload in men and postmenopausal women; AMH only around IVF; paternal-age effects on SDF, see [paternal-age]) more than they change oxidative-stress targets.

### Direct-to-consumer genetic "detox" and antioxidant panels

Consumer panels that bundle MTHFR, GSTM1, SOD2 and similar variants into "detox" scores have no validated algorithm. In the Food4Me trial, personalized nutrition advice helped, but adding genotype added nothing [tst-celismorales2017], and a meta-analysis found genotype-based advice no better than general advice at changing behaviour [tst-king2023]. ACMG asks that consumers be told about limited clinical validity and given access to genetics professionals [tst-acmg2016]; the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics calls nutrigenomics emerging and loosely regulated [tst-camp2014].

## Where the evidence is contested

- **Sperm DNA fragmentation testing.** Expert groups and EAU support targeted testing [tst-agarwal2020, tst-esteves2021, tst-minhas2021], backed by miscarriage meta-analyses [tst-robinson2012, tst-mcqueen2019]. ASRM, AUA/ASRM and ESHRE advise against routine use because prediction of IVF/ICSI pregnancy is weak [tst-asrm2013, tst-schlegel2021, tst-eshre2023, tst-collins2008, tst-cissen2016]. Real-world practice is highly variable [tst-agarwal2023].
- **MiOXSYS / ORP.** Correlates with semen quality and SDF (pooled r = 0.24) and predicted ICSI outcomes in one cohort [tst-pannerselvam2022, tst-henkel2022, tst-tan2024]; critics show the index is driven by sperm concentration and disagrees with ROS assays [tst-joao2022, tst-homa2019], and clinical benefit is unproven [tst-castleton2022].
- **AMH and natural fertility.** No association in two prospective cohorts [tst-steiner2017, tst-depmann2017] versus a modest association below 1 ng/mL in a larger one [tst-nelson2024].
- **CGM in healthy people.** Personal glycemic responses differ [tst-zeevi2015, tst-hall2018] versus device discordance [tst-howard2020]; glucose variability linked to isoprostanes in one diabetes type but not the other [tst-monnier2006, tst-wentholt2008].
- **GGT as an oxidative-stress marker.** Lee's epidemiology [tst-lee2004, tst-lim2004] versus Mendelian randomization showing no causal effect on diabetes [tst-nano2017].
- **Uric acid.** Pro-oxidant causal role [tst-sautin2008] versus null trials and umbrella review [tst-li2017, tst-badve2020, tst-doria2020, tst-mackenzie2022].
- **Homocysteine.** Null heart outcomes [tst-lonn2006, tst-bonaa2006, tst-marticarvajal2017] versus stroke benefit in an unfortified population [tst-huo2015].
- **Omega-3 index.** Proposed modifiable risk factor [tst-harris2004] and positive EPA trial [tst-bhatt2019] versus null VITAL and no benefit from high achieved levels in STRENGTH [tst-manson2019a, tst-nissen2021].
- **Vitamin D thresholds and testing.** 30 ng/mL [tst-holick2011] versus 20 ng/mL [tst-ross2011]; the Endocrine Society now advises against routine testing [tst-demay2024].
- **hs-CRP.** Endorsed as optional [tst-pearson2003, tst-ridker2008] versus insufficient evidence [tst-uspstf2018].
- **HFE screening.** Expanded case-finding [tst-pilling2019] versus insufficient evidence and low female penetrance [tst-whitlock2006, tst-allen2008].
- **GSTM1 and crucifers.** Null carriers benefit more [tst-london2000] versus less [tst-gasper2005] versus no difference [tst-steck2007].
- **MTHFR.** Case-control association with male infertility [tst-li2024] versus professional advice against testing [tst-hickey2013, tst-acog2018].
- **Sex and age.** Women higher isoprostanes [tst-block2002, tst-keaney2003] versus slightly lower [tst-basu2009]; isoprostanes falling with age [tst-keaney2003, tst-basu2009] versus the ageing-oxidation narrative [tst-liguori2018].
- **G6PD drug lists.** Sparse and conflicting evidence for some drugs, with CPIC authors split on nitrofurantoin [tst-gammal2023]; standard-dose vitamin C low risk [tst-gammal2023] but very high IV doses risky [tst-alangari2026].

## Practical bottom line

**Worth doing for most adults:** HbA1c or fasting glucose, a lipid panel (with apoB if triglycerides are high or you have diabetes or obesity), ferritin with transferrin saturation, and the liver enzymes in a routine panel. Each points to a lever: weight, glucose, alcohol, iron.

**Worth doing in specific situations:**
- B12 with MMA if vegan, over 60, or on metformin or acid suppressants.
- HFE genotype if TSAT is high or a relative has hemochromatosis.
- G6PD before high-risk drugs or IV vitamin C if your ancestry puts you at risk.
- hs-CRP if you are at intermediate cardiovascular risk.
- Omega-3 index if deciding on fish oil and you rarely eat fish.
- 25(OH)D only with an established indication.

**For couples trying to conceive:**
- Semen analysis (two samples) for the male partner early, not only after the woman has been tested.
- FSH and testosterone if semen is abnormal.
- SDF testing for recurrent pregnancy loss, unexplained infertility, varicocele decisions or failed IVF, ideally as SCSA or TUNEL from a lab that reports its own cut-offs.
- AMH and AFC only when planning IVF or egg freezing. A low AMH does not mean you cannot conceive naturally.

**Skip:** total antioxidant capacity and consumer oxidative-stress panels, telomere-length tests, MTHFR, SOD2 and "detox" gene panels, routine selenium or zinc levels, urinary isoprostane or 8-OHdG kits, and home "egg quality" tests.

## What we don't know

- Whether any oxidative-damage biomarker, measured in an individual, can guide treatment to better outcomes. No trial has tested biomarker-guided antioxidant therapy against usual care with live birth or hard clinical endpoints.
- Whether SDF-guided interventions (testicular sperm, varicocele repair, lifestyle change) improve live birth in randomized trials, as opposed to lowering the SDF number.
- Whether ORP adds anything beyond semen analysis and SDF once sperm concentration is accounted for.
- Whether CGM-guided eating in people without diabetes changes any long-term outcome.
- Whether genotype-by-diet interactions (GSTM1 and crucifers, APOE and fats) are large enough to justify testing; the existing trials are small or retrospectively genotyped.
- Why population studies disagree on sex and age differences in isoprostanes, and what reference ranges by sex, age and body size would look like.
