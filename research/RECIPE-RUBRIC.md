# Recipe scoring rubric

Every recipe on the Recipes page gets a 1–10 score in seven categories: five for oxidative stress and two for fertility. A score measures **how much of each evidence-backed ingredient or cooking method one serving delivers**. It does not claim the dish was tested in a trial. Each category is built from factors on the site, so the evidence behind each point is one click away (factor ids in brackets).

Scores rate the recipe **as written** by its original author. Changes that would raise a score go under "Boosts", not into the recipe.

Points are added from the base, then the total is capped at 10 and floored at 1. Nutrient amounts are per serving, estimated from USDA FoodData Central (or the recipe's own nutrition panel, when it shows one and agrees with FDC). One fruit or vegetable serving is 80 g, and potatoes don't count.

## Oxidative stress

### 1. Plant polyphenols (`plants`)
Colourful produce, berries, cocoa, herbs and spices, EVOO phenolics, coffee and tea.
Factors: [high-fruit-vegetable-intake] [blueberries] [berries-other] [cocoa-flavanols] [herbs-spices] [extra-virgin-olive-oil] [leafy-greens] [tomatoes-lycopene] [green-tea] [coffee]

- **Base:** 0.
- **Fruit and vegetable servings:** <0.5 = 0, 0.5 = +1, 1 = +2, 2 = +3, 3 = +4, ≥4 = +5.
- **High-polyphenol items, +1 each, max +4,** each in a meaningful amount per serving:
  - berries ≥ ½ cup (75 g)
  - cocoa powder ≥ 1 tbsp or ≥70% dark chocolate ≥ 10 g
  - extra-virgin olive oil ≥ 1 tbsp (15 mL)
  - herbs and spices ≥ 1 tsp dried or 1 tbsp fresh in total
  - cooked tomato product ≥ ½ cup
  - dark leafy greens ≥ 1 cup raw or ½ cup cooked
  - coffee, green or black tea, or matcha as an ingredient
  - anthocyanin vegetables or fruit ≥ 25 g per serving (about ¼ red onion or ⅓ cup shredded red cabbage): red onion, red cabbage, pomegranate seeds, cherries, purple produce. Syrups and molasses don't count.
- **Diversity:** ≥4 different plant families in the dish = +1.

### 2. Hormetic compounds: Nrf2 and sulfur compounds (`nrf2`)
Crucifers (preparation decides how much sulforaphane forms), myrosinase sources, watercress, wasabi and horseradish, alliums, turmeric.
Factors: [broccoli-sprouts] [broccoli-mature] [cruciferous-other] [mustard-seed-myrosinase] [watercress-peitc] [wasabi-horseradish-itc] [garlic-alliums] [turmeric-curcumin]

- **Base:** 1.
- **Crucifers** (broccoli, cabbage, kale, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, bok choy, collards, rocket/arugula, radish), ≥ 80 g per serving:
  - raw or steamed ≤ 4 min (myrosinase survives): **+5**
  - cooked longer (roast, boil, stir-fry > 5 min, simmered in soup): **+3**, or **+5** if the dish also adds a raw myrosinase source after cooking (mustard seed or powder, raw radish or daikon, raw crucifer, wasabi or horseradish)
  - 40–79 g: halve these points
- **Concentrated isothiocyanate sources, +3:** broccoli sprouts ≥ ¼ cup, watercress ≥ ½ cup raw, or real wasabi, horseradish or brown mustard seed or powder in a meaningful amount.
- **Alliums:** ≥1 garlic clove per serving or ≥¼ onion per serving = +1; both, or ≥2 cloves = +2.
- **Turmeric:** ≥ ½ tsp per serving cooked with fat or black pepper = +1.

### 3. Fat quality (`fats`)
Long-chain omega-3, EVOO, nuts and seeds; penalties for frying, oxidised oils and processed meat.
Factors: [omega3-fish] [extra-virgin-olive-oil] [walnuts-nuts] [flaxseed-lignans] [cooking-oil-choice] [oxidized-frying-oils] [trans-fats] [processed-red-meat]

- **Base:** 2.
- **EPA + DHA:**

  | Per serving | Points |
  |---|---|
  | ≥1500 mg | +6 |
  | 1000–1499 mg | +5 |
  | 500–999 mg | +4 |
  | 250–499 mg | +2 |
  | 100–249 mg | +1 |

- **Extra-virgin olive oil:** main fat ≥ 1 tbsp per serving = +2; ≥ 1 tsp per serving = +1.
- **Nuts and seeds** (walnuts, almonds, flax, chia, hemp, pumpkin and so on): ≥ 15 g per serving = +1; ≥ 30 g = +2.
- **Penalties:**
  - deep-fried: −3
  - butter, cream, coconut oil or lard as the main fat, or saturated fat > 10 g per serving: −2
  - processed meat (bacon, sausage, ham, salami, deli meat): −2

### 4. Blood sugar and fibre (`glycemic`)
Factors: [dietary-fiber-microbiome] [legumes] [whole-grains] [refined-sugar-high-glycemic] [glycemic-control]

- **Base:** 0.
- **Fibre per serving:**

  | Per serving | Points |
  |---|---|
  | ≥12 g | +5 |
  | 8–11.9 g | +4 |
  | 5–7.9 g | +3 |
  | 3–4.9 g | +2 |
  | 1–2.9 g | +1 |

- **Main starch:**
  - whole grain, legume or no starch: +2
  - refined (white rice, white pasta, white bread, white couscous, flour tortillas): 0. Corn tortillas count as whole grain (whole nixtamalised corn)
  - mostly sugar: −2
- **Added sugar per serving** (honey, maple and syrups count): 0–4 g = +2; 5–12 g = +1; 13–25 g = 0; > 25 g = −2.
- **Pairing:** the carbohydrate comes with meaningful protein, fat or vinegar in the same dish = +1.

### 5. Low-oxidant cooking (`cooking`)
Heterocyclic amines, PAHs, AGEs, acrylamide and oxidised frying oil all come from how food is cooked.
Factors: [high-temperature-cooking] [marinating-meat] [acrylamide] [oxidized-frying-oils] [cooking-oil-choice] [herbs-spices]

Score the method of the main component. If a secondary component is worse, use the midpoint of the two.

| Method | Score |
|---|---|
| Raw or no-cook; steam, poach, boil, simmer, braise, stew, slow-cook; bake or roast at ≤ 180 °C / 350 °F; microwave | 10 |
| Sauté or stir-fry at medium-high heat in a stable oil; roast vegetables at ≤ 220 °C / 425 °F; bake fish at 200–220 °C | 8 |
| Pan-sear, roast or broil fish or poultry with browning; grill vegetables | 6 |
| Grill, broil or pan-fry red meat or poultry to well done | 4 (6 with a ≥ 30-min herb or acid marinade) |
| Deep-fry in fresh oil | 3 |
| Charred or blackened meat, processed meat cooked at high heat, starch toasted dark | 2 |

- **Adjustments:**
  - starchy food browned at high heat (crisp roast potatoes, fries, dark toast): −1
  - PUFA-rich seed oil (sunflower, corn, soybean, grapeseed) used for high-heat frying or roasting: −1
  - meat with a spice or herb rub or marinade, already counted in the table above: no double credit

## Fertility

### 6. Sperm support (`sperm`)
Nutrients and patterns linked to semen quality in trials or cohorts.
Factors: [walnuts-nuts] [omega3-fish] [tomatoes-lycopene] [zinc-status] [selenium-supplement] [high-fruit-vegetable-intake] [mediterranean-diet] [processed-meat] [refined-sugar-high-glycemic] [alcohol]

- **Base:** 1.
- **Omega-3 and nuts (max +4 from this group combined):**
  - EPA + DHA ≥ 500 mg = +3; 250–499 mg = +2
  - walnuts or mixed nuts ≥ 30 g = +2; ≥ 60 g = +3. The nut RCTs used 60–75 g per day.
- **Lycopene:** cooked tomato product ≥ ½ cup = +2; ≥ 1 cup fresh tomato = +1.
- **Zinc:** ≥ 3 mg = +1; ≥ 5.5 mg = +2.
- **Selenium:** ≥ 25 µg = +1.
- **Folate:** ≥ 100 µg DFE = +1.
- **Produce:** ≥ 2 fruit or vegetable servings = +1.
- **Penalties:**
  - processed meat: −3
  - added sugar > 25 g per serving: −1
  - alcohol not cooked off (raw, or simmered < 15 min): −1

### 7. Egg and pregnancy support (`egg`)
Preconception and pregnancy nutrients, plus the "profertility" pattern.
Factors: [folic-acid] [vitamin-b12-folate-status] [iron-egg] [iodine-status] [omega3-fish] [mercury-fish] [profertility-diet] [soy] [mediterranean-diet] [alcohol]

- **Base:** 1.
- **Folate:** ≥ 200 µg DFE = +3; 100–199 µg = +2; 50–99 µg = +1. Food folate supports intake but doesn't replace the 400–800 µg folic acid supplement.
- **DHA from low-mercury fish:** EPA + DHA ≥ 500 mg = +2; 250–499 mg = +1.
- **Iron:** ≥ 4 mg, with ≥ 25 mg vitamin C in the same dish if the iron is plant iron = +1.
- **Choline:** ≥ 150 mg = +1. The adequate intake in pregnancy is 450 mg/day (NASEM); eggs, fish, meat and soy are the main sources.
- **Iodine source** (dairy, eggs, seafood, seaweed, or iodised salt named in the recipe): +1.
- **B12:** ≥ 1 µg = +1.
- **Profertility components:** ≥ 2 different components from this list = +1: whole grain, soy food, seafood, dairy, ≥ 2 produce servings. Produce alone is only one component.
- **Penalties:**
  - high-mercury fish (swordfish, shark, king mackerel, bigeye tuna, marlin, tilefish, orange roughy): −3
  - alcohol not cooked off: −2
  - liver (preformed vitamin A in pregnancy): −1

## Scoring conventions
These settle edge cases so every recipe is scored the same way.

- **Thresholds are strict:** 0.97 tsp does not meet a 1 tsp threshold.
- **Halved points** (the 40–79 g crucifer band) are rounded down.
- **Crucifer preparation:** the +5 "myrosinase survives" tier needs raw crucifers, or crucifers steamed or microwaved for ≤ 4 min. Any sauté, stir-fry, roast or simmer counts as cooked (+3), however short.
- **Cooking midpoint:** use it only for a separate, harsher component, such as seared meat or crisp roast potatoes beside a stew. Gently softening onions, garlic or greens as the first step of a simmered or baked dish doesn't lower the score.
- **Legumes** count as at most one fruit-and-vegetable serving per dish.
- **Potatoes and sweet potatoes:** as the main starch, white potatoes score 0 (high glycemic, like refined starch) and sweet potato, squash or other starchy vegetables score +1. Sweet potato counts as a vegetable serving; white potato doesn't.
- **Option choices** ("king or sockeye", "1 or 2 cans"): score the first-listed option, and put the other one in boosts if it scores higher.
- **Folate:** count natural food folate only. Ignore folic acid from fortified flour, pasta or rice, because fortification differs by country.
- **Servings:** score per the original's serving count. When the original gives a range ("serves 4 to 6") and its nutrition panel doesn't settle it, use the lower number, which is a full meal-sized portion. If the original's serving is unrealistically small (one energy bite), score a realistic portion and say so in `changes`.

## Pregnancy watch-outs (flagged, not scored)
- raw or lightly cooked sprouts
- raw or undercooked eggs, meat or fish
- unpasteurised cheese or milk
- deli meats served cold
- high-mercury fish
- alcohol
- liver
- more than ~200 mg caffeine per serving

These follow FDA/USDA and ACOG food-safety advice.

## Complexity
| Level | Active time | Ingredients (not counting salt, pepper, oil, water) | Components |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | ≤ 20 min | ≤ 10 | one pot, pan or bowl, or no cooking |
| Medium | ≤ 45 min | ≤ 15 | up to two components |
| Involved | longer | more | several techniques or components |

A recipe that meets any part of a higher level moves up.
