# Body, habits and medicines: what survives a confounding audit

This audit re-checks 42 of the 56 "body" factors on Redox Compass (exercise, sleep, light, stress, weight, alcohol, smoking, vaping, cannabis, medicines, skin and UV, iron and vitamin status) against studies designed to separate cause from coincidence. It asks two questions for each headline: is it confounded, and has newer or better evidence changed the picture? Every source is dated so you can see what is new.

## How confounding works in this area

Most claims about lifestyle and health start with cohorts: follow many people, record what they do, see who gets ill. The trouble is that what people do is tangled up with who they already are.

- **Reverse causation ("sick quitters").** People who are becoming ill stop drinking, stop exercising, sleep longer and go outside less. Then the "abstainers", the "inactive" and the "long sleepers" look unhealthy because illness put them there. In Whitehall II, physical activity began falling up to nine years before a dementia diagnosis [abody-sabia2017]. Former drinkers inside the "non-drinker" group made moderate drinking look protective for decades [abody-stockwell2016].
- **Healthy-user and healthy-adherer effects.** People who donate blood, take saunas, use their CPAP every night or run marathons tend to be healthier and better off to begin with. Blood donors live longer, but in a 2025 target-trial emulation the gap vanished after adjustment (HR 0.57 crude, 1.0 adjusted) [abody-rahman2025].
- **Residual confounding by socioeconomic status.** Shift workers are more often in lower social classes; one cohort's heart-disease excess disappeared once social class was included [abody-yadegarfar2008].
- **Genetic confounding.** The same genes can drive both a behaviour and survival. Identical twins who differed in activity did not differ in mortality, and rats bred for fitness did not live longer when exercised [abody-karvinen2015].
- **Time-related biases in drug studies.** Immortal-time bias gave metformin an illusory 20-94% cancer reduction in 13 cohorts [abody-suissa2012]. Protopathic bias made NSAIDs look like a miscarriage cause: women take painkillers for the cramping of a miscarriage already under way [abody-daniel2015b].
- **Collider bias.** Studying only sick people (heart failure, dialysis) can make obesity look protective, the "obesity paradox" [abody-horita2023].

The tools that cut through this are randomised trials, Mendelian randomization (MR: using genes that nudge an exposure as a natural experiment), sibling and twin comparisons, negative controls and target-trial emulation. MR has its own pitfalls. Non-linear MR methods that produced J-shaped curves for BMI and other exposures turned out to be biased; the BMI paper was reanalysed in 2024 [abody-burgess2023, abody-burgess2024, abody-hamilton2024], and genetic instruments for behaviours such as TV watching overlap strongly with education and BMI [abody-vandevegte2020].

## The most important reversals and confirmations

**1. Moderate drinking does not protect (2014-2024).** Without correction, 87 cohorts reproduce the J-curve; after separating lifetime abstainers from former drinkers and adjusting for study quality, low-volume drinkers show no benefit (RR 0.97) [abody-stockwell2016]. The 2023 update of 107 cohorts agrees (RR 0.93, not significant; harm from 45 g/day) [abody-zhao2023]. Genetics settles much of it: in 512,715 Chinese adults, genotype-predicted alcohol intake raised stroke risk in a straight line with no dip [abody-millwood2019]; people carrying an ADH1B variant who drink less have less coronary disease [abody-holmes2014]; UK Biobank MR shows rising cardiovascular risk and a linear rise in mortality [abody-biddinger2022, abody-kassaw2024]. WHO staff conclude no safe level for cancer can be set [abody-anderson2023]. The site already says J-curves are confounded; it can now say so with citations.

**2. Exercise: the mortality number is softer than it looks (1998 to 2025).** The original Finnish twin study supported causality (OR 0.44 within discordant twins) [abody-kujala1998]. Later work on the same twin resource found no difference within identical twin pairs [abody-karvinen2015] and only a short-lived 7% gap that weakened after accounting for family and prevalent disease [abody-kankaanpaa2025]. MR finds no effect on coronary disease, stroke or mortality [abody-bahls2021, abody-han2026], though one MR supports lower hypertension and diabetes with vigorous activity [abody-zhao2024]. The only long-term RCT in older adults was not significant [abody-stensvold2020]. None of this says exercise is useless: it reliably improves fitness, blood pressure and glucose. It says the 30-37% figure is a ceiling.

**3. Statin side effects are mostly nocebo (2021-2022).** In SAMSON, symptoms in placebo months were 90% of those in statin months [abody-howard2021]; StatinWISE n-of-1 trials in people who had quit statins found no difference from placebo [abody-herrett2021]; the CTT meta-analysis agrees [abody-ctt2022]. This confirms and strengthens the site.

**4. Inflammation became a proven cause, CRP did not (2011-2025).** Genetically raised CRP does not raise coronary risk [abody-ccgc2011], but the IL-6 receptor pathway does [abody-il6r2012], and blocking IL-1-beta (CANTOS) or using colchicine in stable coronary disease cut events [abody-ridker2017, abody-nidorf2020]. Colchicine right after a heart attack did not (CLEAR, 2025) [abody-jolly2025].

**5. The glucose-swing claim did not replicate (2006 vs 2008-2011).** The site's headline r = 0.86 between glucose variability and isoprostanes came from 21 patients [abody-monnier2006]. Two later CGM studies, in type 1 and in well-controlled type 2 diabetes, found no relationship [abody-wentholt2008, abody-siegelaar2011]. Separately, aggressively pushing HbA1c below 6% in high-risk patients raised mortality [abody-accord2008].

Other notable shifts: GLP-1 drugs now have a hard-outcome trial (SELECT, HR 0.80) [abody-lincoff2023]; short sleep gained causal support while long sleep looks like an illness marker [abody-daghlas2019, abody-cappuccio2010]; the obesity paradox dissolves in never-smokers and in corrected MR [abody-gbmc2016, abody-burgess2024]; and the NSAID-miscarriage link shrank once protopathic and immortal-time bias were handled [abody-daniel2014, abody-daniel2015a, abody-daniel2015b].

## Where the evidence is contested

- **Alcohol at low doses.** Abstainer-bias meta-analyses and MR find no benefit [abody-zhao2023, abody-millwood2019], but GBD 2020 still models a J-shaped burden curve for people over 40, with minimum risk at 0.1-1.9 drinks a day depending on region [abody-gbd2022]. A 2025 US National Academies review for the Dietary Guidelines also re-examined moderate drinking [abody-nasem2025]; we could not retrieve its graded conclusions this session, so the site should not quote numbers from it without checking. Non-linear MR, used by some of the genetic papers, can be biased [abody-hamilton2024].
- **Whether exercise lengthens life.** Cohort dose-response is strong [abody-arem2015]; twin, MR and RCT data are weak or null [abody-karvinen2015, abody-bahls2021, abody-ballin2021]. Defenders of the cohort reading note that the main trial compared training with controls who were told to follow activity guidelines [abody-stensvold2020], so it tested more versus some exercise, not exercise versus none.
- **Sitting as a cause.** One MR finds TV watching causal for coronary disease (OR 1.44 per 1.5 h) [abody-vandevegte2020]; others find nothing [abody-bahls2021, abody-han2026]; an umbrella review grades the observational evidence weak [abody-rahmati2025].
- **Sauna.** Large benefits in the 2015 KIHD paper [abody-laukkanen2015] shrank to HR 0.86 in a 2022 re-analysis of the same cohort [abody-kunutsor2022]; trials are few and small [abody-hussain2018].
- **Shift work.** Meta-analysis supports a 17% CVD excess [abody-torquati2018]; adjustment for social class removed it in one cohort [abody-yadegarfar2008]; breast cancer links are null in prospective data [abody-travis2016].
- **Stress and IVF.** Pre-treatment distress does not predict IVF failure [abody-boivin2011], but distress measured during treatment does [abody-purewal2018], which may reflect how the cycle is going.
- **Periodontal treatment and diabetes.** A 514-person trial was null for HbA1c [abody-engebretson2013]; a 264-person, 12-month trial found a 0.6% fall [abody-daiuto2018].
- **Cannabis and sperm.** One small clinic study found more DNA damage [abody-verhaeghe2020]; a meta-analysis found no significant association with abnormal semen [abody-belladelli2021].
- **CPAP.** Overall trials are null for cardiovascular events [abody-mcevoy2016]; the benefit seen in good adherers [abody-sanchezdelatorre2023] is exactly where healthy-adherer bias lives.
- **Prenatal acetaminophen.** Sibling analyses and umbrella reviews point to confounding [abody-ahlqvist2024, abody-sheikh2025, abody-tayebihillali2026]; a Navigation Guide review urges precaution [abody-prada2025].
- **Metformin and ageing.** A 2025 target-trial emulation favoured metformin over sulfonylureas for reaching 90 (HR 0.70) [abody-shadyab2025], but against a non-placebo comparator; registry and twin data contradicted the earlier survival advantage [abody-keys2022].

## What changed recently (2022-2026)

- 2022: UK Biobank MR on alcohol and cardiovascular disease [abody-biddinger2022]; PATH shows exclusive vaping not linked to CVD while dual use is [abody-berlowitz2022]; MR meta-analysis of smoking's causal reach [abody-larsson2022].
- 2023: alcohol meta-analysis of 107 cohorts [abody-zhao2023]; WHO "no safe level" statement [abody-anderson2023]; SELECT trial [abody-lincoff2023]; CPAP IPD meta-analysis [abody-sanchezdelatorre2023].
- 2024: corrected non-linear BMI MR [abody-burgess2024]; alcohol mortality MR [abody-kassaw2024]; Swedish sibling acetaminophen study [abody-ahlqvist2024]; PPI-dementia MR null [abody-xie2024].
- 2025: twin study attributing the activity-mortality link to genes and reverse causation [abody-kankaanpaa2025]; fitness MR null for longevity [abody-kjaergaard2025]; CLEAR colchicine trial null [abody-jolly2025]; blood donation target-trial emulation [abody-rahman2025]; BMJ acetaminophen umbrella review [abody-sheikh2025]; light at night and CVD [abody-windred2025]; cold-water meta-analysis [abody-cain2025].
- 2026: VILPA replication weakens after excluding prevalent disease [abody-koemel2026]; physical activity MR null for mortality [abody-han2026]; acetaminophen meta-meta-analysis [abody-tayebihillali2026].

## Practical bottom line

- Drink less; there is no health reason to start, and the "glass of wine for the heart" idea has not survived better-designed studies.
- Keep exercising: the benefits on fitness, blood pressure, glucose and strength are proven. Just do not treat cohort mortality percentages as what you personally will gain.
- Protect sleep duration; short sleep now has causal evidence behind it. Very long sleep is more likely a sign of illness than a cause.
- Losing excess fat matters, and the obesity paradox is mostly an artefact; GLP-1 drugs now have heart-outcome evidence.
- If statins gave you muscle aches, a supervised re-challenge usually works.
- Treat sauna, cold plunges, meditation and morning light as pleasant habits with modest, uncertain effects, not proven longevity tools.
- Be wary of any single cohort that finds big benefits from a habit healthy people already choose.
