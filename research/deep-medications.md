# Medications, oxidative stress & fertility

> **This page informs; it does not prescribe.** Never stop, start or change the dose of a prescribed medicine because of anything here. Take questions to the prescriber or pharmacist. Several of these drugs (metformin, statins, GLP-1 drugs, antidepressants, opioids) treat conditions that harm health and fertility far more than the side effects discussed below.

## Why medicines belong on a redox site

Many common drugs work on the same machinery this site is about. Metformin dampens the mitochondrial respiratory chain. Statins lower the oxidizable LDL that feeds lipid peroxidation. Acetaminophen is detoxified with glutathione. NSAIDs block the prostaglandins that help muscles adapt to training and help the ovary release an egg. Some drugs also change nutrient status (B12, iron, magnesium) or the gut microbes that turn food polyphenols into absorbable compounds.

Three themes recur:

1. **Hormesis.** Exercise works partly through brief oxidative and energy-stress signals. Drugs that blunt those signals (metformin, perhaps statins, high-dose NSAIDs) can blunt some training gains, much as gram-level antioxidant supplements do ([antioxidant-megadoses-during-training]).
2. **Fertility side effects that a standard test misses.** Paroxetine doubled sperm DNA fragmentation while the routine semen analysis stayed normal [med-tanrikut2010]. NSAIDs can stop the follicle from releasing its egg while the cycle looks normal [med-micu2011].
3. **Interactions.** Supplements popular with antioxidant-minded readers can interfere with drugs (St John's wort with the pill, vitamin E with anticoagulants, NAC with nitroglycerin), and high-dose antioxidants may protect tumour cells during cancer treatment.

## Metformin

**How it works.** Metformin collects inside mitochondria and inhibits respiratory-chain complex I, lowering cellular energy charge and activating AMPK; it also has AMPK-independent, gut and lysosomal actions [med-owen2000, med-rena2017]. It suppresses NF-kB-linked inflammation even in people without diabetes [med-cameron2016].

**Exercise adaptations.** This is the best-studied drug-exercise interaction, and it is genuinely contested.

| Study | Who | Result |
|---|---|---|
| MASTERS RCT [med-walton2019] | 94 healthy adults 65+, 14 wk resistance training, 1700 mg/day | Placebo gained more lean mass and thigh muscle; strength trend smaller on metformin |
| Konopka RCT [med-konopka2019] | 53 older adults, 12 wk aerobic training | Metformin blunted gains in insulin sensitivity and VO2max and abolished the rise in mitochondrial respiration |
| Malin [med-malin2012] | 32 adults with prediabetes | Insulin-sensitivity gain 25-30% higher with exercise alone (not significant) |
| Moreno-Cabanas [med-morenocabanas2022] | 63 adults with metabolic syndrome, 16 wk HIIT | VO2max gain halved in metformin users (12.7% vs 25.3%) |
| DARE [med-boule2013] | 225 adults with type 2 diabetes, 22 wk | No attenuation of HbA1c, fitness or strength gains |
| Pilmark RCT [med-pilmark2021] | 29 glucose-intolerant adults, 12 wk | VO2peak +15% in both groups; no difference |
| Look AHEAD [med-terada2019] | 1982 adults with diabetes | Slightly less weight loss on metformin, fitness gains unchanged |
| Meta-analysis 2026 [med-etayourtasun2026] | 9 trials, 827 adults | Modest VO2peak penalty (-1.19 mL/kg/min) and smaller BP falls; glucose, weight, lipids unchanged |

Metformin also raises lactate during exercise [med-grammer2026, med-boule2011]. Muscle gene-expression work from MASTERS suggests metformin may dampen hypertrophy pathways while nudging some aging-related pathways favourably [med-kulkarni2020]. Bottom line: a modest, inconsistent blunting, seen mostly in older or prediabetic people taking metformin for research. For people with diabetes it is not a reason to stop either metformin or exercise.

**The anti-aging (TAME) story.** TAME was proposed partly because a UK cohort found metformin-treated diabetics outlived matched non-diabetics [med-barzilai2016, med-bannister2014]. A Danish registry and twin reanalysis found the opposite: higher mortality than people without diabetes (adjusted IRR 1.32, twins 1.64) [med-keys2022]. Critics argue benefits in healthy people are unknown and could clash with exercise [med-konopka2019tame, med-miller2020].

**Vitamin B12.** Metformin 850 mg three times a day lowered B12 by 19% over 4.3 years, raised deficiency risk by 7.2 percentage points and raised homocysteine [med-dejager2010]. In the 13-year DPP follow-up, each year of use raised deficiency odds 13%, and neuropathy was more common in users with low B12 [med-aroda2016]. Pooled trials show a fall of about 57 pmol/L within 3 months [med-chapman2016]. Long-term users can ask about periodic B12 testing.

**PCOS and fertility.** Cochrane: metformin may improve live birth versus placebo (OR 1.59, low quality) but quadruples GI side effects; whether it adds anything to clomiphene is uncertain, and clomiphene is better in obese women [med-sharpe2019]. ASRM says metformin should not be first-line for anovulation because letrozole or clomiphene work much better [med-asrm2017], and the 2023 international PCOS guideline puts letrozole first [med-teede2023]. Metformin's main PCOS role is metabolic ([pcos], [glycemic-control]).

**Men.** Sperm data are tiny and uncontrolled (one study of 34 hyperinsulinaemic men reported better morphology and chromatin) [med-bosman2015]. A Danish cohort linked paternal metformin during sperm development to birth defects (aOR 1.40) [med-wensink2022], but a Norway-Taiwan study of over 3 million offspring that controlled for diabetes severity found none (pooled RR 0.89) [med-meng2024]. The second, larger and better-adjusted study is more reassuring.

## GLP-1 drugs: semaglutide and tirzepatide

**Weight and inflammation.** Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly cut weight 14.9% vs 2.4% over 68 weeks [med-wilding2021]; tirzepatide 15 mg cut it 20.9% vs 3.1% over 72 weeks [med-jastreboff2022]. Switching to placebo after 20 weeks regained 6.9% of weight [med-rubino2021]. A 40-RCT meta-analysis found lower CRP (-0.54 mg/L), TNF-alpha and malondialdehyde and higher adiponectin [med-bray2021]; a 2026 update confirmed CRP and adiponectin but found IL-6 and TNF-alpha effects non-significant and the MDA result based on only 3 trials [med-kanbay2026]. Most of the redox benefit is probably the weight loss itself ([weight-loss], [excess-adiposity]).

**Lean mass.** Lean tissue made up about 35% of weight lost on semaglutide and 25% on tirzepatide, similar to diet-based weight loss (26%), but only about 17.5% when lifestyle included resistance training [med-eisa2026]. Some argue the loss is proportional and adaptive [med-neeland2024]; another review found muscle losses exceeded benchmarks in about two thirds of incretin trials [med-batsis2026]. Practical answer: lift weights and eat enough protein while on these drugs ([resistance-training]).

**Women: PCOS, contraception and washout.**

- In PCOS, GLP-1 drugs beat metformin for weight and insulin sensitivity [med-han2019], and a 28-woman pilot found higher IVF pregnancy rates with liraglutide plus metformin [med-salamun2018]. But the 2026 meta-analysis informing the PCOS guideline found only modest extra weight loss (BMI -1.38) and insufficient evidence on reproductive outcomes [med-forslund2026].
- **Tirzepatide and the pill.** A single 5 mg dose cut ethinyl estradiol peak levels 59% (AUC 20%); the label advises switching to a non-oral method or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase [med-fda-zepbound2023]. Other GLP-1 drugs showed no clinically significant effect on the pill [med-skelley2024].
- **Washout.** The semaglutide label says stop at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy because of its long half-life [med-fda-wegovy2021]. Reviews stress washout planning and reliable contraception [med-dilbaz2026, med-drummond2025].
- **If exposed early in pregnancy.** Malformation risk was not raised versus insulin (RR 0.95) [med-cesta2024], and a 2026 systematic review found no consistent safety signal [med-ozbek2026]. A Danish cohort linked periconceptional use to preterm birth only in women with diabetes [med-hviid2026]. Critics note these studies lack glycaemic-control data [med-drummond2025].

**Men.** An 8-week diet that cut 16.5 kg raised sperm concentration 1.49-fold, and the gain lasted a year in men who kept the weight off, including those maintained on liraglutide [med-andersen2022]. In obese diabetic men with low testosterone, semaglutide raised testosterone and normal sperm forms, while testosterone therapy lowered sperm counts [med-gregoric2025]. Reviews find testosterone rises with LH/FSH preserved but no semen change in healthy men, and too few trials overall [med-deameh2026, med-peel2026].

## Statins

**Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects.** Statins block isoprenoid synthesis, which reduces Rac-dependent NADPH oxidase activity in cells and animals [med-oesterle2017]. In people, atorvastatin 80 mg cut oxidized LDL 39% and lowered hsCRP and NF-kB activity [med-singh2008]; rosuvastatin cut hsCRP 37% and nearly halved cardiovascular events in JUPITER [med-ridker2008]. Simvastatin's fall in F2-isoprostanes tracked LDL lowering, and adding vitamin E 600 mg/day added nothing [med-decaterina2002] - suggesting much of the "antioxidant" effect is simply less LDL to oxidize.

**Muscle symptoms and CoQ10.** In 19 double-blind trials (123,940 people), statins produced an excess of muscle pain of about 11 per 1000 person-years in year 1; only about 1 in 15 muscle complaints on statins was actually drug-caused [med-ctt2022]. Statins lower blood CoQ10, which motivates supplementation [med-taylor2015]. The evidence conflicts:

| Source | Result |
|---|---|
| Banach 2015, 6 RCTs [med-banach2015] | No significant effect on pain or CK |
| Taylor 2015 RCT, confirmed myalgia [med-taylor2015] | Ubiquinol 600 mg/day raised CoQ10 fourfold but did not reduce pain |
| Qu 2018, 12 RCTs [med-qu2018] | Less pain, weakness, cramps and tiredness |

**Exercise.** Simvastatin 40 mg cut the 12-week fitness gain from 10% to 1.5% and turned a mitochondrial gain into a loss [med-mikus2013]. But in 10,043 veterans, fit statin users had the lowest mortality of all (HR 0.30) [med-kokkinos2013], and atorvastatin did not reduce habitual activity versus placebo [med-panza2016].

**Pregnancy.** In July 2021 the FDA removed the pregnancy contraindication, citing no increase in birth defects, while advising most patients to stop on learning they are pregnant and allowing very high-risk patients to continue [med-fda-statins2021]. Large cohorts and meta-analyses found no excess malformations [med-bateman2015, med-vahedianazimi2021, med-dutta2026]; a Taiwan cohort found more low birth weight (RR 1.51) and preterm birth (RR 1.99) [med-chang2021].

**Sperm.** One 17-man pilot found a 31% fall in sperm count on atorvastatin [med-ponsrejraji2014], but a systematic review and a clinic study of 118 users found no clinically relevant effect [med-anagnostis2021, med-keihani2018a].

## SGLT2 inhibitors (brief)

Proposed reasons for their heart and kidney benefits include natriuresis, ketone fuel use, lower inflammation, oxidative stress and AGE signalling, mostly from mechanistic work [med-cowie2020]. The practical point for readers of this site: these drugs can cause **euglycemic ketoacidosis** with near-normal glucose [med-peters2015], and ketogenic diets raise that risk; one case developed within a week of starting a ketogenic diet [med-mistry2021]. Do not combine them with [ketogenic-diet], [low-carb-diet] or extended [intermittent-fasting] without the prescriber.

## Acetaminophen (paracetamol)

**Glutathione.** Each dose is partly turned into a reactive metabolite that is neutralized by glutathione. Even 600-1200 mg doses speed up turnover of the cysteine used to make glutathione [med-lauterburg1987]. Starting 4 g/day (the label maximum) raised ALT above three times normal in 31-44% of healthy adults within two weeks, versus none on placebo [med-watkins2006]. Overdose exhausts glutathione and causes liver failure; the glutathione precursor [n-acetylcysteine] is the antidote. Occasional normal doses are not a meaningful oxidative-stress problem for healthy people.

**Pregnancy debate.** A 2021 consensus statement signed by 91 scientists urged use only when indicated, at the lowest dose for the shortest time [med-bauer2021]; a 2025 Navigation Guide review counted 27 of 46 studies as positive for neurodevelopmental associations [med-prada2025]. Teratology experts warned against causal inference [med-alwan2022], and a study of 2.48 million Swedish children found small associations (autism HR 1.05, ADHD 1.07) that vanished in sibling comparisons (HR 0.98 for both) [med-ahlqvist2024]. Even the precautionary statement notes that alternatives for high fever and severe pain are limited, so all sides agree acetaminophen should remain available when indicated [med-bauer2021].

**Men.** Mild analgesics in pregnancy were linked to cryptorchidism in a Danish cohort but not a Finnish one, with anti-androgenic effects in rats [med-kristensen2011]. Men with the highest urinary paracetamol took longer to conceive (fecundability OR 0.67), though not when exposure was modelled continuously [med-smarr2016]. Reviews flag paracetamol as a possible concern for sperm DNA [med-drobnis2017].

## NSAIDs

**Exercise.** Maximal OTC ibuprofen (1200 mg/day) halved quadriceps growth from 8 weeks of strength training in young adults (3.7% vs 7.5%) [med-lilja2018]. In older adults, ibuprofen 1.2 g/day or acetaminophen 4 g/day for 12 weeks increased gains (muscle volume +10.9% and +12.5% vs +8.6%) [med-trappe2011]. Reviews conclude occasional use is harmless and chronic use may hurt those with the most growth potential [med-schoenfeld2012, med-lundberg2018].

**Men.** In young men, daily ibuprofen lowered the testosterone/LH ratio (compensated hypogonadism) and repressed testosterone production in testis tissue [med-kristensen2018].

**Women: ovulation.** In women with inflammatory arthritis on continuous NSAIDs around ovulation, 35.6% of cycles showed luteinized unruptured follicles versus 3.4% untreated; etoricoxib (a COX-2 inhibitor) caused most, and ibuprofen 1600 mg/day caused none [med-micu2011]. Case reports show ovulation returns after stopping [med-smith1996, med-stone2002]. NSAID use around conception was linked to miscarriage (aHR 1.89) [med-li2018]; a 2024 meta-analysis found the miscarriage association non-significant (OR 1.20) but birth defects modestly higher (OR 1.19) [med-chen2024].

## Drugs that affect sperm

| Drug | Main effect | Evidence | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paroxetine and other SSRIs | DNA fragmentation 13.8% to 30.3% in 4 weeks; normal standard semen analysis [med-tanrikut2010]; sertraline and escitalopram similar [med-akasheh2014, med-koyuncu2011] | Small, mostly uncontrolled | Appears so [med-beeder2020] |
| Finasteride 1 mg | No change in 181 healthy young men [med-overstreet1999]; counts rose 11.6-fold after stopping in subfertile men [med-samplaski2013] | RCT + clinic series | Yes |
| Finasteride 5 mg / dutasteride | Sperm count -34% / -29% at 26 weeks, lower volume and motility [med-amory2007] | RCT | Largely |
| Opioids incl. methadone | Testosterone about 165 ng/dL lower [med-bawor2015]; lower semen quality, more DNA fragmentation per review [med-drobnis2017] | Observational, low quality | Probably, poorly studied |
| Testosterone, anabolic steroids | Shut down sperm production: see [testosterone-anabolic-steroids] | Strong | Usually, slowly |
| Sulfasalazine | Gross semen abnormalities in most men; pregnancies about 2.5 months after stopping [med-toovey1981, med-omorain1984] | Consistent older studies | Yes |
| Low-dose methotrexate | No change in semen or DNA fragmentation [med-perezgarcia2023]; no excess malformations (aOR 1.01) [med-eck2017] | Small cohort + registry | Not applicable |
| Tamsulosin, silodosin | Anejaculation in 35% on tamsulosin 0.8 mg [med-hellstrom2006]; ejaculatory disorder in 95% of sexually active silodosin users [med-sakata2012] | RCT + cohort | Yes |
| Chemotherapy, radiation | Direct germ-cell damage: see [chemo-radiation] | Strong | Varies |
| Statins, PPIs | Mostly null; see sections above | Mixed | - |

Cannabis is covered in [cannabis]. A large SSRI study often cited for semen damage [med-safarinejad2008] is under a 2023 journal Expression of Concern and is not relied on here. Male depression itself was linked to lower conception in infertility trials [med-evanshoeker2018] and weakly in a preconception cohort, where medication use mediated part of the association [med-yland2022].

## Drugs and egg/ovarian function

- **NSAIDs around ovulation:** see above [med-micu2011].
- **Antidepressants while trying to conceive.** Severe depressive symptoms lowered fecundability (FR 0.62) whether or not treated, while medication itself did not appear harmful [med-nillni2016]. Another cohort found lower cycle-specific fecundability with antidepressants after adjusting for depression history (FR 0.66) [med-casillalennon2016], and maternal antidepressant use was associated with first-trimester loss in infertility trials [med-evanshoeker2018]. A review found insufficient evidence that SSRIs reduce fertility [med-sylvester2019], and ACOG's 2023 guideline supports SSRIs as first-line pharmacotherapy in pregnancy with individualized decisions [med-acog2023].
- **Hormonal contraception.** About 83% conceive within a year of stopping, with no difference by method or duration in a meta-analysis [med-girum2018]. A 17,954-woman cohort found short delays: about 5-8 cycles after injectables (FR 0.65), 4 after the patch, 3 after the pill or ring, 2 after IUDs and implants; total years of use did not matter [med-yland2020].
- **GLP-1 drugs:** see the contraception and washout points above.

## Proton-pump inhibitors and antibiotics (brief)

Two or more years of PPIs was associated with B12 deficiency (OR 1.65) [med-lam2013] and iron deficiency (OR 2.49, higher with dose and duration) [med-lam2017], and PPIs with hypomagnesemia (RR 1.43) [med-cheungpasitporn2015]. A B12 meta-analysis found the association modest and heterogeneous [med-choudhury2023], and in the COMPASS RCT (17,598 people, 3 years) pantoprazole caused no excess harms except enteric infections (OR 1.33) [med-moayyedi2019]. PPI users have less diverse gut microbiota with more oral bacteria [med-imhann2016]. For sperm, one registry linked earlier PPI use to low motile counts (OR 2.96) [med-huijgen2016] while a 248-user clinic study found nothing [med-keihani2018b].

A 4-day broad-spectrum antibiotic course disrupted gut microbiota in healthy men; it largely recovered in 6 weeks, but 9 common species were missing at 6 months [med-palleja2018]. Because gut microbes convert polyphenols, this may briefly change what people get from polyphenol-rich foods ([dietary-fiber-microbiome]); that consequence has not been measured.

This matters for fertility-preservation patients, who are often sold antioxidant blends ([chemo-radiation], [high-dose-antioxidant-cocktail]).

## Where the evidence is contested

- **Does metformin blunt exercise benefits?** Yes in several RCTs [med-walton2019, med-konopka2019, med-malin2012] and a 2026 meta-analysis showing a modest VO2peak penalty [med-etayourtasun2026]; no in others [med-boule2013, med-pilmark2021, med-terada2019]. Differences in age, disease status and outcome measured probably explain much of the split.
- **Is metformin an anti-aging drug?** The UK survival advantage [med-bannister2014] that inspired TAME [med-barzilai2016] was not reproduced in Danish twin data [med-keys2022]; critics urge tempered expectations [med-konopka2019tame].
- **Paternal metformin and birth defects:** signal in Denmark [med-wensink2022], null after better confounding control in Norway and Taiwan [med-meng2024].
- **GLP-1 drugs and muscle:** adaptive and proportionate [med-neeland2024, med-eisa2026] versus exceeding benchmarks [med-batsis2026]. **Oxidative markers:** "strong" evidence [med-bray2021] versus a thin MDA base and variable cytokines [med-kanbay2026].
- **GLP-1 drugs and fertility:** promising pilot [med-salamun2018] versus insufficient evidence in the guideline meta-analysis [med-forslund2026]; pregnancy-exposure reassurance [med-cesta2024, med-ozbek2026] versus methodological doubts [med-drummond2025].
- **CoQ10 for statin muscle symptoms:** null [med-banach2015, med-taylor2015] versus positive [med-qu2018], with nocebo explaining most symptoms [med-ctt2022].
- **Statins and exercise:** blunting in one RCT [med-mikus2013] versus best survival in fit statin users [med-kokkinos2013] and no effect on activity [med-panza2016].
- **Statins in pregnancy:** no malformation signal [med-bateman2015, med-dutta2026] but possible low birth weight and preterm birth [med-chang2021].
- **Acetaminophen in pregnancy:** precaution [med-bauer2021, med-prada2025] versus confounding [med-alwan2022, med-ahlqvist2024].
- **NSAIDs and muscle:** harm in the young [med-lilja2018], benefit in the old [med-trappe2011].
- **SSRIs and sperm:** consistent small studies [med-tanrikut2010, med-akasheh2014, med-koyuncu2011] but poor overall quality [med-sylvester2019], one key paper under an Expression of Concern [med-safarinejad2008], and depression itself as a confounder [med-yland2022].
- **SSRIs and female fertility:** no harm [med-nillni2016] versus lower fecundability [med-casillalennon2016] and miscarriage signal [med-evanshoeker2018].
- **Finasteride 1 mg:** harmless on average [med-overstreet1999] versus marked recovery after stopping in subfertile men [med-samplaski2013].
- **Methotrexate in fathers:** old stop-3-months advice versus reassuring registry, semen and guideline data [med-eck2017, med-perezgarcia2023, med-sammaritano2020].
- **PPIs:** observational nutrient deficits [med-lam2013, med-lam2017] versus RCT safety [med-moayyedi2019]; sperm harm [med-huijgen2016] versus none [med-keihani2018b].
- **Antioxidants during cancer treatment:** caution [med-bairati2005, med-lawenda2008, med-ambrosone2020] versus no demonstrated harm in small RCTs [med-block2007, med-yasueda2016].
- **Contraception and return of fertility:** no delay [med-girum2018] versus short, method-specific delays [med-yland2020].

## Practical bottom line

- **Do not change prescribed medicines on your own.** Bring this list to the prescriber if you are trying to conceive.
- **On metformin or a statin and exercising?** Keep doing both. Any blunting is partial; the drugs and exercise each have proven benefits. Long-term metformin users can ask about B12 checks.
- **On a GLP-1 drug?** Lift weights and eat enough protein. If you might conceive: on tirzepatide, use non-oral or back-up contraception for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase; plan a washout (semaglutide at least 2 months) with the prescriber.
- **Trying to conceive (women):** avoid continuous NSAIDs around mid-cycle if you can, especially COX-2 inhibitors; acetaminophen is the usual alternative. Stopping the pill or ring usually means about 3 cycles to normal fertility; injectables take longer.
- **Trying to conceive (men):** ask about sperm effects of SSRIs (a DNA fragmentation test can help), finasteride if your count is low, sulfasalazine (switching usually restores sperm in 2-3 months), opioids, and never use testosterone to treat low testosterone if you want children.
- **Pain relief:** occasional acetaminophen or ibuprofen at labelled doses is fine for most people; regular high doses are where liver, muscle, hormone and ovulation effects show up.
- **On an SGLT2 inhibitor?** Do not start a ketogenic diet or long fasts without the prescriber.
- **Supplements:** tell every prescriber what you take. No high-dose antioxidants during chemotherapy or radiotherapy without the oncology team. St John's wort can make the pill fail. Fish oil at usual doses is not a bleeding problem.

## What we don't know

- Whether metformin's exercise blunting matters for hard outcomes (falls, disability, survival) in people who need the drug, and whether timing doses away from training helps.
- Whether TAME or similar trials will show any benefit of metformin in people without diabetes.
- Long-term muscle function, bone and fertility outcomes on GLP-1 drugs, and their safety if continued into pregnancy; washout data for tirzepatide specifically.
- Whether SSRI-induced sperm DNA fragmentation lowers live-birth rates, how quickly it reverses, and whether other antidepressants differ.
- Whether paternal medicines (SSRIs, opioids, PPIs) affect offspring health.
- Whether gut-microbiome disruption by antibiotics or PPIs changes the benefits of polyphenol-rich diets.
- Which, if any, antioxidants are safe or helpful during specific cancer treatments.
