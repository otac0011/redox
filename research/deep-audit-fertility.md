# Fertility claims under audit: confounding, retractions and what changed (2020-2026)

This page re-checks 52 sperm, egg and IVF factors on Redox Compass against better-designed and newer evidence. It asks two questions of each claim. Does the finding survive designs that rule out confounding? Has a newer trial, meta-analysis or guideline changed the answer? Evidence was checked against PubMed and the HFEA website in October 2026.

## How confounding works in fertility research

Fertility research has three traps of its own, on top of the usual ones.

**1. The pregnancy itself changes behaviour (reverse causation).** A healthy early pregnancy produces strong hormone signals that cause nausea and food aversions, and coffee is one of the first things women go off. Women whose pregnancies are failing feel less sick and keep drinking coffee, so a cohort will find "coffee drinkers miscarry more" even if coffee does nothing. The large cohort meta-analysis behind the site's "7% per 100 mg/day" figure itself warns that adjustment for pregnancy symptoms and smoking may be incomplete [afert-chen2016]. Mendelian randomization (MR) uses genes that make people drink more coffee and so sidesteps the nausea problem. Two MR studies found no effect of coffee on pregnancy loss, while the same method did detect harm from smoking [afert-yuan2021, afert-britonunes2023].

**2. Who ends up in a fertility clinic (selection).** Most egg and sperm data come from couples who are already in treatment. Men with a varicocele and normal fertility are rarely tested. Women who come back to use frozen eggs are older and have a worse prognosis than those who never return. People who take supplements, follow a "fertility diet" or take folic acid are also the ones who plan carefully and look after their health (the healthy-user effect).

**3. Selection on an extreme value, then retesting (regression to the mean).** Men are offered treatment because their sperm DNA fragmentation (SDF) or white-cell count was high on one test. Retest them after any treatment, or none, and the average will fall. In the only randomised trial of antibiotics for white cells in semen (leukocytospermia), it often cleared by itself, and antibiotics did no better than no treatment [afert-yanushpolsky1995]. The site's claim that treating infection "cuts SDF by a third" comes from a study with no untreated control group [afert-gallegos2008].

## The research-integrity problem in reproductive medicine

Fertility medicine has become a test case for research integrity. Ben Mol, Esmée Bordewijk, Wentao Li and colleagues checked the published data of trials directly. Of 35 women's-health RCTs from two authors at one institute, 26 had suspicious overlaps in their baseline and outcome tables. Their baseline numbers were also unlikely to come from genuine randomisation [afert-bordewijk2020a]. A similar check of 14 trials by another Egyptian author found duplicated tables and plagiarism [afert-mol2024]. In PCOS ovulation-induction trials, those that would not share individual data were much more often at high risk of bias and gave different pooled answers [afert-bordewijk2020b, afert-li2020]. In anaesthesia, John Carlisle found that 26% of submitted trials were "zombies" (fatally flawed by false data) once individual patient data were requested [afert-carlisle2021]. INSPECT-SR applied trustworthiness checks to trials in 50 Cochrane reviews. It raised authenticity concerns for about a quarter of them, and the usual Risk-of-Bias ratings did not pick these up [afert-wilkinson2025].

The intrauterine hCG story is the clearest example of how this changes conclusions. In 2018 Cochrane rated a live-birth benefit in day-3 transfers as moderate-quality evidence [afert-craciunas2018]. The 2026 individual-patient-data (IPD) meta-analysis used only the 7 trials that shared their data and passed integrity checks (2244 women). It found no effect on live birth (OR 0.99) [afert-zou2026]. All of the apparent benefit came from trials that failed those checks.

## The most important reversals and confirmations

**Male antioxidants: three large trials, no babies (2020-2025).** The 2019-2022 Cochrane reviews found a possible live-birth gain, but only from small trials at high risk of bias [afert-deligny2022]. Since then three placebo-controlled trials have reported:
- MOXI (2020, 174 men): live birth 15% vs 24% with placebo [afert-steiner2020].
- FAZST (2020, 2370 men): folic acid plus zinc did not change live birth (34% vs 35%) and slightly raised DNA fragmentation [afert-schisterman2020].
- SUMMER (2025, 1171 men): the antioxidant did not raise ongoing pregnancy (33.8% vs 37.5%). In months 4-6, when any effect on new sperm should show, pregnancies were lower: 15.5% vs 21.5% [afert-deligny2025].

On the other side, meta-analyses still report better semen numbers and pregnancy rates [afert-agarwal2023, afert-melhem2026]. They also report a small SDF drop of about 4 points [afert-szabo2025]. None of this has turned into more births.

**Intrauterine hCG: a benefit that came from untrustworthy trials (2026).** Covered above [afert-zou2026].

**Steroids for implantation failure: no benefit, possible harm (2023).** In 715 women, prednisone did not raise live birth (37.8% vs 38.8%). It increased early pregnancy loss (17.3% vs 9.9%) and preterm delivery (11.8% vs 5.5%) [afert-sun2023].

**PGT-A: the same answer again (2025).** In couples with severe male factor, testing embryos left cumulative live birth unchanged (60.4% vs 60.9%) but cut pregnancy loss after the first transfer (5.8% vs 19.1%) [afert-lin2025]. This matches the 2021 NEJM trial [afert-yan2021] and ASRM's 2024 position [afert-asrm2024pgta].

**Caffeine and miscarriage: a likely confounded association (2021-2023).** Cohorts still show a dose-response [afert-chen2016, afert-lyngso2017], and one older case-control study found it survived adjustment for nausea [afert-giannelli2003]. MR, which is immune to the nausea bias, finds no effect [afert-yuan2021, afert-britonunes2023]. In Norwegian data that combined both methods, only smoking and BMI had consistent effects on time to conception [afert-wootton2023].

**Iodine: trials did not confirm the cohort promise.** The cohort links between mild deficiency and slower conception or lower child IQ [afert-mills2018, afert-bath2013] have not held up in trials. A trial of 832 mildly deficient pregnant women found 200 ug/day iodine did not improve child IQ [afert-gowachirapant2017]. A pooled analysis of individual data was weaker than the single UK cohort [afert-levie2019, afert-dineva2020].

**Confirmations.**
- Paternal age: sibling comparisons [afert-donofrio2014], de novo mutation counts [afert-kong2012] and donor-egg cycles, where the egg's age is fixed [afert-begon2023], all support a real, if modest, causal effect.
- Varicocele: SDF falls after repair, more with time and with higher grade, which supports a causal role [afert-szabo2025].
- Low AMH: still a poor predictor of natural fertility over long follow-up [afert-harris2023].

## Where the evidence is contested

- **Testicular sperm for high SDF.** Supporters point to about 25 points lower SDF and higher pooled live birth (OR about 2.5) [afert-esteves2017, afert-khoo2024]. Against: those data are non-randomised, and the one RCT that included a testicular arm found worse embryo development with testicular sperm [afert-hozyen2022]. The HFEA rated TESE/TESA after SDF testing red in September 2026 [afert-hfea2026sdf].
- **Embryo glue.** Cochrane 2020 rates the live-birth gain (RR 1.21) as moderate certainty [afert-heymann2020]. A 2026 meta-analysis finds RR 1.14, not significant, and 1.06 in low-bias trials [afert-rocha2026]. The HFEA rates it yellow [afert-hfea2026].
- **AMH and natural fertility.** Null results come from the NIH-funded Time to Conceive cohort and its follow-up [afert-steiner2017, afert-harris2023]. A larger cohort of buyers of a commercial hormone test finds a modest effect (HR 0.77) [afert-nelson2024].
- **PGT-A in older women and recurrent loss.** Low-quality data and a small pilot RCT favour it [afert-mumusoglu2025, afert-beebeejaun2025]. Adequately powered RCTs do not [afert-yan2021, afert-lin2025].
- **Androgens in poor responders.** DHEA is null in Cochrane. Testosterone looks positive in Cochrane and a POSEIDON-restricted meta-analysis [afert-naik2024, afert-conforti2025], but not in a network meta-analysis [afert-etrusco2025]. HFEA rates androgens grey [afert-hfea2026].
- **Weight loss before treatment.** In PCOS, a cross-trial comparison found more live births after lifestyle weight loss [afert-legro2016]. In obese infertile women generally, the LIFEstyle and FIT-PLESE RCTs found none [afert-mutsaerts2016, afert-legro2022], and a 2024 meta-analysis found more pregnancies but not more births [afert-caldwell2024].
- **Diet patterns.** Mediterranean and "profertility" patterns are each linked to IVF live birth in single cohorts, but every study has moderate-high risk of bias and none has been independently replicated [afert-gaskins2019, afert-kellow2022, afert-winter2023].
- **Sperm-count decline.** The updated meta-regression reports a 51.6% fall in mean concentration among unselected men from 1973 to 2018 [afert-levine2023]. Critics argue that sperm count varies widely and non-pathologically across populations, and that a decline narrative over-reads heterogeneous historical samples [afert-boulicault2022]. No factor on the site depends on this debate, but it frames much public discussion.

## What changed recently (by year)

- **2020:** MOXI and FAZST, both null for male supplements; first Bordewijk integrity papers.
- **2021:** Dang ICSI RCT; Yan PGT-A NEJM RCT; MR study finds coffee does not cause pregnancy loss.
- **2023:** ESHRE add-on recommendations; prednisone RCT (JAMA); ERA meta-analysis null; a second MR study on coffee.
- **2024:** egg-freezing meta-analyses (return about 11%, per-woman live birth about 28%); ASRM PGT-A opinion; Darwish integrity paper; two endometrial scratch trials retracted; Elprince DHEA trial retracted.
- **2025:** SUMMER RCT; Lin PGT-A BMJ RCT; INSPECT-SR in Cochrane reviews; Papaleo myo-inositol trial retracted.
- **2026:** intrauterine hCG IPD meta-analysis; embryo-glue meta-analysis no longer significant; Cochrane editorial note on the female antioxidant review; Kotb DHEA trial retracted; HFEA ratings, including TESE red after SDF testing.

## Practical bottom line

1. Age (yours and, to a smaller extent, your partner's) matters far more than any supplement. Egg freezing works best at 35 or younger and with many eggs. Pooled data put live birth at about half for women who froze at 35 or younger, and only about 11% of women ever use their eggs [afert-hirsch2024, afert-kirubarajan2024].
2. Male fertility supplements have now failed in three large trials. Spend the money on fixing causes (varicocele, smoking, heat, medicines) instead.
3. For IVF, a plain cycle is the evidence-based default. Newer trials have weakened or overturned add-ons, not rescued them. Be most wary of add-ons whose positive evidence comes from small single-centre trials.
4. Folic acid remains essential for its proven neural-tube benefit. Avoid real iodine and iron deficiency, but do not expect extra doses to boost fertility.
5. Caffeine under about 200 mg/day in pregnancy is still a sensible precaution. Just know the miscarriage risk figures are probably inflated by confounding.
