# Timing of supplements and nutrient status: stores, half-lives and how often to dose

Some nutrients have to be taken every day and others don't. The difference comes down to two things: how long the compound stays in the blood (its half-life), and whether the body keeps a reserve somewhere, such as red cells, fat, muscle or the liver. As a rule of thumb, blood levels take about five half-lives to reach a new steady state, and about five half-lives to wash out once you stop. A substance that leaves the blood within hours but works through a slowly changing tissue (creatine in muscle, omega-3s in cell membranes) still needs weeks to load, and its effect lasts weeks after you stop. This page sorts 49 supplement and nutrient-status factors by that logic, using measured human time courses wherever we could find them.

## The timing biology in one page

**Clocks set by half-life (hours to days).** Taurine (half-life about 1 h) [tnut-ghandforoush2010], alpha-lipoic acid (about 0.5 h) [tnut-breithaupt1999], NAC (about 6 h) [tnut-holdiness1991], methylene blue (about 5 h) [tnut-peter2000], quercetin (about 11 h) [tnut-graefe2001], astaxanthin (about 16 h) [tnut-odeberg2003], CoQ10 (about 33 h) [tnut-tomono1986, tnut-bhagavan2006] and plasma vitamin E (about 57 h) [tnut-leonard2005] all reach steady blood levels within a day to about a week. Hydrogen water is the extreme case: breath hydrogen peaks 10-15 minutes after drinking and soon returns to baseline [tnut-shimouchi2009].

**Clocks set by blood-cell or tissue turnover (weeks to months).**
- Omega-3s: in red cells (the "omega-3 index") EPA has an incorporation half-life of about 28 days and reaches steady state at about 180 days. Plasma cholesteryl esters plateau in 4-8 weeks, and fat tissue was still rising after a year [tnut-katan1997, tnut-browning2012].
- Vitamin D: circulating 25(OH)D has a half-life of about 15 days [tnut-jones2014], but vitamin D itself sits in fat with a body half-life of about 2 months [tnut-jones2008].
- Carotenoids: serum half-lives run from about 26 days (lycopene) to 76 days (lutein) [tnut-burri2001].
- Folate: plasma folate plateaus at about 3 months, and red-cell folate keeps rising for 6 months [tnut-hao2008, tnut-daly1997].
- Muscle stores: creatine fills in 6 days (loading) to 28 days (3 g/day) and empties in about 30 days [tnut-hultman1996]. Muscle carnosine falls only 2-4% a week [tnut-baguet2009]. Muscle carnitine rose only after 24 weeks [tnut-wall2011].
- Selenium: plasma selenium plateaus by about 10 weeks [tnut-hurst2010].

**Clocks set by large stores (months to years).**
- Iron: stores removed by one blood donation took more than 168 days to return in most donors who took no iron [tnut-kiss2015, tnut-mast2020].
- Vitamin E in fat: needs at least 2 years to reach a new steady state, and declines only slowly after stopping [tnut-handelman1994]. Tissues lag plasma for months [tnut-burton1998].
- B12: the body holds 1-5 mg, so deficiency can take years to show [tnut-ods2025b12].

**Clocks set by the target tissue.**
- Sperm: newly made sperm reach the ejaculate after about 64 days (42-76 days in a heavy-water labelling study) [tnut-misell2006], consistent with the classic estimate of about 64 days for spermatogenesis [tnut-heller1963].
- Eggs: the follicle that ovulates starts differentiating about 85 days earlier and is selected 15-20 days before ovulation [tnut-gougeon1986].

For conception, the slower of the two clocks sets the lead time: the time to load the nutrient, or the time to build the cell.

## Answers to the four questions

### 1. Windows: does a month before matter more than two weeks before?

Mostly yes, for anything that acts on sperm or egg as they develop. New sperm take about 64 days (42-76) to appear in the ejaculate [tnut-misell2006, tnut-heller1963], and the ovulatory follicle's cohort is committed about 85 days before ovulation [tnut-gougeon1986]. Supplements that load fast (CoQ10, vitamin C, NAC, astaxanthin, quercetin) reach steady blood levels within days to a week [tnut-martucci2019, tnut-holdiness1991, tnut-odeberg2003]. For these the lead time is set by the cell, so about 3 months before conception is the natural target. The trials match that: CoQ10 ran 60 days before IVF [tnut-xu2018] and 6 months for sperm [tnut-balercia2009], carnitine 2-6 months [tnut-lenzi2003, tnut-lenzi2004], selenium 3 months [tnut-scott1998] and lycopene 12 weeks [tnut-williams2020].

Some effects arrive later in development and can still be changed late:
- A 2-month course of vitamins C and E lowered sperm DNA fragmentation [tnut-greco2005], shorter than a full sperm cycle.
- 10 weeks of DHA lowered DNA fragmentation through seminal plasma without changing sperm membranes [tnut-martinezsoto2016].
- Two months of carnitine already improved motility in a crossover trial [tnut-lenzi2003].

These fit protection during late maturation and storage in the epididymis. None of the trials compared start times directly, so this is inference.

Where loading itself is slow, it adds to the lead time:
- Omega-3s in red cells take 4-6 months to plateau [tnut-katan1997]. Three months of DHA did not change sperm membrane DHA [tnut-conquer2000].
- 25(OH)D takes about 2-3 months to reach steady state on daily dosing (five half-lives of about 15 days) [tnut-jones2014]. A clinician-supervised loading dose shortens this [tnut-blombergjensen2018].
- For neural tube protection, what counts is red-cell folate at day 28 of pregnancy [tnut-crider2014]. Red-cell folate keeps rising for 6 months on folic acid [tnut-daly1997], so starting 3 months ahead beats 1 month. One month still raises plasma folate before the neural tube closes [tnut-hao2008].

### 2. Kinetics: fast, plateau, or slow-growing?

- **Fast (hours to days).** Hydrogen water [tnut-shimouchi2009], methylene blue [tnut-rodriguez2016], glycine for next-day fatigue [tnut-bannai2012], drug-interaction induction by St John's wort (within 2 weeks) [tnut-imai2008], and the gut effect of unneeded iron [tnut-lund1999].
- **Build and plateau (weeks).** Creatine (6-28 days) [tnut-hultman1996], CoQ10 in plasma (about 7 days) [tnut-martucci2019], selenium in plasma (about 10 weeks) [tnut-hurst2010], plasma omega-3 (4-8 weeks) [tnut-katan1997], magnesium (about 1 month) [tnut-zhang2016], GlyNAC (2-16 weeks) [tnut-sekhar2011, tnut-kumar2023], urolithin A (1-4 months) [tnut-andreux2019, tnut-liu2022, tnut-singh2022], nicotinamide's skin effect (3-12 months) [tnut-chen2015], and iron repletion (about 3 months) [tnut-vaucher2012, tnut-vonsiebenthal2023].
- **Slow (months to years).** Red-cell omega-3 index (about 6 months) and fat-tissue omega-3 (more than a year) [tnut-katan1997, tnut-browning2012], fat-tissue vitamin E (at least 2 years) [tnut-handelman1994], B-vitamin effects on brain atrophy (2 years) [tnut-smith2010], and the small cancer benefit of a multivitamin (about 11 years) [tnut-gaziano2012].

### 3. Persistence: what outlasts the exposure, and why?

- **Large stores and slow pools.**
  - B12: deficiency takes years because the body holds 1-5 mg [tnut-ods2025b12].
  - Vitamin D: one 500,000 IU dose kept 25(OH)D above placebo for 12 months [tnut-sanders2010], because stores turn over slowly [tnut-jones2008, tnut-heaney2003].
  - Iron: one donation is not made up for months without iron [tnut-kiss2015].
  - Omega-3: the red-cell index stays high for at least 4 weeks after stopping [tnut-cao2006], and its 28-day half-life means months to wash out [tnut-katan1997].
  - Vitamin E in fat declines over more than a year [tnut-handelman1994].
  - Muscle stores: carnosine takes 6-15 weeks to wash out [tnut-baguet2009] and creatine about 30 days [tnut-hultman1996].
- **Harms that outlast the pills.** The beta-carotene excess in smokers faded 4-6 years after stopping in ATBC [tnut-virtamo2003]. In CARET it was still present at 6 years, especially in women [tnut-goodman2004]. The vitamin E prostate-cancer excess in SELECT became clearer with longer follow-up [tnut-klein2011].
- **Effects that stop when the pills stop.** Nicotinamide's skin-cancer protection disappeared after stopping [tnut-chen2015]. GlyNAC benefits had faded 12 weeks after stopping [tnut-kumar2021]. Oral glutathione returned to baseline within a month [tnut-richie2015]. CYP3A induction by St John's wort cleared in about a week [tnut-imai2008]. In a radiotherapy trial, extra second cancers appeared only while vitamin E was being taken [tnut-bairati2005].

### 4. Frequency: does it need to be daily?

- **Iron: alternate days absorb better per dose.** One dose of 60 mg or more raises hepcidin for about 24 hours and cuts absorption of the next day's dose by 35-45% [tnut-moretti2015]. Alternate-day dosing absorbs more per dose [tnut-stoffel2017, tnut-stoffel2020]. At equal total doses it gave the same ferritin, fewer gut side effects and less iron deficiency at 6 months [tnut-vonsiebenthal2023]. Splitting a dose twice daily does not help [tnut-moretti2015, tnut-stoffel2017]. Clinical trials and a meta-analysis found daily and alternate-day dosing roughly equal for haemoglobin [tnut-pasupathy2023, tnut-dhanvijay2025].
- **Vitamin D: daily or weekly, not large boluses.** Daily, weekly and monthly doses gave similar 25(OH)D after 2 months [tnut-ishshalom2008]. In outcome trials, however, boluses did worse:
  - An annual 500,000 IU dose raised falls, especially in the first 3 months [tnut-sanders2010].
  - Monthly 60,000 IU raised falls compared with 24,000 IU [tnut-bischoff2016].
  - Bolus regimens gave no protection against respiratory infection [tnut-martineau2017] and no reduction in cancer mortality [tnut-keum2022].
- **Omega-3: the weekly total matters more than daily timing.** Doses mimicking fish eaten 1-4 times a week raised levels in proportion to dose [tnut-browning2012]. The red-cell index tracks dose and body weight [tnut-flock2013].
- **Short half-life compounds that act on a slow tissue.** Creatine needs only steady intake. Once muscle is full, 2 g/day maintains it [tnut-hultman1996].
- **Short half-life compounds with no store.** Taurine, NAC and alpha-lipoic acid are cleared within hours. Trials used daily (NAC: twice-daily) dosing [tnut-sun2016, tnut-paschalis2018, tnut-ziegler2006]. No trial has compared other schedules.
- **Saturable absorption.** At most about 5 mg of lycopene is absorbed from a single dose, whether the dose is 10 or 120 mg [tnut-diwadkar2003]. Vitamin C absorption falls for single doses above 500 mg [tnut-levine1996]. Spreading intake over meals beats one large dose.
- **Intermittent senolytics ("hit and run").** The idea is that senescent cells take weeks to reaccumulate, so a few days of dosing could work for weeks [tnut-kirkland2020]. Three days of dasatinib plus quercetin reduced senescent cells measured 11 days later [tnut-hickson2019]. Intermittent dosing was feasible in lung fibrosis [tnut-justice2019]. But the first randomised trial missed its primary bone endpoint at 20 weeks; a bone-formation marker rose only at 2-4 weeks [tnut-farr2024]. For fisetin, intermittent dosing extended lifespan in one mouse study [tnut-yousefzadeh2018] but not in the NIA testing program [tnut-harrison2024]. No human outcome data exist.

## Where the evidence is contested

- **Vitamin D bolus versus daily.** Supporters of daily dosing point to falls after annual and monthly boluses [tnut-sanders2010, tnut-bischoff2016], respiratory infection protection limited to daily or weekly dosing [tnut-martineau2017], cancer-mortality benefit only with daily dosing [tnut-keum2022], and the short half-life of the parent vitamin [tnut-hollis2013]. The other side has evidence too:
  - The 2021 meta-analysis found no significant interaction by dosing frequency [tnut-jolliffe2021], and the 2025 update found no overall effect and no effect modification by schedule [tnut-jolliffe2025].
  - Blood levels converge across schedules [tnut-ishshalom2008].
  - Monthly 60,000 IU gave a borderline reduction in cardiovascular events in D-Health [tnut-thompson2023], while daily 2000 IU in VITAL was null [tnut-manson2019].
  - D-Health's monthly regimen did not reduce mortality and showed a late cancer-mortality signal [tnut-neale2022].
- **Iron: alternate-day versus daily.** Isotope and hepcidin studies favour alternate days [tnut-moretti2015, tnut-stoffel2017, tnut-stoffel2020]. Clinical trials and a meta-analysis show similar haemoglobin and ferritin, with a non-significant edge to daily for haemoglobin [tnut-pasupathy2023, tnut-dhanvijay2025, tnut-vonsiebenthal2023].
- **How long beta-carotene harm persists.** It faded within 4-6 years in ATBC [tnut-virtamo2003] but persisted beyond 6 years in CARET [tnut-goodman2004].
- **Lycopene half-life.** Short dosing studies give 5-9 days [tnut-cohn2004], long depletion studies about 26 days [tnut-burri2001]. Both may be right, describing a fast blood pool and a slow tissue reservoir [tnut-diwadkar2003].
- **How fast GlyNAC works.** One group reports glutathione restored in 2 weeks [tnut-sekhar2011] with broad benefits by 16-24 weeks [tnut-kumar2023, tnut-kumar2021]. A 114-person industry trial found no rise at 2 weeks except in people who started with high oxidative stress and low glutathione [tnut-lizzo2022].
- **Oral glutathione.** It raised stores over 1-6 months in one trial [tnut-richie2015] but did nothing in 4 weeks in another [tnut-allen2011]. Trial length may explain the gap.
- **Omega-3 and sperm.** Three months of DHA did not reach sperm membranes [tnut-conquer2000], yet 10 weeks lowered DNA fragmentation [tnut-martinezsoto2016]. Whether sperm benefit needs membrane loading at all is unresolved.
- **Senolytic schedules.** Pilot data and reviews support hit-and-run dosing [tnut-hickson2019, tnut-kirkland2020]. The randomised bone trial was null on its primary endpoint [tnut-farr2024], and fisetin failed in the NIA program [tnut-harrison2024].
- **Antioxidants and cancer treatment.** Harm was confined to the supplementation period in a randomised trial [tnut-bairati2005]. Observational chemotherapy data could not separate use before treatment from use during it [tnut-ambrosone2020].

## Practical bottom line

- **Start first, about 3 months before trying to conceive:**
  - Correct vitamin D deficiency. Steady state takes about 2-3 months, or less with a supervised loading dose [tnut-jones2014, tnut-chu2018].
  - Raise omega-3 intake. The red-cell index needs months [tnut-katan1997, tnut-chiu2018].
  - Take folic acid. Red-cell folate builds for months [tnut-daly1997, tnut-crider2014].
  - Start any sperm or egg supplement you choose (CoQ10, carnitine, selenium if your status is low), to cover a full sperm cycle or follicle cohort [tnut-misell2006, tnut-gougeon1986, tnut-xu2018].
- **Can wait or works late.** Fast-loading compounds reach blood steady state within days to a week. Starting later mainly shortens how many developing cells are exposed. Some sperm DNA effects appeared after 2 months [tnut-greco2005].
- **Should be daily (or near daily):**
  - Short half-life compounds you have chosen to use: NAC, taurine, alpha-lipoic acid, nicotinamide [tnut-holdiness1991, tnut-ghandforoush2010, tnut-chen2015].
  - Vitamin C from food [tnut-levine1996].
  - Creatine until muscle is full, then a small daily dose [tnut-hultman1996].
- **Need not be daily:**
  - Iron for deficiency works on alternate days with fewer side effects [tnut-vonsiebenthal2023].
  - Oily fish 1-4 times a week is enough [tnut-browning2012].
  - Vitamin D can be weekly, but avoid big monthly or annual boluses [tnut-ishshalom2008, tnut-sanders2010].
  - Carotenoid-rich vegetables only need to be eaten most days [tnut-burri2001].
- **Stop ahead:**
  - High-dose antioxidant mixtures that loosen sperm chromatin: about one sperm cycle before trying [tnut-menezo2007, tnut-misell2006].
  - St John's wort: about a week before relying on drugs it interacts with [tnut-imai2008].
  - High-dose antioxidants during cancer treatment, unless the oncology team agrees [tnut-bairati2005].
- **After a blood donation:** a small daily iron dose shortens recovery from about 5-6 months to about 2.5 months [tnut-kiss2015].

## What we don't know

- No trial has compared starting a fertility supplement 1, 2 or 3 months before conception. Lead times are extrapolated from development times [tnut-misell2006, tnut-gougeon1986].
- Human tissue loading and wash-out are largely unmeasured for CoQ10, NAC, carnitine, urolithin A, NR/NMN, MitoQ, astaxanthin, ergothioneine and taurine. Most trials report only the end of treatment.
- For vitamin C and ergothioneine, the abstracts we could verify do not give precise build-up or wash-out times. We extrapolated, or left the field as not measured.
- No trial has shown that intermittent senolytic dosing improves a clinical outcome in humans [tnut-farr2024].
- Whether bolus vitamin D harms people who are already replete, or only fails to help, is not settled [tnut-neale2022, tnut-jolliffe2025].
- Skipped as having no meaningful time dimension (genotypes): G6PD deficiency and GSTM1/GSTT1 genotype.
