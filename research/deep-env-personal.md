# Personal care and consumer products: parabens, triclosan, UV filters, fragrance, hair, nails, menstrual products and PFAS

Shampoo, lotion, sunscreen, deodorant, perfume, makeup, hair treatments, nail polish, tampons and dental floss all put chemicals on or into the body every day. Some of these chemicals are measurable in almost everyone's urine. The useful questions are narrower than the headlines: which products dominate exposure, does that exposure do measurable harm at everyday levels, and does changing products change anything that matters?

The short answers: product swaps lower urine levels of short-lived chemicals within days; for most of these chemicals the human evidence of harm at everyday exposure is weak, inconsistent and heavily confounded; the clearest real-world harms are contact allergy, acute injuries from salon hair treatments, and probably some cancer risk from frequent use of chemical hair straighteners. Phthalates and bisphenols are covered in [plastics-chemicals] and the environment deep dive; here they appear only where personal-care products are the source.

## The core science

**How exposure happens.** Leave-on products applied to large areas (body lotion, sunscreen, body spray, foundation) deliver far more than rinse-off products. In 400 men, using sunscreen or hand/body lotion within 6 hours before a urine sample raised urinary parabens by 66-156% and 79-147%, and cologne raised the diethyl-phthalate metabolite MEP by 83% [epc-nassan2017]. In a controlled-use study, personal-care products supplied over 99% of women's paraben exposure [epc-huang2021].

**Short half-lives.** Parabens (dermal half-life about 9-12 h [epc-shin2023]), triclosan (about 21 h [epc-sandborgh2006]) and diethyl phthalate leave the body within a day or two. That cuts both ways: exposure falls quickly when products change, but a single urine sample may misclassify long-term exposure. For parabens and benzophenone-3 the problem is smaller than for BPA, because people keep the same products: intraclass correlations were about 0.4-0.5 for parabens and 0.62 for BP-3, against 0.24 for BPA [epc-smith2012, epc-meeker2013, epc-dewalque2015]. A small controlled study found much poorer reliability [epc-huang2021].

**Oxidative stress.** The human evidence is thin. In pregnant women, butyl- and propylparaben and BP-3 tracked 17-27% higher urinary isoprostane per interquartile increase [epc-watkins2015]. A larger repeated-measures study confirmed associations of parabens, BP-3 and triclosan with 8-OHdG and isoprostane, but none survived once correlated phthalates were modelled [epc-ferguson2019], and phthalates themselves track these markers [epc-ferguson2015]. In vitro, parabens raise mitochondrial ROS and oxidative DNA adducts in sperm [epc-samarasinghe2018], and UV-nail-lamp light causes ROS-signature mutations in cells [epc-zhivagui2023]. In short: plausible mechanisms, weak human data, and chemicals that travel together.

**Confounding.** Product use is patterned by sex, ethnicity, income and lifestyle. Methylparaben is higher in women and non-Hispanic Black Americans [epc-calafat2010]; triclosan and BP-3 are higher in higher-income groups [epc-han2016]; BP-3 is a marker of time outdoors [epc-minguez2019b]. Products used by Black women often contain unlabelled parabens, diethyl phthalate and other endocrine disruptors [epc-helm2018]. Every outcome study in this field has to separate the chemical from the person using it, and most cannot fully.

## 1. Parabens ([parabens])

Parabens are cheap, effective preservatives. Methylparaben was in 99.1% and propylparaben in 92.7% of US urine samples in NHANES 2005-2006 (medians 63.5 and 8.7 ug/L) [epc-calafat2010]. The EU banned five parabens in 2014 and capped propyl- and butylparaben at 0.14% and methyl- and ethylparaben at 0.4% each [epc-eu2014a, epc-eu2014b]; the US sets no specific limits. German 24-hour urine data put about 5% of people above acceptable intake for a single paraben, but under deliberately conservative assumptions [epc-moos2017]. Whole-body application of butylparaben cream produced serum peaks within hours with no short-term change in reproductive or thyroid hormones in young men [epc-janjua2007].

**Breast cancer.** Parabens were measured in 20 breast tumours (mean 20.6 ng/g) [epc-darbre2004]. There was no comparison with normal tissue, and the accompanying editorial and a published letter stressed that presence does not show cause [epc-harvey2004, epc-jeffrey2004]. A 2023 study reported that stopping paraben and phthalate products "reversed" cancer-associated gene expression in breast aspirates [epc-dairkee2023], but it was uncontrolled and used surrogate markers.

**Substitution.** Paraben-free products need another preservative. Methylisothiazolinone, widely used in cosmetics in the 2010s, caused a contact-allergy epidemic (2.0% to 3.7% of patch-tested patients in two years) [epc-lundov2013] that halved after EU restrictions [epc-uter2020].

### Parabens and sperm ([parabens-sperm])

| Study | Men | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Meeker 2011, Boston clinic | 190 | No link with semen quality; butylparaben with sperm DNA damage [epc-meeker2011] |
| Nishihama 2017, Tokyo | 42 | No associations [epc-nishihama2017] |
| Jurewicz 2017, Poland | 315 | Lower motility, more abnormal forms and DNA stainability [epc-jurewicz2017] |
| Shen 2023, Shanghai | 795 | Paraben mixture with lower count and motility [epc-shen2023] |
| Li 2026, Wuhan (2 urines) | clinic men | Butylparaben with comet-assay DNA damage [epc-li2026] |
| Kiwitt-Cardenas 2024, Spain | young men | Parabens unrelated to DNA fragmentation [epc-kiwitt2024] |
| Dodge 2015, EARTH couples | 218 couples | Paternal parabens unrelated to IVF fertilization or live birth [epc-dodge2015] |

There is no meta-analysis, positive findings cluster in mixture models and in butylparaben (the least common paraben), and no study has measured sperm after reducing exposure.

### Parabens and female fertility ([parabens-egg])

In the EARTH IVF cohort (245 women, 356 cycles), urinary parabens were unrelated to oocyte counts, embryo quality, fertilization, implantation, pregnancy or live birth [epc-minguez2016]; mixture analyses in 420 women were null overall [epc-minguez2019], and follicular-fluid parabens did not predict pregnancy [epc-dimitriadis2025]. In the LIFE preconception cohort, women's top-quartile methylparaben went with 34% lower fecundability, but continuous models were null [epc-smarr2017]. A cohort using pooled daily urines found higher butylparaben with *higher* fecundability [epc-rosenvollmar2023]. Only the third quartile of propylparaben tracked lower antral follicle count in 511 Polish women [epc-jurewicz2020]. A systematic review called the female paraben-fecundability link preliminary [epc-hipwell2019].

## 2. Triclosan and triclocarban ([triclosan-triclocarban])

Triclosan-containing consumer soaps were no more effective than plain soap at preventing illness or reducing hand bacteria [epc-aiello2007]. More than 200 scientists called triclosan and triclocarban persistent endocrine disruptors without benefit in most uses [epc-halden2017], and in 2016 the FDA ruled 19 consumer-wash antiseptics, including both, not generally recognized as safe and effective, effective September 2017 [epc-fda2016]. Arizona wastewater loads of triclosan then fell 89% and triclocarban 80%, while parabens rose 72% [epc-adhikari2022]; benzalkonium chloride and chloroxylenol, which the FDA deferred, became common replacements [epc-rundle2019]. The EU restricts triclosan to a few product types at up to 0.3% and tightened rules again in 2024 [epc-eu2014a, epc-eu2024].

The counterweight is toothpaste: a Cochrane review of 30 trials found triclosan/copolymer fluoride toothpaste reduced plaque, gingivitis and caries slightly, with no serious safety concerns over up to 3 years [epc-riley2013]. Toothpaste use raised plasma triclosan dramatically but did not change thyroid hormones in 14 days [epc-allmyr2009], and a systematic review could not conclude that current exposure affects thyroid function [epc-homburg2022]. A birth-weight meta-analysis found no clear overall effect, with stronger inverse links only where exposure was higher [epc-patti2021, epc-zhong2020].

**Sperm ([triclosan-sperm]).** A 2024 meta-analysis of 5 small case-control and cross-sectional studies linked triclosan to lower concentration (SMD -0.42) and motility (SMD -1.30), with high heterogeneity [epc-adegbola2024]. Individual studies range from modest motility links in 471 men [epc-zhu2016] to "limited (none or modest)" effects in 406 men [epc-yuan2022] and about 1 percentage point fewer normal forms without dose-response in 262 Boston men [epc-nassan2019]. In 443 Shanghai couples, higher male triclosan went with 23% lower fecundability [epc-zhu2022].

**Ovarian reserve ([triclosan-egg]).** Higher triclosan tracked fewer antral follicles in Boston (-4%) [epc-minguez2017] and Poland [epc-jurewicz2019], and the top quartile went with 16% lower fecundability in a Canadian pregnancy cohort that measured triclosan once, after conception [epc-velez2015]. A systematic review found little to no link with time to pregnancy [epc-hipwell2019], and a pooled-urine cohort found the opposite direction [epc-rosenvollmar2023].

## 3. UV filters, and how they compare with the sunscreen benefit ([uv-filters])

Daily sunscreen is one of the best-proven protective habits on this site ([sunscreen]). The question here is narrower: are the organic filters absorbed, and does that matter?

| Study | Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Matta 2019 (FDA) | RCT, 24 adults, 4 applications/day x 4 days | All 4 filters above 0.5 ng/mL on day 1; oxybenzone 169-210 ng/mL [epc-matta2019] |
| Matta 2020 (FDA) | RCT, 48 adults, single then maximal use | All 6 filters above 0.5 ng/mL after one application; oxybenzone 258 ng/mL, homosalate up to 23 ng/mL [epc-matta2020] |
| Janjua 2004/2008 | 32 adults, 10% filters whole-body | BP-3 up to 300 ng/mL; in plasma within 1-2 h; no exposure-related hormone change [epc-janjua2004, epc-janjua2008] |
| Gonzalez 2006 | 25 adults, 5 days | BP-3 still excreted 5 days after the last application [epc-gonzalez2006] |
| Mohammed 2019 | Volunteers, zinc oxide nanoparticles | No penetration into living skin [epc-mohammed2019] |

FDA's 0.5 ng/mL is a trigger for further toxicology testing, not a harm level. In its 2019 proposed rule, FDA proposed only zinc oxide and titanium dioxide as safe and effective and asked for more data on 12 organic filters including oxybenzone [epc-fda2019]. The EU capped oxybenzone at 6% (face, hands, lips) and 2.2% (body) in 2022 [epc-eu2022] and banned 4-MBC from 2025 [epc-eu2024]. A comprehensive review found BP-3 and its metabolite BP-1 estrogenic at human-relevant levels and US urinary BP-3 10-20 times European and Asian levels, endorsed the EU limits, and still advised against giving up sunscreen [epc-mustieles2023]. Octocrylene can generate benzophenone in stored products [epc-downs2021], a finding challenged in a letter [epc-surber2021].

**Fertility.** Thirteen of 29 filters triggered calcium signals in human sperm in vitro, several by activating CatSper like progesterone [epc-rehfeld2016]. In the LIFE study, men's BP-2 and 4-hydroxybenzophenone went with lower couple fecundability (FOR 0.69 and 0.74) and BP-2 with a few of 35 semen endpoints, but BP-3, the main sunscreen filter, was not linked to semen [epc-buck2014, epc-buck2015]. Spanish young men showed only small FSH shifts and no semen associations [epc-adoamnei2018], and Danish associations appeared only in men with skin-barrier mutations [epc-joensen2018]. In women undergoing IVF, BP-3 went with *better* pregnancy outcomes among outdoor workers, which the authors attribute partly to lifestyle confounding [epc-minguez2019b]. See [uv-filters-sperm].

**Weighing it.** On one side: a randomized trial showing fewer skin cancers and less photoageing with daily sunscreen ([sunscreen]). On the other: proven absorption, endocrine activity in cells and animals, and inconsistent human associations. The trade-off favours sunscreen; the choice of filter is where precaution is cheap (mineral products, clothing, shade).

## 4. Fragrance and synthetic musks ([fragrance-musks])

Women who used perfume in the previous 24 h had 2.92 times higher urinary MEP, the metabolite of the fragrance fixative diethyl phthalate [epc-parlett2013]. Polycyclic musks such as galaxolide and tonalide are found in the blood of users (up to 4,100 ng/L) and predicted by lotion and perfume use [epc-hutter2005, epc-hutter2009]; older women carry more [epc-hutter2010], and musks appear in breast milk at lower intakes than legacy pollutants [epc-reiner2007]. Musks are very weakly estrogenic but anti-androgenic and anti-progestagenic in cell assays [epc-vanderburg2008]; reviews range from "no evidence for substantial human risk" [epc-kafferlein1998] to calls for epidemiology [epc-taylor2014].

The proven harm is allergy: about 1.9% (conservatively 0.8%) of European adults have clinically relevant fragrance contact allergy [epc-diepgen2015]. From 2026-2028 the EU requires about 80 fragrance allergens to be named on labels [epc-eu2023]; the US MoCRA law requires an FDA allergen-labelling rule that has not yet been issued [epc-fdamocra]. In a US online survey, 34.7% of adults reported symptoms from fragranced products [epc-steinemann2016] (self-reported). Indoors, terpene fragrances react with ozone to form formaldehyde and particles for hours after use [epc-carslaw2022]. Many fragrance chemicals are not on labels [epc-dodson2012].

## 5. Hair dyes, relaxers and straighteners ([hair-dyes-relaxers])

| Outcome | Study | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Uterine cancer | Sister Study, 33,947 women | Straighteners HR 1.80; >4 uses/year HR 2.55; dyes not associated [epc-chang2022] |
| Uterine cancer | Black Women's Health Study, 44,798 | Heavy use HR 1.18 (CI 0.81-1.71) overall; 1.64-1.71 in postmenopausal heavy/long users [epc-bertrand2023] |
| Breast cancer | Sister Study, 46,709 | Permanent dye HR 1.45 in Black women, 1.07 in White women; straighteners 1.18 [epc-eberle2020] |
| All cancers | Nurses' Health Study, 117,200 | Permanent dye: solid cancers HR 0.98; small rises for a few subtypes [epc-zhang2020] |
| Bladder cancer | Meta-analysis | Personal dye use RR 0.93 [epc-turati2014] |
| Other cancers | Sister Study, 46,287 | Thyroid HR 1.71, pancreatic 2.66, little dose-response [epc-bailey2026] |
| Fibroids | BWHS, 23,580 | Relaxers IRR 1.17, with frequency and burn trends [epc-wise2012] |
| Fecundability | PRESTO, 11,274 | Former users FR 0.89; 10+ years 0.71 [epc-wise2023] |
| Hairdressers | Sister Study, 50,800 | Endometriosis HR 1.61; no excess breast or uterine cancer [epc-ogunsina2026] |

The signal is strongest for frequent straightener use and uterine cancer, but it rests on one cohort and was not replicated overall in Black women, who use relaxers most. An independent systematic review called the evidence insufficient [epc-farooq2024]; a review authored and funded by a private risk-consulting firm concluded relaxers are not a cancer risk factor [epc-hernandez2025]. Many cancer sites were tested [epc-bailey2026], fibroid results may be biased by misclassification [epc-ogunsina2025], and absolute uterine cancer risk is low.

Smoothing treatments are a separate, acute issue. Keratin treatments released airborne formaldehyde up to 1.19 ppm, including one product labelled formaldehyde-free [epc-pierce2011]. The "formaldehyde-free" alternative glyoxylic acid is absorbed through the scalp and converted to oxalate, causing kidney injury [epc-robert2024]; 36 published cases had a median onset of 2 days and six needed dialysis [epc-aamir2025]. FDA announced a plan to ban formaldehyde in these products in 2023; as of August 2026 the proposed rule was still under White House review and no ban is in effect [epc-fda2026hair].

## 6. Nail products and salon solvents ([nail-products-solvents])

Painting nails with a polish containing 0.97% triphenyl phosphate raised its urinary metabolite about 7-fold 10-14 h later; gloves blocked most uptake [epc-mendelsohn2016]. Nail technicians absorb it across their shift [epc-estill2021] and breathe formaldehyde, toluene and benzene; one of six Colorado salons exceeded the NIOSH formaldehyde limit [epc-lamplugh2019]. UV gel-lamp light caused ROS-type DNA damage and mutations in cultured mouse and human cells [epc-zhivagui2023]; there are no human outcome data. For fertility, high occupational solvent exposure tracked low motile sperm counts in Canadian clinic men (OR 3.83 and 2.90 in two series) [epc-cherry2001], while a systematic review judged solvent effects on time to pregnancy equivocal [epc-snijder2012]. Reviewers call for better data on cosmetologists [epc-pak2013].

## 7. Menstrual and intimate-care products ([menstrual-products])

The 2024 study by Shearston and colleagues measured 16 metals in 30 tampons from 14 brands. All were detectable, with geometric means of 120 ng/g lead, 6.74 ng/g cadmium and 2.56 ng/g arsenic; organic tampons had less lead but more arsenic [epc-shearston2024]. **What it shows:** tampons contain trace metals. **What it does not show:** whether any metal leaves the tampon, crosses the vaginal wall or raises blood levels; the authors said so and called for that research [epc-shearston2024, epc-shearston2024b].

Since then: an FDA laboratory study of 11 US products found that releases under exaggerated extraction conditions gave margins of safety indicating negligible concern [epc-jin2026, epc-fdatampon]; a model by scientists from a major tampon manufacturer estimated under 1 ng of lead per tampon available to tissue [epc-ellison2026] (industry-authored); and the only biomarker study found non-significantly higher blood mercury and urinary isoprostane in tampon users [epc-singh2019]. Of 55 chemicals extractable from menstrual and intimate products, only 19 leached under realistic simulated use [epc-marcelis2025]. Dioxins in tampons give exposures thousands of times below diet [epc-devito2002]. Reviews list phthalates, VOCs, parabens and fragrance in menstrual products but no per-cycle exposure data [epc-marroquin2024, epc-upson2022].

Douching is the clearer target: frequent douching went with 81% higher blood 1,4-dichlorobenzene [epc-ding2020]. Menstrual cups were safe in a 43-study meta-analysis, with rare toxic shock and IUD dislodgement [epc-vaneijk2019].

## 8. PFAS in cosmetics and dental floss ([pfas-personal-care])

Under MoCRA, FDA found 51 PFAS in 1,744 cosmetic formulations; of the 25 most used, 5 looked low-concern, 1 had a potential safety concern, and for most safety could not be established [epc-fda2025pfas]. Product testing found polyfluoroalkyl phosphate esters, which can break down to persistent acids, in 99.7% of 38 products [epc-harris2022]. Short-chain PFAS cross skin models; long chains lodge in the skin [epc-ragnarsdottir2024]. One modelling study suggests cosmetics may rival diet for short-chain PFCAs [epc-wang2025]; a European study found cosmetics a smaller emission source than textiles [epc-putz2022]. Flossing with PTFE-based Oral-B Glide was associated with higher serum PFHxS in 178 women, in a study whose interviews came years after blood draw [epc-boronow2019]. Because long-chain PFAS have serum half-lives of 2.7-5.3 years [epc-li2018], removing a cosmetic source lowers levels slowly. For most people, water and food matter more; see [pfas] and [water-filtration].

## 9. Does "clean beauty" help? ([clean-beauty-labels])

| Study | Who | Intervention | Change in urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| HERMOSA 2016 | 100 Latina teenage girls | 3 days of labelled-free products | MEP -27%, methylparaben -44%, propylparaben -45%, triclosan -36%, BP-3 -36%; ethyl/butylparaben rose [epc-harley2016] |
| Jovanovic 2026 | 103 French women 18-30 | 5 days without usual products | BPA -39%, methylparaben -30%, MEP -22% [epc-jovanovic2026] |
| Kim 2021 (RCT) | 51 mothers | Web-based behavioural programme, 1 month | MEHP, MEOHP, BPA, methyl- and propylparaben down 4-28% vs control [epc-kim2021] |
| Kang 2025 | 74 Korean teenage girls | 2-day cosmetics restriction | No overall change; BPA -33% and benzophenones -12 to -23% in baseline users [epc-kang2025] |

Targeted label reading works for the named chemicals. "Clean" and "natural" do not: 93.8% of 1,470 "clean" products contained at least one potential contact allergen, mostly fragrance and botanicals [epc-tran2022]; 8.3% of dermatitis patients tested reacted to an essential oil [epc-geier2022]; dermatologists warn that natural does not mean safe [epc-rubin2019]. The French authors concluded that regulation would be more efficient and fairer than individual effort [epc-jovanovic2026]. MoCRA now requires safety substantiation and adverse-event reporting but does not define "clean" [epc-fdamocra, epc-filley2023]. No study has measured a health outcome after switching.

## 10. Audit: lubricants and sperm ([lubricants-sperm])

The site says many lubricants harm sperm in vitro but lubricant use did not lower fecundability in two cohorts. That still holds. The cohorts found no reduction for any lubricant type (water-based FR 1.02, "fertility-friendly" 1.01, oil 1.23, silicone 1.27) [epc-mcinerney2018, epc-steiner2012]. No new cohort or trial has appeared since 2018. Two 2024 systematic reviews confirm lab harm driven by osmolality and pH, but only 2 of 24 studies were in vivo [epc-gumerova2024, epc-molin2024], echoing the earlier conclusion that a randomized trial is needed [epc-mesen2014]. Newer lab studies blur the "sperm-friendly" label: all commercial gels including Pre-Seed reduced forward progression and only egg white did not [epc-markram2022]; "sperm-safe" gels still cut motility, though less than pleasure gels [epc-tomlinson2023]; Pre-Seed recovered to control levels only after an initial drop [epc-pradit2025]; the clinic gel Aquagel cut progressive motility to 49% of control [epc-mackenzie2019]. Older data showing Pre-Seed did not harm motility, ROS or DNA in collected samples stand [epc-agarwal2008, epc-agarwal2013]. A new angle: painful intercourse and poor lubrication themselves predicted longer time to pregnancy [epc-bond2025], so discouraging lubricant is not risk-free.

## Where the evidence is contested

1. **Parabens and breast cancer.** Tumour measurements [epc-darbre2004] versus critiques that presence is not causation [epc-harvey2004, epc-jeffrey2004]; a gene-expression "reversal" study [epc-dairkee2023] is uncontrolled.
2. **Parabens and oxidative stress.** Positive pregnancy associations [epc-watkins2015] versus attribution to phthalate co-exposure [epc-ferguson2019].
3. **Parabens and sperm.** Null [epc-meeker2011, epc-nishihama2017, epc-kiwitt2024] versus positive [epc-jurewicz2017, epc-shen2023, epc-li2026].
4. **Parabens and female fertility.** IVF null [epc-minguez2016, epc-minguez2019] versus a quartile-only TTP signal [epc-smarr2017] and an opposite finding [epc-rosenvollmar2023].
5. **How reliable is one urine sample?** Moderate for parabens and BP-3 [epc-smith2012, epc-dewalque2015] versus poor in a controlled-use study [epc-huang2021].
6. **Triclosan: hazard or useful?** No benefit in soap and an FDA ban [epc-aiello2007, epc-halden2017, epc-fda2016] versus modest gum benefits from toothpaste [epc-riley2013]; substitutes are under-studied [epc-rundle2019].
7. **Triclosan and fertility.** Meta-analytic and cohort harm [epc-adegbola2024, epc-zhu2022, epc-minguez2017, epc-velez2015] versus weak or opposite results [epc-yuan2022, epc-nassan2019, epc-hipwell2019, epc-rosenvollmar2023].
8. **UV filters.** Absorption and endocrine activity [epc-matta2020, epc-mustieles2023] versus no human harm shown, confounding by sun exposure [epc-minguez2019b] and a strong sunscreen benefit ([sunscreen]); octocrylene-benzophenone claims disputed [epc-downs2021, epc-surber2021].
9. **Hair straighteners and cancer.** Sister Study positive [epc-chang2022, epc-eberle2020] versus null overall in Black women [epc-bertrand2023], an insufficient-evidence review [epc-farooq2024] and an industry-consultancy review finding no risk [epc-hernandez2025].
10. **Tampon metals.** Content and "no safe lead level" [epc-shearston2024, epc-shearston2024b] versus FDA and industry release studies finding negligible exposure [epc-jin2026, epc-ellison2026].
11. **PFAS from cosmetics.** Possibly rivals diet for some PFAS [epc-wang2025] versus a minor source compared with textiles [epc-putz2022].
12. **Clean beauty.** Targeted swaps work [epc-harley2016, epc-kim2021] versus "natural" products full of allergens [epc-tran2022, epc-rubin2019] and failure in low users [epc-kang2025].
13. **Fragrance.** Broad self-reported effects [epc-steinemann2016] versus low general-population allergy prevalence [epc-diepgen2015] and weak musk toxicity [epc-kafferlein1998, epc-vanderburg2008].

## Practical bottom line

- **Keep using sunscreen.** If you want to reduce absorbed filters, choose mineral (zinc oxide or titanium dioxide) products and rely more on clothing and shade [epc-fda2019, epc-mohammed2019]. Do not trade sun protection for chemical caution.
- **Focus on leave-on products.** Body lotion, sunscreen, body spray, perfume and foundation drive exposure [epc-nassan2017]. Choose paraben-free and fragrance-free versions; read ingredient lists rather than trusting "clean" or "natural" [epc-harley2016, epc-tran2022].
- **Use plain soap.** Antibacterial consumer soaps add nothing [epc-aiello2007]. Triclosan toothpaste is a reasonable choice for gum health [epc-riley2013] if you are not trying to minimise triclosan.
- **Hair.** Use chemical straighteners and relaxers less often, avoid scalp burns, and avoid keratin/"Brazilian" smoothing treatments and glyoxylic-acid products [epc-chang2022, epc-pierce2011, epc-aamir2025]. Permanent dye is not clearly linked to cancer [epc-zhang2020].
- **Nails.** Ventilate, protect hands under UV gel lamps, and if you work in a salon, insist on local exhaust and gloves [epc-mendelsohn2016, epc-lamplugh2019].
- **Menstrual products.** Use the lowest absorbency you need and skip douches and feminine sprays [epc-ding2020]; there is no evidence that switching tampon brands changes body metal levels [epc-jin2026].
- **PFAS.** Switch to non-PTFE floss and avoid "PTFE" or "perfluoro-" cosmetics, but fix water and food sources first ([pfas]) [epc-boronow2019].
- **Trying to conceive.** These swaps lower urine levels within days and cost little; start about 3 months ahead if you want them to cover a full sperm or follicle cycle [epc-heller1963, epc-gougeon1986]. Expect no proven fertility benefit; lifestyle factors with stronger evidence ([tobacco-smoking], [excess-adiposity], [alcohol]) matter more.
- **Lubricants.** Use one if sex is uncomfortable; it does not appear to lower natural fertility [epc-mcinerney2018, epc-bond2025]. For semen samples for testing, IUI or IVF, use none or a clinic-approved isotonic product.

## What we don't know

- Whether lowering paraben, triclosan, UV-filter or fragrance exposure improves any health or fertility outcome; no intervention study has measured one.
- How much of the tampon-metal content, cosmetic PFAS or fragrance musk reaches the blood over years of real use.
- Whether the Sister Study uterine-cancer link with straighteners is causal, and which chemicals would be responsible.
- The safety of replacement preservatives, antibacterials and UV filters.
- Whether UV nail lamps raise skin-cancer risk in people.
- Long-term toxicokinetics of synthetic musks in humans.
- Whether lubricants matter for couples with poor semen quality; no randomized trial exists.
