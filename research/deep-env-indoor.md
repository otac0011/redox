# Indoor air and the home: flame retardants, gas stoves, VOCs, cleaning products, dust, solvents, mould, radon and ventilation

Most people spend most of their day indoors, and indoor air is not simply outdoor air with a roof on it. Homes add their own pollutants: gases from cooking burners, chemicals that migrate out of furniture and settle in dust, vapours from paint, fragrances and cleaners, spores from damp walls, and radon seeping up from the ground. This page covers nine of these, audits five factors the site already has ([indoor-combustion-smoke], [hepa-air-purifier], [outdoor-air-pollution], [secondhand-smoke], [mobile-phones]), and keeps asking the same three questions: how big is the exposure, does it matter for health (including sperm and eggs), and does doing something about it actually help?

The short answer: the best-established indoor hazards are **radon** (lung cancer, especially in smokers), **damp and mould** (asthma and respiratory symptoms) and **cleaning sprays** (adult asthma and lung-function decline). **Gas stoves** clearly add NO2 and benzene to indoor air; how much childhood asthma that causes is genuinely disputed. **Flame retardants** and **VOCs** have strong exposure data and weak, inconsistent health data in adults. Fertility links exist for **occupational solvents** and, in one IVF cohort, organophosphate **flame retardants**, but none is firmly established.

## The core science

Indoor pollutants reach oxidative-stress pathways in three main ways:

1. **Oxidant gases.** NO2 from gas burners is itself a free radical that oxidizes the antioxidants and lipids lining the airways. Ozone that drifts indoors reacts with terpenes from fragrances and cleaners to make formaldehyde and fine particles [eia-angulomilhem2020].
2. **Reactive chemicals.** Formaldehyde, acrolein and other aldehydes bind DNA and proteins and use up glutathione. In 128 adults sampled repeatedly, 16 urinary VOC metabolites tracked urinary 8-OHdG and 8-OHG, markers of oxidized DNA and RNA [eia-qian2021].
3. **Radiation.** Radon decay products emit alpha particles in the airways, causing DNA breaks and ROS bursts, which may explain part of the synergy with tobacco [eia-sethi2012].

A recurring theme: oxidative-marker associations are usually found in small, repeated-measures panels and not in large representative samples. Serum PBDEs showed no link to oxidative markers in NHANES [eia-yuan2017], and low indoor NO2 showed none with urinary 8-OHdG or MDA in 144 COPD patients [eia-mchugh2023]. Where indoor exposures have firm health effects, those effects run mostly through airway inflammation and, for radon, DNA damage, not through a measurable systemic "oxidative load".

## 1. Flame retardants: PBDEs and their organophosphate replacements

**Sources.** For decades, US furniture foam met California's open-flame standard (TB117) by adding flame retardants, first PBDEs and then, after the commercial penta- and octa-BDE mixtures were phased out in 2004, organophosphate esters (OPEs or OPFRs) such as TDCIPP, TPHP and TCIPP [eia-sjodin2020]. These chemicals migrate into house dust. In 50 US homes, dust TDCPP and TPHP were found in 96-98% of samples at up to 1.8 mg/g [eia-meeker2010]. In 2014, California switched to a fabric smolder standard (TB117-2013) that furniture can meet without added flame retardants [eia-rodgers2021]. Organophosphate metabolites remain near-universal: they were found in 85-95% of EARTH participants [eia-carignan2017, eia-ingle2018], and Australian pooled urine shows stable or rising levels for several of them from 2012 to 2023 [eia-que2025].

**Routes.** Hands matter more than air. In 53 adults, hand-wipe levels of TPHP and TDCIPP predicted their urinary metabolites, whereas dust levels did not (top versus bottom hand-wipe TPHP: 2.4-fold higher urinary DPHP) [eia-hoffman2015]. This is why hand-washing works.

**Persistence.** PBDEs are persistent: serum half-lives were about 0.9-4.1 years depending on the congener in a person who moved from North America to lower-exposure Australia [eia-sjodin2020]. In a Boston IVF cohort, PBDE levels fell by up to 80% between 2005 and 2016 recruits [eia-ingle2020]. The organophosphate replacements clear in days, so a urine sample reflects roughly the last day, although some metabolites were fairly stable over 5 days (ICC 0.51-0.81) [eia-hoffman2015].

**Health evidence.** The firmest finding is for child brain development: a Navigation Guide review judged the evidence "sufficient" that developmental PBDE exposure lowers IQ, by about 3.7 points per 10-fold higher exposure [eia-lam2017]. For oxidative stress, small panels link PBDE mixtures to higher urinary isoprostanes in pregnancy [eia-sehgaln2025] and OPE metabolites to 8-OHdG, 8-isoprostane and MDA [eia-ingle2020ox, eia-li2026ope], while NHANES found nothing [eia-yuan2017].

**Sperm.** House-dust TPHP went with 19% lower sperm concentration in 50 clinic men [eia-meeker2010], and serum PBDEs with lower motility in small clinic and community studies [eia-abdelouahab2011, eia-albert2018, eia-yu2018]; a Chinese clinic study of 1,015 men linked OPE mixtures to lower motility and morphology [eia-li2023ope]. But the best-designed US study (220 men, repeated urine and semen samples) found weak, inconsistent associations [eia-ingle2018], and in 796 sperm donors the "OPE mixture" signal came from organophosphate pesticide metabolites, not the flame retardants [eia-tang2025]. In healthy young men, BDE-47 did not track sperm chromatin damage [eia-albert2018]. See [flame-retardants-sperm].

**Eggs and IVF.** In 211 EARTH women, the highest versus lowest quartile of summed OPFR metabolites went with 10% lower fertilization, 31% lower implantation, 41% lower clinical pregnancy and 38% lower live birth [eia-carignan2017]. Fathers' levels were tied to fewer fertilized eggs and top-quality embryos but not to live birth [eia-carignan2018]. No independent IVF cohort has replicated the maternal finding; a 99-woman Taiwanese study linked two metabolites to low AMH with very wide confidence intervals [eia-lien2025]. For PBDEs, results conflict outright: longer time to pregnancy in a Californian cohort [eia-harley2010] (disputed in a letter [eia-goodman2010]), failed implantation with detectable follicular BDE-153 in 65 IVF patients [eia-johnson2012], but *higher* implantation and live birth with serum BDE-153 in 215 EARTH patients [eia-ingle2020]. See [flame-retardants-egg].

**What lowers exposure.**

| Intervention | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| One week of instructed house cleaning, hand-washing, or both | 32 mothers, sequential intervention | Some urinary OPFR metabolites and (in high-exposure women) hand-wipe levels fell, by about half in some cases [eia-gibson2019] |
| Replacing pre-2014 upholstered furniture, or only its foam, with TB117-2013 products | California homes, 6-18 months follow-up | All measured PBDEs and organophosphate flame retardants in dust fell in nearly all homes [eia-rodgers2021] |

## 2. Gas and propane stoves

**Emissions.** Gas stoves leak 0.8-1.3% of their gas unburned, three-quarters of it while switched off, and emit NOx in proportion to gas burned; without ventilation, a small kitchen can exceed the US 1-hour NO2 standard (100 ppb) within minutes [eia-lebel2022]. Burners and ovens also emit 2.8-6.5 ug of benzene per minute, 10-25 times more than electric coil or radiant stoves, while induction hobs and the food emitted none; benzene spread to bedrooms and stayed elevated for hours [eia-kashtan2023]. Unburned gas itself carries benzene (detected in 99% of 185 California samples) [eia-lebel2022hap]. National modelling estimates that gas and propane stoves add 4.0 ppb to long-term NO2 exposure on average, about 75% of the WHO guideline, and four times more in homes under 800 ft2 than in homes over 3000 ft2, with higher exposure in Black, Hispanic and Native American households [eia-kashtan2024, eia-who2021aqg]. A modelling study of the highest-emitting 5% of stoves found benzene cancer risks above one in a million under heavy use without ventilation, and good vented hoods lowered them [eia-garg2025].

**Health.** A 2013 meta-analysis of 41 studies found gas cooking associated with childhood asthma (OR 1.32) and indoor NO2 with current wheeze [eia-lin2013]. Applying that kind of estimate to US stove prevalence gave the headline that 12.7% of US childhood asthma is attributable to gas stoves [eia-gruenwald2022]. That number assumes causality, and the debate is set out below.

**Intervention trials.**

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Paulin 2014 | 3-arm RCT, homes with unvented gas stoves | Electric stove: kitchen NO2 -51% at 3 months; HEPA+carbon purifier: -27% immediately; new range hood: no significant change [eia-paulin2014] |
| Pilotto 2004 | RCT, 18 schools, 118 asthmatic children | Replacing unflued gas heaters: classroom NO2 15.5 vs 47.0 ppb; daytime and night-time asthma symptoms roughly halved or better; lung function unchanged [eia-pilotto2004] |
| Howden-Chapman 2008 | RCT, 409 children with asthma | Non-polluting heaters: fewer school absences, doctor visits and night symptoms; lung function not significantly better [eia-howdenchapman2008] |
| Sehgal 2026 | Before-after, 85 adults with poorly controlled asthma | Gas to electric stove: NO2 21.0 to 6.3 ppb; asthma control score 2.6 to 1.5 in 2-3 months; no control group [eia-sehgal2026] |

The randomized evidence that removing indoor gas combustion helps asthma comes from **heaters**, which burn far more gas than stoves. For stoves, only an uncontrolled study has measured symptoms.

**Oxidative stress.** At typical indoor levels (median 6.8 ppb) NO2 did not track CRP, urinary 8-OHdG or MDA in COPD patients [eia-mchugh2023]. Gas stoves are a respiratory and benzene issue more than a measurable systemic oxidative-stress issue.

## 3. VOCs and formaldehyde

**Sources.** New pressed-wood furniture, flooring and insulation (formaldehyde), fresh paint and adhesives, fragranced air fresheners and cleaners (terpenes, which form formaldehyde and particles when they react with ozone) [eia-angulomilhem2020], and anything burned indoors. Candles burning in a draught emit soot-dominated PM2.5, PAHs, ultrafine particles, NOx and formaldehyde, with large differences by wax and wick [eia-andersen2021]. In new homes, VOCs such as toluene, hexane and methyl ethyl ketone were highest at first occupancy and declined over the following year [eia-yamaguchi2006].

**Guidance.** WHO's indoor air guidelines cover formaldehyde, benzene, naphthalene, NO2, PAHs, radon, trichloroethylene and tetrachloroethylene [eia-who2010indoor]. The formaldehyde guideline of 0.1 mg/m3 (0.08 ppm) is set against sensory irritation and argued to protect against nasal cancer because the animal dose-response is strongly non-linear [eia-nielsen2010].

**Health.** Three meta-analyses agree on a modest association with childhood asthma: OR 1.17 [eia-mcgwin2010], 1.10 [eia-yu2020] and 1.20 [eia-lam2021] per 10 ug/m3 of formaldehyde, all built mostly on cross-sectional studies. Among 61,320 Singapore Chinese adults, long-term heavy incense use was associated with squamous-cell carcinomas of the respiratory tract (RR 1.8) but not with lung cancer overall [eia-friborg2008]. In a blinded chamber study, office workers' cognitive scores were 61% higher in low-VOC conditions [eia-allen2016].

**Houseplants do not clean the air.** Converting 196 chamber results into clean-air delivery rates showed that 10 to 1,000 potted plants per square metre of floor would be needed to match the removal already provided by normal air exchange [eia-cummings2020].

**Reproduction.** The reproductive data on formaldehyde come from occupational exposure and are covered under solvents below.

## 4. Household cleaning products

**Respiratory evidence.** In the European Community Respiratory Health Survey, weekly use of cleaning sprays (42% of participants) was associated with new asthma symptoms or medication (RR 1.49) and wheeze (RR 1.39), with dose-response; glass, furniture and air-freshener sprays drove the risk [eia-zock2007]. Over 20 years, women responsible for home cleaning lost FEV1 at 22.1 mL/yr and occupational cleaners at 22.4 mL/yr, against 18.5 mL/yr in women who did not clean; no difference was seen in men [eia-svanes2018]. Weekly surface-disinfectant use at work was associated with COPD in 73,262 US nurses [eia-dumas2019], and healthcare workers using bleach had higher asthma risk in a meta-analysis of high-risk-of-bias studies [eia-romerostarke2021]. In 30,012 French adults, persistent irritant/spray use tracked asthma symptoms and so, unexpectedly, did "green" products [eia-pachecodasilva2026]. Children in bleach-using homes reported more infections in a cross-sectional study [eia-casas2015].

**Quaternary ammonium compounds (quats).** Benzalkonium (ADBAC) and DDAC are in many disinfecting sprays and wipes, and their use rose during COVID-19 [eia-arnold2023]. QACs were detected in the blood of 80% of 43 volunteers, and blood levels tracked higher inflammatory cytokines and lower mitochondrial function [eia-hrubec2021]. In mice, an ADBAC+DDAC disinfectant reduced fertility and fecundity [eia-melin2014], affected ovulation, estrus and sperm concentration and motility [eia-melin2016], and lowered LH, FSH and Sertoli-cell metabolism [eia-kirkpatrick2025].

**Human reproduction.** There are no human fertility studies of household quats. In nurses, a retrospective analysis linked sterilizing agents and disinfectants to 2-fold late miscarriage [eia-lawson2012], while a prospective cohort found no association between high-level disinfectant use and miscarriage overall (HR 0.84) [eia-ding2021].

## 5. House dust and dust control

US indoor dust consistently contains phthalates, phenols, replacement flame retardants, fragrances and PFAS [eia-mitro2016], and dust is a reservoir for microplastics ([microplastics]) and lead ([heavy-metals]). Children swallow about 56-72 mg of soil and dust a day, adults 12-32 mg [eia-cohen2024].

| Intervention | Outcome | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaning and/or hand-washing, 1 week | Flame-retardant biomarkers | Some fell by about half [eia-gibson2019] |
| Household education and dust control (Cochrane, 17 trials) | Children's blood lead | No clear effect [eia-nussbaumerstreit2020] |
| Mite-control measures (Cochrane, 54 trials) | Asthma in mite-allergic patients | No effect (review now flagged as outdated) [eia-gotzsche2008] |
| Tailored multi-component home programme, 937 children | Asthma symptom days | 3.39 vs 4.20 per 2 weeks, lasting a year after the intervention [eia-morgan2004] |

The lesson: generic, single-step dust control rarely moves health outcomes, but it does lower short-lived chemical biomarkers, and targeted multi-component programmes can help asthma.

## 6. Occupational solvents and chemical fumes

**Oxidative markers.** Industrial painters with solvent levels below biological exposure limits had higher MDA and antioxidant-enzyme activity and lower thiols than controls [eia-moro2010]; young female dry-cleaning workers had higher MDA and altered cytokines [eia-li2024pce].

**Sperm.** In Canadian fertility clinics, high solvent exposure was associated with motile sperm under 12 million/mL (OR 3.83, replicated at 2.90 in a second series), though moderate exposure replicated in only one series [eia-cherry2001]. In 14 UK clinics, high glycol ether exposure in the previous 3 months gave an adjusted OR of 2.25 for low motile sperm count [eia-cherry2008]. Shipyard painters exposed to 2-ethoxyethanol and 2-methoxyethanol had more oligospermia [eia-welch1988]. Two Chinese studies link occupational formaldehyde to lower motility [eia-wang2015fa, eia-lv2022]. See [occupational-solvents-sperm].

**Pregnancy and fertility in women.** A meta-analysis found major malformations more common with maternal solvent exposure (OR 1.64) and a non-significant miscarriage trend (OR 1.25) [eia-mcmartin1998]; formaldehyde-exposed women had RR 1.76 for miscarriage with recall and selection bias possible [eia-duong2011]. UK dry-cleaning machine operators had 17.1% miscarriage against 10.9% in unexposed pregnancies [eia-doyle1997], and solvent-exposed pharmaceutical lab workers had more miscarriage and longer time to pregnancy [eia-attarchi2012]. A 49-study review called the time-to-pregnancy evidence for solvents equivocal, unlike lead and pesticides [eia-snijder2012]. See [occupational-solvents-egg].

## 7. Damp and mould

Building dampness and mould are associated with 30-50% increases in respiratory and asthma outcomes [eia-fisk2007] and with new-onset asthma (summary estimates 1.27-1.48; mould odour 1.73) [eia-quansah2012]. Cochrane found moderate- to very-low-quality evidence that repairing mould-damaged houses and offices reduces adult wheeze (OR 0.64) and respiratory infections [eia-sauni2015]. Insulating low-income New Zealand homes made them warmer and drier and reduced wheeze (OR 0.57) and days off school and work [eia-howdenchapman2007]. WHO's central recommendation is to prevent persistent dampness and microbial growth [eia-who2009mould]; German clinical guidance on mould diagnostics was updated in 2023 [eia-hurrass2024]. The oxidative-stress angle is only mechanistic, and there are no fertility data.

## 8. Radon

Radon is the clearest indoor carcinogen. Pooled European case-control data (7,148 cases) show lung cancer risk rising 16% per 100 Bq/m3 of usual radon, linearly and with no threshold; by age 75 the absolute risk at 0, 100 and 400 Bq/m3 is about 0.4%, 0.5% and 0.7% for lifelong non-smokers but 10%, 12% and 16% for smokers [eia-darby2005]. North American pooled data agree (OR 1.11 at 100 Bq/m3) [eia-krewski2005]. WHO recommends a national reference level of 100 Bq/m3, and no more than 300 Bq/m3 where that is unachievable, and attributes 3-14% of lung cancers to radon [eia-whoradonfs, eia-who2009radon]. Sub-slab depressurisation brought all of 170 UK homes below 200 Bq/m3, with more than 80% reductions in over three-quarters of them [eia-groveskirkby2008]. For smokers, quitting reduces radon-related risk more than mitigation does [eia-mendez1998]. Radon is not linked to fertility at residential levels.

## 9. Ventilation and CO2

Ventilation is the shared fix for most indoor-sourced pollutants. A multidisciplinary review found higher office ventilation (up to about 25 L/s per person) associated with fewer sick-building symptoms, and home ventilation above 0.5 air changes per hour with fewer allergic manifestations in Nordic children [eia-sundell2011]. Office workers in lower-ventilation areas took 53% more short-term sick leave [eia-milton2000]. In student bedrooms, lowering CO2 from about 2,400-2,600 to 660-835 ppm improved objectively measured sleep, next-day sleepiness and a logical-thinking test [eia-stromtejsen2016].

Whether CO2 itself impairs thinking is unresolved (see below). A CO2 monitor is best read as a gauge of how well a room is ventilated.

## Audit of existing factors

| Factor | Verdict | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| [indoor-combustion-smoke] | Holds | Large cookstove RCTs (HAPIN, CAPS) cut exposure but not birth weight or pneumonia [eia-clasen2022, eia-mccollum2024, eia-mortimer2017]; US wood-stove filter and changeout trials did not reduce child infections or (for changeouts) indoor PM2.5 [eia-walker2022, eia-ward2017] |
| [hepa-air-purifier] | Weakened | Surrogate benefits confirmed (BP -2 to -4 mmHg, CRP -7%) [eia-walzer2020, eia-xia2021, eia-wittkopp2022], but 4- to 12-week trials in older adults and asthmatic children found no clinical benefit [eia-brook2026, eia-qiu2025] |
| [outdoor-air-pollution] | Holds (egg side weaker) | Danish nationwide cohort: male infertility HR 1.24 per 2.9 ug/m3 PM2.5, null in women [eia-sorensen2024]; 20-year AMH cohort null [eia-zou2025]; 21,851-sample US DFI study positive [eia-fouks2025] |
| [secondhand-smoke] | Holds | Newer meta-analyses put CVD excess at 22-27% [eia-zhang2020shs, eia-fischer2015]; fertility claims rest on thin data [eia-neal2005] |
| [mobile-phones] | Holds | WHO-commissioned reviews: very uncertain evidence, no front-pocket effect [eia-kenny2024, eia-cordelli2024]; Swiss association fading over time [eia-rahban2023] |

The confounding story is similar across these: indoor and outdoor exposures cluster with income, housing quality, smoking and crowding. Where randomized trials exist, they confirm that interventions lower exposure (stove swaps, filters, cleaner cookstoves), but clinical benefits have been smaller or absent when exposure stays well above guideline levels (cookstoves) or starts low (filters in cleaner settings).

## Where the evidence is contested

1. **Gas stoves and childhood asthma.** A meta-analysis found OR 1.32 with no publication bias [eia-lin2013], used to attribute 12.7% of US childhood asthma to gas stoves [eia-gruenwald2022]. A systematic review by a consultancy that has worked for gas-industry clients judged the studies too biased for causal inference [eia-li2023gas], an American Gas Association-funded commentary rejected the attributable fraction [eia-li2024gas], an independent commentary called it a case study in questionable research practices [eia-cox2023], and the ISAAC global analysis found open fires, not gas, clearly associated with asthma [eia-wong2013]. Randomized heater removal supports harm from indoor gas combustion [eia-pilotto2004, eia-howdenchapman2008].
2. **Flame retardants and fertility.** EARTH found large OPFR-IVF associations [eia-carignan2017] that are unreplicated; PBDE findings run in opposite directions [eia-harley2010, eia-goodman2010, eia-johnson2012, eia-ingle2020]; semen associations are inconsistent [eia-meeker2010, eia-ingle2018, eia-tang2025].
3. **Flame retardants and oxidative stress.** Positive in small panels [eia-sehgaln2025, eia-ingle2020ox, eia-li2026ope], null in NHANES [eia-yuan2017].
4. **Quats and reproduction.** Academic mouse studies show subfertility [eia-melin2014, eia-melin2016, eia-kirkpatrick2025]; industry committees disputed them [eia-hostetler2015] and a review weighted towards unpublished guideline studies rated them unreliable [eia-desesso2021]; an independent review lists QACs as a class of emerging concern [eia-arnold2023].
5. **Disinfectants and miscarriage.** Retrospective 2-fold late miscarriage [eia-lawson2012] versus prospective null [eia-ding2021].
6. **Formaldehyde guideline and cancer.** Non-linear threshold reasoning supports the WHO 0.1 mg/m3 value [eia-nielsen2010]; linear unit-risk approaches would set lower values.
7. **Mould and systemic illness.** Respiratory effects are consistent [eia-fisk2007, eia-quansah2012, eia-sauni2015]. "Mycotoxin illness" is unproven according to an allergy society [eia-bush2006], and CDC warned about unvalidated urine tests [eia-kawamoto2015]; critics argue an earlier occupational-medicine statement minimizing mould harm had litigation-related conflicts [eia-craner2008].
8. **Radon at low doses.** Pooled case-control studies show a linear, no-threshold risk [eia-darby2005, eia-krewski2005]; an ecological county analysis suggested the opposite [eia-cohen1995], explained by smoking confounding [eia-puskin2003].
9. **CO2 and cognition.** Effects at 1,000 ppm [eia-satish2012] and large ventilation effects [eia-allen2016] versus no effect of pure CO2 up to 3,000-5,000 ppm [eia-zhang2017, eia-scully2019] and none in sleeping children [eia-klausen2023].
10. **Purifiers.** Surrogate benefits [eia-walzer2020, eia-wittkopp2022] versus very low certainty [eia-xia2021] and null clinical trials [eia-qiu2025, eia-brook2026, eia-barn2018, eia-walker2022].
11. **Air pollution and female fertility.** IVF and AMH associations [eia-legro2010, eia-lamarca2020] versus a 20-year longitudinal null [eia-zou2025] and a null nationwide female cohort [eia-sorensen2024].
12. **Secondhand smoke size of effect.** About 30% and "80-90% of active smoking" [eia-barnoya2005] versus a null spousal-smoking cohort [eia-enstrom2003, eia-daveysmith2003] and newer pooled estimates of 22-27% [eia-zhang2020shs, eia-fischer2015].
13. **Phones and sperm.** Associations without dose-response [eia-kim2021, eia-rahban2023] versus WHO reviews finding very uncertain evidence [eia-kenny2024, eia-cordelli2024], whose methods are themselves disputed [eia-melnick2025].

## Practical bottom line

**Do first (best evidence, low cost):**
- **Test for radon** with a 3-month passive detector; mitigate above 100 Bq/m3 [eia-whoradonfs, eia-darby2005]. If you smoke, quitting matters more [eia-mendez1998].
- **Fix damp and mould** at the source (leaks, ventilation, insulation, heating) [eia-sauni2015, eia-howdenchapman2007].
- **Swap sprays for wipes and cloths**, use plain detergent for routine cleaning, keep bleach and quats for real need, and never mix bleach with acids or ammonia [eia-zock2007, eia-svanes2018].
- **Wash hands before eating** and wet-mop / HEPA-vacuum weekly; it cuts flame-retardant biomarkers within a week [eia-gibson2019].

**If you cook on gas:** use an outdoor-venting hood on high, back burners, and an open window; consider induction when replacing the stove; a HEPA-plus-carbon purifier lowers NO2 [eia-paulin2014, eia-kashtan2023].

**When buying furniture or renovating:** choose TB117-2013, flame-retardant-free upholstered furniture (replacing just the foam also works) [eia-rodgers2021]; low-VOC paint; air new items out for months [eia-yamaguchi2006]; skip plug-in fresheners and frequent candles or incense [eia-andersen2021, eia-friborg2008]; do not count on plants [eia-cummings2020].

**If you are trying to conceive and work with chemicals:** start reducing occupational solvent, formaldehyde and disinfectant exposure about 3 months before trying, for both partners, using exhaust ventilation, respirators and reassignment [eia-cherry2008, eia-doyle1997].

**Keep air moving:** ventilate bedrooms at night, aiming for CO2 mostly below about 1,000 ppm as a rule of thumb [eia-stromtejsen2016]; on high outdoor-pollution days close windows and filter instead ([hepa-air-purifier]).

## What we don't know

- Whether lowering flame-retardant exposure improves IVF outcomes, semen or child development; no intervention study has measured health outcomes.
- Whether the EARTH OPFR-IVF association replicates in another cohort.
- Whether replacing gas stoves reduces childhood asthma in a randomized trial; only heater trials and an uncontrolled stove study exist.
- Whether household (not occupational) quats affect human fertility.
- Whether fragranced products, candles and incense at typical home use change any health outcome.
- How fast sperm recover after occupational solvent exposure stops.
- Whether purifiers have clinical benefits over months in moderately polluted homes.
- Whether CO2 itself, rather than what accompanies it, affects cognition at indoor levels.
