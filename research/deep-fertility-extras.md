# IVF add-ons, ovarian reserve & the couple's preconception plan

Deep-dive notes for Redox Compass. Structured data for this topic lives in the patch file (20 factors). Every PubMed reference below was fetched and checked during this research session (title, year, DOI, abstract and retraction status; none is retracted). The UK regulator's ratings come from the HFEA website, read on 1 October 2026 [fer-hfea2026, fer-hfea2026sdf, fer-hfea2026prp, fer-hfea2025androgen]. This page builds on the existing sperm and egg pages and links to their factors in square brackets, such as [maternal-age] or [short-abstinence].

## The short version

- **Most IVF "add-ons" do not raise the chance of a baby.** The UK regulator (HFEA) rates 17 add-ons and gives none of them a green light for live birth [fer-hfea2026]. ESHRE's 2023 review of 42 add-on questions found no recommendation backed by high-quality evidence. It positively recommends only one add-on: hyaluronan "embryo glue", used with single embryo transfer [fer-eshre2023addons].
- **Some add-ons are worse than useless.** PGT-A, endometrial receptivity testing, IVIG, steroids and PRP carry HFEA red ratings, for cost, lost embryos or safety risks without benefit [fer-hfea2026, fer-hfea2026prp]. In the biggest PGT-A trial, cumulative live birth was *lower* with testing (77.2% vs 81.8%) [fer-yan2021].
- **AMH measures egg number, not egg quality or natural fertility.** Women aged 30-44 with low AMH conceived naturally as often as women with normal AMH [fer-steiner2017]. AMH does predict how many eggs IVF or egg freezing will produce [fer-broer2013].
- **Egg freezing is a numbers-and-age game.** With 10-15 eggs frozen at 35 or younger, cumulative live birth reached about 85%, against a plateau of about 36% when freezing at 36 or older [fer-cobo2016]. Most women never come back to use their eggs [fer-blakemore2021].
- **In pregnancy, antioxidant pills failed and aspirin works.** Vitamin C + E did not prevent pre-eclampsia in four large trials [fer-poston2006, fer-villar2009, fer-roberts2010, fer-mccance2010]. Low-dose aspirin cut preterm pre-eclampsia by about 60% in high-risk women [fer-rolnik2017].
- **Fertility is a couple issue.** A male factor is involved in about half of infertile couples [fer-thonneau1991, fer-agarwal2015]. Damage in the sperm is repaired by the egg, so high sperm DNA fragmentation matters most when the egg is older [fer-setti2021, fer-meseguer2011]. Sperm take about 2-3 months to make and eggs about 3 months to mature, so both partners share one 3-month runway [fer-misell2006, fer-gougeon1986].

## 1. IVF add-ons

### What an "add-on" is, and why they spread

An add-on is an optional extra on top of standard IVF or ICSI, usually at extra cost, that claims to raise the chance of a baby. Most target one of four things: choosing embryos (PGT-A, time-lapse imaging), helping implantation (scratching, embryo glue, hCG, receptivity tests, immune treatments), boosting egg number or quality (growth hormone, DHEA, PRP, mitochondria, antioxidants), or choosing sperm (PICSI, MACS, microfluidics).

They are very common. In an Australian national survey, 82% of IVF patients had used at least one add-on, usually paying extra. Half assumed add-ons were known to be safe, and 66% later reported some regret [fer-lensen2021survey]. An Oxford review of UK fertility clinic websites found that most of the extras on offer lacked good evidence [fer-heneghan2016].

### How the HFEA rates them

The HFEA's scientific committee rates each add-on with a five-colour scale, mainly for the outcome "chances of having a baby" [fer-hfea2026]:

- **Green:** high-quality evidence shows it works. *No add-on currently has green for live birth.*
- **Yellow/amber:** unclear; evidence conflicts.
- **Grey:** cannot rate; not enough moderate or high-quality evidence.
- **Black:** moderate or high-quality evidence shows no effect.
- **Red:** safety concerns, and/or good evidence that it may reduce success.

ESHRE's 2023 good-practice recommendations cover a wider list and use "recommended", "can be considered", "not recommended for routine clinical use" and "not recommended" [fer-eshre2023addons].

### Notes on each add-on

**Endometrial scratching.** The idea is that a small injury to the womb lining causes a healing response that makes it more receptive. Early small trials looked dramatic. Then the pragmatic PIP trial (1364 women) found exactly the same live birth rate with and without scratching, 26.1% in both groups, with a median pain score of 3.5 out of 10 [fer-lensen2019]. Cochrane's 2026 update, restricted to low-risk-of-bias trials, puts the effect between "none" and "a few points" (OR 1.12, 0.98-1.28) [fer-perera2026]. An individual-participant-data meta-analysis that excluded trials failing integrity checks found a modest gain (OR 1.29) [fer-vanhoogenhuijze2023], but it was criticised in print [fer-aktoz2024]. HFEA amber; ESHRE not for routine use [fer-hfea2026, fer-eshre2023addons].

**Embryo glue.** Hyaluronan is a sticky sugar found in uterine fluid at implantation. Cochrane found that 0.5 mg/mL hyaluronan in the transfer medium probably raises live birth from about 33% to 37-44% (moderate certainty). It also raises multiple pregnancy by about 45% [fer-heymann2020]. The gain appears with women's own eggs, not clearly with donor eggs [fer-heymann2022]. ESHRE recommends it together with single embryo transfer; HFEA still calls it unclear [fer-eshre2023addons, fer-hfea2026]. Of all the add-ons, this is the most defensible.

**Assisted hatching.** Assisted hatching thins or opens the embryo's shell (zona pellucida). Across 14 trials that reported live birth, it made no clear difference (OR 1.09), and it may raise twins [fer-lacey2021]. ESHRE: not recommended [fer-eshre2023addons].

**Time-lapse imaging.** Time-lapse incubators photograph embryos continuously, so they stay undisturbed and give timing data for choosing between them. Two large blinded trials found no gain: TILT (live birth 33.7% with time-lapse vs 33.0% standard) [fer-bhide2024] and SelecTIMO (12-month ongoing pregnancy 50.8% vs 49.4%) [fer-kieslinger2023]. HFEA rates it black [fer-hfea2026].

**PGT-A.** PGT-A removes a few cells from each blastocyst and tests them for whole-chromosome errors (aneuploidy). Aneuploidy rises steeply with the egg's age [fer-franasiak2014], so filtering out abnormal embryos sounds logical. The catch is that PGT-A cannot create normal embryos. It only chooses among the ones already made, and every embryo it wrongly rejects is a lost chance.

- The STAR trial (661 women) found no gain in ongoing pregnancy per woman (41.8% vs 43.5%) [fer-munne2019].
- In Yan et al. (1212 women with at least three good blastocysts), cumulative live birth was 77.2% with PGT-A vs 81.8% without. Pregnancy loss was lower with testing (8.7% vs 12.6%) [fer-yan2021].
- A 2026 systematic review counting live birth per randomised woman found no gain, including over age 35, no shorter time to a baby, and a near doubling of cost [fer-iwarsson2026]. Cochrane is likewise uncertain [fer-cornelisse2020].

HFEA rates PGT-A red for having a baby but green for reducing miscarriage [fer-hfea2026]. ESHRE: not for routine use [fer-eshre2023addons].

**ICSI with normal sperm.** ICSI injects one sperm into each egg. It is essential for real male-factor infertility. By 2012, however, it was used in 67% of US cycles *without* male factor [fer-boulet2015]. In couples with normal sperm count and motility, the Dang trial (1064 couples) found no live-birth gain (35% vs 31%) and no less total fertilisation failure (5% vs 6%) [fer-dang2021]. ASRM and ESHRE advise against routine use [fer-asrm2020icsi, fer-eshre2023addons].

**Intrauterine hCG.** Cochrane found a benefit only when 500 IU or more was infused before a day-3 transfer, and no evidence for blastocyst transfers, which are now standard [fer-craciunas2018]. ESHRE: not recommended [fer-eshre2023addons].

**Immune tests and treatments.** These rest on the idea that the mother's immune system (especially natural killer cells) rejects the embryo. HFEA says immune rejection of the fetus rarely if ever happens. It rates IVIG and steroids red, because they have real risks (a blood product with thrombosis and kidney risk; infection, high blood pressure and possible preterm birth) and no proven benefit. Intralipids are rated grey [fer-hfea2026]. ESHRE recommends against blood immune tests, uterine NK-cell testing and all immunomodulators [fer-eshre2023addons, fer-eshre2023rif]. Cochrane is uncertain about steroids (OR 1.37, 0.69-2.71) [fer-boomsma2022].

**Endometrial receptivity testing.** A biopsy in a practice cycle is used to "personalise" transfer timing. In the independent JAMA trial of euploid frozen transfers, live birth was 58.5% with test-timed transfer vs 61.9% standard [fer-doyle2022]. HFEA red; ESHRE not recommended [fer-hfea2026, fer-eshre2023addons].

**Sperm selection.** These methods aim to give the egg sperm with less oxidative DNA damage. PICSI (hyaluronan binding) did not raise term live birth in 2772 couples (27.4% vs 25.2%) [fer-miller2019]. Cochrane 2026 says it may make no or a slight difference to live birth but probably slightly reduces miscarriage (RR 0.59) [fer-garg2026]. MACS was null in randomised trials [fer-romany2014, fer-mantravadi2024]. Microfluidic chips lower DNA fragmentation in the prepared sample, but trial results conflict: one small positive RCT [fer-aydin2022] and one null RCT [fer-huong2026]. HFEA: PICSI black, SDF-guided selection grey, and testicular sperm for male factor red [fer-hfea2026, fer-hfea2026sdf]. For cheaper first steps with high SDF, see [short-abstinence] and [varicocele].

**Growth hormone for poor responders.** Growth hormone adds about 1.4 eggs. Cochrane pooling suggests more live births (OR 1.77), but rates the evidence very low certainty [fer-sood2021]. The placebo-controlled LIGHT trial (stopped early, 113 women) found 14.5% vs 13.7% [fer-norman2019]. ESHRE lists it among adjuncts that are not recommended [fer-eshre2023addons].

**PRP "ovarian rejuvenation" and intrauterine PRP.** Platelet-rich plasma is the patient's own concentrated platelets, injected into the ovaries or infused into the womb. In the PROVA RCT, ovarian PRP changed nothing: mature eggs, euploid embryos, AMH and antral follicle count were all unchanged [fer-herlihy2024]. A double-blind RCT found about 1.5 more mature eggs over three retrievals but exactly the same number of euploid blastocysts (0.81 vs 0.81) [fer-barrenetxea2024]. For intrauterine PRP, the only low-risk-of-bias trial was null for live birth (OR 1.10, 0.38-3.14) [fer-vaidakis2024]. HFEA rates every PRP indication red [fer-hfea2026prp].

**Mitochondrial augmentation (AUGMENT).** Ageing eggs have weaker mitochondria, so AUGMENT injected mitochondria from the woman's own ovarian precursor cells along with the sperm. The randomised pilot was discontinued: fewer embryos reached blastocyst (23.3% vs 41.1%), and live birth per transferred embryo was identical (41.6% vs 41.2%) [fer-labarta2019]. ESHRE: mitochondrial replacement to improve egg quality is not recommended [fer-eshre2023addons].

**DHEA and testosterone priming.** Cochrane (2024, moderate certainty) found that DHEA probably makes little or no difference to live birth (OR 1.30, 0.95-1.76). Testosterone looked more promising, but on weaker evidence [fer-naik2024]. HFEA rates DHEA black in poor responders [fer-hfea2025androgen]. See the existing [dhea] factor.

**Antioxidants in culture media.** This is the one place an antioxidant reaches the embryo directly, with no question of absorption. In a sibling-egg RCT (133 patients), adding acetyl-L-carnitine, NAC and alpha-lipoic acid to the medium raised good day-3 embryos (50.2% vs 40.7%). Ongoing pregnancy was higher only in a 35-40 subgroup, and live birth was not the primary outcome [fer-gardner2020]. It is promising but unreplicated. Oral antioxidants for IVF are not recommended by ESHRE [fer-eshre2023addons].

## 2. Ovarian reserve: what AMH and follicle counts can and cannot tell you

**What they measure.** Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) is made by small growing follicles. The antral follicle count (AFC) is an ultrasound count of follicles 2-10 mm across. Both reflect how many follicles are left, which is egg *quantity* [fer-asrm2020reserve].

**What they predict well: response to IVF stimulation.** In an individual-patient meta-analysis of 5705 IVF patients, AMH and AFC each predicted poor response well (AUC 0.78 and 0.76) [fer-broer2013]. They also predict an excessive response, with about 82% sensitivity [fer-broer2011]. That makes them useful for choosing drug doses and setting expectations before IVF or egg freezing.

**What they do not predict: natural fertility, IVF live birth or egg quality.**

- In the Time to Conceive cohort (750 women aged 30-44 trying naturally), women with low AMH (<0.7 ng/mL) had the same or better chance of conceiving: 65% vs 62% by 6 cycles and 84% vs 75% by 12 cycles [fer-steiner2017].
- For live birth after IVF, age was the best single predictor and AMH/AFC added nothing [fer-broer2013].
- ASRM now states that these markers predict egg yield but are "poor predictors of reproductive potential independently from age" [fer-asrm2020reserve].

Egg quality, meaning the chance an egg is chromosomally normal, is driven by age (see [maternal-age]) [fer-franasiak2014]. A 30-year-old with a low AMH has fewer eggs than her peers, but they are mostly 30-year-old eggs.

**AMH and menopause.** AMH is the best available single predictor of age at natural menopause, but the prediction intervals are too wide for individual planning [fer-depmann2016]. It is more useful close to the event. In women in their late 40s, an AMH below 10 pg/mL meant a 51-79% chance of the final period within 12 months, depending on age [fer-finkelstein2020].

### Egg freezing

**How well it works.** Vitrified eggs survive warming at about 75-85% and perform about as well as fresh eggs [fer-doyle2016, fer-cobo2016, fer-blakemore2021]. The odds per egg are low, though: about 6% of thawed eggs became a baby in one programme, falling with age [fer-doyle2016]. So the two levers are age at freezing and number of eggs.

The Cobo authors concluded that at least 8-10 mature eggs are needed for reasonable success [fer-cobo2016]. Counselling models (Goldman, Doyle) turn age and egg number into personal estimates, but they are partly extrapolated from fresh IVF rather than from women who came back to use frozen eggs [fer-goldman2017, fer-doyle2016]. ASRM's 2021 guideline says live birth after planned freezing cannot yet be predicted precisely, though it is clearly better when freezing is done younger [fer-asrm2021oc].

**When it makes sense.** The case is strongest for women in their early-to-mid 30s who expect to delay pregnancy by several years and can afford one or two cycles. Before cancer treatment, it is standard care [fer-eshre2020fp]. It makes much less sense after 40, when the yield of euploid eggs is low [fer-franasiak2014, fer-wennberg2019].

**The "social" egg freezing debate.** Supporters argue it gives women more equal footing at work and more time to find a partner [fer-goold2009]. Clinic-based authors argue for freezing younger, while noting that cost-effectiveness peaks at 37-38, when biological odds are already lower [fer-cobo2016review]. Critics point out that most women never use their eggs: 9% had returned in the Cobo cohort, 15% in Sweden and 38% after 10-15 years in New York [fer-cobo2016, fer-wennberg2019, fer-blakemore2021]. Ethicists warn that employer-paid freezing can become pressure to postpone [fer-mertes2015]. ASRM considers planned egg freezing ethically permissible, provided people are told plainly about its uncertain efficacy [fer-asrm2024ethics].

## 3. Pregnancy and oxidative stress (brief)

**Placental oxidative stress is real.** Reactive oxygen species have normal signalling roles in the placenta. In excess, they are involved in miscarriage, pre-eclampsia and membrane rupture [fer-burton2011]. In pre-eclampsia, a stressed placenta, either poorly anchored early in pregnancy or ageing late in pregnancy, releases factors that damage the mother's blood vessels [fer-burton2019].

**But antioxidant pills failed.** Vitamin C 1000 mg plus vitamin E 400 IU daily was tested in four large trials:

- VIP: 2404 high-risk women; pre-eclampsia 15% vs 16%, and *more* low-birthweight babies (28% vs 24%) [fer-poston2006].
- WHO trial: 1365 high-risk women in low-nutrition countries; no benefit [fer-villar2009].
- US MFMU trial: 10,154 first-time mothers; pre-eclampsia 7.2% vs 6.7% [fer-roberts2010].
- DAPIT: 762 women with type 1 diabetes; 15% vs 19%, not significant [fer-mccance2010].

Pooled analyses found no prevention and some harm signals: more gestational hypertension and more rupture of the membranes at term (Cochrane RR 1.77) [fer-condeagudelo2011, fer-rumbold2015e]. This is the [vitamin-c-e-combined] story repeated in pregnancy.

**What works: low-dose aspirin, a non-antioxidant.** In ASPRE, women screened as high risk took aspirin 150 mg nightly from 11-14 weeks to 36 weeks. Preterm pre-eclampsia fell from 4.3% to 1.6% [fer-rolnik2017]. USPSTF recommends 81 mg/day after 12 weeks for people at high risk [fer-uspstf2021]. Across 77 trials, antiplatelet drugs cut pre-eclampsia by 18% and preterm birth by 9% [fer-duley2019]. See [aspirin-preeclampsia-egg].

**Folic acid** prevents neural tube defects. The MRC trial found a 72% reduction in recurrence [fer-mrc1991], and USPSTF recommends 0.4-0.8 mg daily for anyone who could become pregnant [fer-uspstf2023]. See [folic-acid].

**Smoking and alcohol in pregnancy.** Smoking during pregnancy raises miscarriage risk by about 32%, rising about 1% for each daily cigarette [fer-pineles2014]; see [tobacco-smoking]. Any alcohol in pregnancy was associated with 19% higher miscarriage risk, and each extra weekly drink (up to 5) added about 6% [fer-sundermann2019]; see [alcohol].

## 4. The couple, not the individual

**Male factor is half the story.** In a French population survey, the woman alone explained infertility in one-third of couples, the man alone in one-fifth, and both partners in 39% [fer-thonneau1991]. A man is therefore involved in roughly half or more of cases. Global estimates of the male share range from 20% to 70% by region [fer-agarwal2015]. AUA/ASRM and ASRM advise evaluating both partners at the same time [fer-schlegel2021a, fer-asrm2021female].

**The egg repairs the sperm, and that ability ages.** Sperm cannot repair their own DNA. After fertilisation, the egg's repair machinery has to fix sperm DNA damage before the embryo first copies its genome. That capacity is limited and probably falls with maternal age [fer-newman2022]. In 540 ICSI cycles, sperm DNA fragmentation of 30% or more made no difference when the woman was 40 or younger. Over 40, pregnancy fell from 20.0% to 7.7% [fer-setti2021]. In another cohort, fragmentation lowered pregnancy with infertile patients' own eggs but not with young donor eggs [fer-meseguer2011]. Even young donor eggs divided more slowly when sperm fragmentation was high, consistent with time spent on repair [fer-esbert2018]. The practical message is that [paternal-age] and sperm DNA damage matter most when [maternal-age] is also high. Couples with an older female partner have more reason to fix reversible male factors quickly.

**One shared timeline.** New sperm first reach the ejaculate about 64 days after they start forming (range 42-76) [fer-misell2006]; the textbook figure is about 74 days [fer-heller1964]. The follicle that ovulates began its final growth about 85 days earlier [fer-gougeon1986, fer-gougeon2010]. Changes started today therefore show up in sperm in about 2-3 months and in eggs in about 3 months. Both partners are on the same runway.

**When to see a specialist (ASRM/ACOG).** See a specialist after 12 months of regular unprotected sex if the woman is under 35. Go after 6 months if she is 35 or older, and right away if she is over 40 [fer-asrm2020def, fer-acog2014]. Earlier evaluation is justified when the history points to a problem, for example irregular or absent periods, known tubal disease or endometriosis, prior chemotherapy, or a known male problem such as an abnormal semen test or varicocele [fer-asrm2020def, fer-asrm2021female, fer-schlegel2021a].

**Both partners' tests.** For her: an ovulation assessment, tubal and uterine assessment, and ovarian reserve testing (AMH/AFC) when planning treatment [fer-asrm2021female, fer-asrm2020reserve]. For him: history and at least one semen analysis, with SDF testing reserved for recurrent miscarriage, failed IVF/ICSI or unexplained infertility [fer-schlegel2021a, fer-agarwal2017stm]. ESHRE's unexplained-infertility guideline sets out the basic work-up for both partners [fer-eshre2023ui].

## Couple's 3-month plan

This plan merges the sperm and egg timelines. Sperm made over the next 64-74 days [fer-misell2006, fer-heller1964], and eggs whose final growth starts now [fer-gougeon1986], are the ones you will conceive with. If she is 35 or older, or IVF is already booked, do not delay treatment to finish the plan. Start everything at once and see the specialist in parallel [fer-acog2014].

**Month -3 (start now): both partners**
- **Both:** stop smoking, and cannabis if used. Smoking roughly halves IVF live-birth odds [fer-waylen2009], and his semen improves 3 and 6 months after quitting [fer-ragheb2025]. See [tobacco-smoking].
- **Both:** cut alcohol. Four or more drinks a week by her was linked to 16% lower IVF live birth, and 21% lower when both drank [fer-rossi2011]. His semen worsens above about 5 units a week [fer-jensen2014]. See [alcohol].
- **Her:** start folic acid 0.4-0.8 mg daily (in a prenatal vitamin) [fer-uspstf2023]. See [folic-acid].
- **Him:** remove testicular heat: no saunas, hot tubs or hot baths. Sauna damage took 6 months to clear [fer-garolla2013]. See [sauna].
- **Both:** if either BMI is 30 or more, start a realistic diet-and-exercise plan. Obesity is linked to about 15% lower IVF live birth [fer-sermondade2019]. See [excess-adiposity].
- **Both:** book tests together. For him, a semen analysis; for her, a review with her doctor. Go straight to a specialist if she is 35+ and you have been trying 6 months, if she is 40+, or if either of you has a known problem [fer-asrm2020def, fer-acog2014, fer-schlegel2021a].
- **Both:** review medicines. If he uses testosterone or anabolic steroids, see a urologist now [fer-brannigan2024]; see [testosterone-anabolic-steroids].

**Month -2: keep going**
- **Both:** consistency matters more than perfection. The sperm being made now, and the follicles in their final growth phase, are the ones you will use [fer-misell2006, fer-gougeon2010].
- **Both:** shift towards a Mediterranean-style diet and moderate exercise. See [mediterranean-diet] and [aerobic-exercise-moderate].
- **Both:** skip megadose antioxidant stacks. Oral antioxidants are not recommended in ART [fer-eshre2023addons]; see [female-antioxidant-supplements] and [male-antioxidant-combination].
- **If heading to IVF:** decide now which add-ons you will decline. The money is usually better spent on a further standard cycle (see the verdict table) [fer-hfea2026, fer-eshre2023addons].

**Month -1: final stretch**
- **Him:** ejaculate every 1-2 days. Abstinence beyond 2 days adds about 3.5 points of SDF [fer-logiudice2024]. See [short-abstinence].
- **Him:** if the first semen test was abnormal, repeat it at least 3 months after the changes began [fer-misell2006].
- **Her:** keep folic acid going; continue to avoid smoking and alcohol [fer-uspstf2023, fer-pineles2014, fer-sundermann2019].

**Trying, or the IVF cycle**
- **Both:** have sex every 1-2 days, especially around ovulation. Short abstinence lowers SDF [fer-logiudice2024].
- **For IUI/IVF/ICSI with high SDF:** ask about a sample after 1 day of abstinence, or a second sample about 3 hours after a first. This lowered SDF from 34.6% to 23.7% [fer-dahan2021]. Ask for conventional IVF rather than ICSI if his semen is normal [fer-dang2021].
- **If she is over 40 and his SDF is high:** fix his reversible causes urgently, because older eggs repair sperm DNA less well [fer-setti2021].
- **Once pregnant:** no smoking or alcohol [fer-pineles2014, fer-sundermann2019]. Ask the obstetric team at booking whether you are high-risk for pre-eclampsia and should take low-dose aspirin from 12 weeks [fer-uspstf2021, fer-rolnik2017]. Do not take high-dose vitamin C + E [fer-poston2006].

## Where the evidence is contested

- **Endometrial scratching.** The IPD meta-analysis found benefit (OR 1.29) [fer-vanhoogenhuijze2023]. A critique questioned it [fer-aktoz2024]. PIP was flat [fer-lensen2019], and Cochrane 2026 calls the effect unclear [fer-perera2026]. Trial-integrity concerns hang over the early positive studies.
- **Embryo glue.** ESHRE recommends it [fer-eshre2023addons] on moderate Cochrane evidence [fer-heymann2020]. HFEA says the evidence is conflicting [fer-hfea2026]. The twin risk is a real cost unless single embryo transfer is used.
- **PGT-A.** Supporters point to fewer miscarriages and faster success per transfer in older women [fer-rubio2017, fer-munne2019], and to near-perfect prediction of failure for embryos labelled aneuploid [fer-tiegs2021]. Critics point to no gain or a loss in cumulative live birth per woman [fer-yan2021, fer-iwarsson2026, fer-cornelisse2020]. They also note that "mosaic" embryos labelled abnormal can make healthy babies, a finding from a study run by a PGT company [fer-capalbo2021], and that per-transfer reporting hides losses [fer-paulson2019].
- **Endometrial receptivity testing.** The developer's RCT reported benefit only per protocol, with about half the patients dropping out [fer-simon2020]. The independent trial was null [fer-doyle2022].
- **Growth hormone.** Cochrane pooling is positive but very low certainty [fer-sood2021]. The one placebo-controlled live-birth trial was null and underpowered [fer-norman2019].
- **PRP.** One RCT found slightly more eggs [fer-barrenetxea2024] and the other found nothing [fer-herlihy2024]. Neither showed more euploid embryos.
- **Immune treatments.** Low-quality meta-analyses and small trials are positive [fer-li2013, fer-singh2019]. Regulators and ESHRE reject these treatments on evidence and safety grounds [fer-hfea2026, fer-eshre2023addons].
- **AUGMENT.** The developers cite early uncontrolled successes [fer-tilly2020]. The only RCT was discontinued without benefit [fer-labarta2019].
- **Sperm selection.** Microfluidics has one small positive RCT [fer-aydin2022] and one null RCT [fer-huong2026]. ESHRE says microfluidics "can be considered" while HFEA rates SDF-guided selection grey [fer-eshre2023addons, fer-hfea2026sdf]. PICSI may cut miscarriage without raising live birth [fer-garg2026, fer-miller2019].
- **AMH and natural fertility.** A small pilot linked low AMH to lower fecundability [fer-steiner2011]. Larger cohorts did not [fer-steiner2017, fer-zarek2015].
- **Egg freezing.** Autonomy arguments [fer-goold2009] and clinic advocacy [fer-cobo2016review] sit against low use rates [fer-blakemore2021, fer-wennberg2019] and concerns about employer pressure [fer-mertes2015].
- **Vitamins C and E in pregnancy.** The DAPIT authors suggested a possible benefit in women with low antioxidant status [fer-mccance2010], and Cochrane found less placental abruption [fer-rumbold2015e]. The large trials were null overall [fer-roberts2010, fer-villar2009], with harm signals [fer-poston2006, fer-condeagudelo2011].
- **Aspirin dose and duration.** ASPRE used 150 mg [fer-rolnik2017] while USPSTF recommends 81 mg [fer-uspstf2021]. StopPRE suggests stopping at 24-28 weeks may be safe when placental markers are normal [fer-mendoza2023].

## Practical bottom line

1. For IVF, default to a standard cycle with no paid extras. Hyaluronan transfer medium with single embryo transfer is the only extra with a reasonable case [fer-eshre2023addons, fer-heymann2020].
2. Decline add-ons rated red: PGT-A for live birth, receptivity testing, IVIG, steroids, PRP. Decline those rated black (time-lapse, PICSI), unless free [fer-hfea2026, fer-hfea2026prp].
3. If his semen is normal, ask for conventional IVF rather than ICSI [fer-dang2021].
4. Use AMH to plan IVF or egg freezing, not to judge whether you can conceive naturally [fer-steiner2017, fer-asrm2020reserve].
5. If freezing eggs, do it younger and aim for 10-15 or more mature eggs; be realistic after 38 [fer-cobo2016, fer-wennberg2019].
6. In pregnancy, take folic acid and, if high-risk, aspirin. Do not take high-dose vitamin C/E [fer-uspstf2023, fer-uspstf2021, fer-poston2006].
7. Test both partners at the same time, and run the 3-month plan together [fer-schlegel2021a, fer-asrm2021female].

## What we don't know

- Whether any add-on helps a small, identifiable subgroup (for example, PGT-A in women over 40, or growth hormone in severe poor responders). Trials big enough to answer this are lacking [fer-iwarsson2026, fer-sood2021].
- Long-term outcomes for children after PGT-A biopsy, AUGMENT or PRP [fer-eshre2023addons, fer-hfea2026prp].
- Whether lowering sperm DNA fragmentation in the dish (microfluidics, MACS) changes live birth, especially with older eggs [fer-garg2026, fer-setti2021].
- Real-world live birth after planned egg freezing in large cohorts of returning users [fer-asrm2021oc].
- Whether any antioxidant strategy, including culture-media antioxidants, helps embryos without the harms seen with oral megadoses [fer-gardner2020, fer-condeagudelo2011].
