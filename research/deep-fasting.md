# Fasting & fasting duration deep dive

This page asks one question that most fasting advice skips: **what actually changes as a fast gets longer, and which of those changes matter for oxidative stress and health in humans?** It covers the hour-by-hour physiology, the oxidative stress biomarker data, every major protocol (time-restricted eating, alternate-day fasting, 5:2, multi-day fasting, the fasting-mimicking diet, Ramadan, and calorie restriction as the comparator), risks, fertility, and interactions. Ketogenic *diets* are covered separately; here ketones appear only as part of the fasting timeline.

**Bottom line.**

1. The physiology of a fast really does change with duration. Liver glycogen carries most of the first day. Fat burning climbs steeply between 18 and 24 h. Ketones stay trivially low (about 0.1 mmol/L) until roughly 20 h, reach the low millimolar range by days 2-3, and keep rising for 2-3 weeks [fst-klein1993, fst-halberg2005, fst-pan2000, fst-owen1969].
2. Those changes have **not** translated into health benefits beyond those of eating less. In trials where calories were matched, fasting protocols did as well as plain calorie restriction but not better: TRE plus CR vs CR alone [fst-liu2022], alternate-day fasting vs daily CR [fst-trepanowski2017, fst-templeman2021], 5:2 vs continuous restriction [fst-harvie2011, fst-sundfor2018], and the Cochrane review [fst-allaf2021].
3. For oxidative stress specifically, the best human evidence is for **sustained calorie restriction**. CALERIE lowered F2-isoprostanes by 13-27% over 2 years [fst-ilyasova2018, fst-redman2018]. Calorie-matched fasting trials found no added drop in oxidative stress markers [fst-wegman2015, fst-peters2025].
4. "Autophagy kicks in at 16/24/48 hours" is a rodent-derived claim. Human autophagy flux has barely been measured, and the few attempts give ambiguous or modest results [fst-alirezaei2010, fst-vendelbo2014, fst-dethlefsen2018, fst-pietrocola2017, fst-bensalem2025].
5. The longer the fast, the more the risks pile up: lean-mass loss, electrolyte problems, uric acid, gallstones, refeeding syndrome after about 5+ days, and suppression of the reproductive axis within 48 h [fst-laurens2021, fst-finnell2018, fst-grundler2024, fst-mehanna2008, fst-cameron1991].

## 1. The physiological timeline of a fast

### 1.1 Fuel switching: glycogen to fat to ketones

The classic studies of human starvation (Cahill and Owen, 1960s) and later isotope work give a consistent picture [fst-cahill2006]:

- **0-12 h (post-absorptive).** Insulin is falling from its post-meal peak, and glucose comes from both liver glycogen and gluconeogenesis. After an overnight fast, gluconeogenesis already supplies about half of glucose output (47% at 14 h) [fst-landau1996]. Beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) is about 0.05-0.06 mmol/L [fst-halberg2005, fst-pan2000].
- **12-24 h.** Liver glycogen is drawn down steadily. Gluconeogenesis supplies 64-67% of glucose by 22 h [fst-rothman1991, fst-landau1996]. Fat mobilisation accelerates: in young men the release of glycerol and palmitate doubled between 12 and 72 h, with 60% of that rise complete by 24 h and the biggest single step between 18 and 24 h [fst-klein1993]. Insulin roughly halves between 12 and 72 h, and 70% of that fall occurs in the first 24 h [fst-klein1993]. The review literature puts the "metabolic switch" at 12-36 h depending on starting glycogen and activity [fst-anton2018]. In lean young men, however, BHB was still only **0.10 mmol/L at 20 h** [fst-halberg2005]. An early time-restricted schedule (last meal 2 pm) raised next-morning BHB to about **0.15 mmol/L** [fst-jamshed2019]. So a 16:8 schedule produces a longer low-insulin period but not meaningful ketosis.
- **24-48 h.** Gluconeogenesis supplies 82% of glucose by about 36 h and 93% by 42 h. Liver glycogen is essentially exhausted [fst-rothman1991, fst-landau1996]. Plasma glucose drifts down by about a quarter, to around 4.1 mmol/L by 72 h [fst-klein1993]. Fatty acids surge; a 10-day fasting metabolomics study proposed falling leptin as one driver of the shift [fst-steinhauser2018].
- **48-72 h.** Ketosis becomes substantial. Brain BHB rose to 0.60 mmol/L after 2 days and 0.98 mmol/L after 3 days. Brain levels ran at about a quarter of plasma levels, which implies plasma BHB of very roughly 2-4 mmol/L by day 3 (our derivation from the reported slope) [fst-pan2000]. Individual variation is large. People without insulinoma completing a supervised 72-h diagnostic fast had a median plasma BHB of about 6 mmol/L [fst-buffet2013]. Exercise speeds ketosis [fst-grundler2024].
- **Days 4-10.** In 1,610 Buchinger fasters taking 75-250 kcal/day, ketonuria was present in over 95% from day 4. Ketosis stayed far below ketoacidosis, and was stronger in younger, heavier and more active people and with fewer carbohydrate calories [fst-grundler2024]. In a 10-day fast, urinary BHB plateaued from day 4 while fatty acids kept rising [fst-laurens2021]. Systemic plasma-proteome changes (over 1,000 proteins) appeared only after about 3 days of a water-only fast [fst-pietzner2024].
- **Weeks.** BHB and acetoacetate keep rising until about day 17 before plateauing [fst-owen1969]. By 5-6 weeks, ketones are the brain's main fuel, which spares muscle protein [fst-owen1967, fst-cahill2006].

### 1.2 Hormones

- **Insulin and glucagon.** Insulin falls fastest in the first day (70% of its 72-h decline by 24 h) [fst-klein1993]. Glucagon rises, and T3 falls, as fasting continues [fst-kerndt1982].
- **Growth hormone.** GH secretion rises about 5-fold by day 2, through more frequent and larger pulses [fst-hartman1992]. On day 5, 24-h integrated GH was about 3 times the fed level [fst-ho1988]. This GH surge is real but transient. It does not mean fasting "builds muscle": net muscle protein release still increases at 72 h [fst-vendelbo2014].
- **IGF-1.** IGF-1 falls with multi-day fasting, to about a quarter of baseline after 10 days in obese men, tracking nitrogen loss [fst-clemmons1981]. Five-day fasting-mimicking cycles lowered IGF-1 by about 22 ng/mL [fst-wei2017]. Long-term calorie restriction does *not* lower IGF-1 in humans unless protein intake also falls [fst-fontana2008]. Lower IGF-1 is therefore mostly a feature of multi-day or low-protein regimens, not of daily TRE.
- **Thyroid and reproductive hormones.** T3 falls with ADF and TRE [fst-stekovic2019, fst-moro2016]. LH and testosterone fall within 48 h [fst-cameron1991] (see section 6).

### 1.3 Autophagy: what is actually known in humans

Autophagy (the recycling of cellular components) is the most over-claimed part of fasting lore. The evidence, by time point:

| Time point | Claim often made | What the evidence actually is | Species | Strength in humans |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12-16 h | "Autophagy starts" | No human flux data. eTRE (last meal 2 pm, about 18 h overnight) raised whole-blood **mRNA** for LC3A and SIRT1 [fst-jamshed2019]. Gene expression is not flux. | Human | Very weak |
| 24-48 h | "Autophagy ramps up / peaks" | Mice: 24-48 h food restriction sharply increased neuronal autophagosomes [fst-alirezaei2010]. Mouse leukocytes showed increased flux at 48 h, but only when a protease inhibitor was injected [fst-pietrocola2017]. | Mouse (mice lose about 20% of body weight in 48 h) | Extrapolated only |
| 36 h | — | Human muscle biopsies at 2-36 h: modest LC3/p62 changes, only in untrained men [fst-dethlefsen2018]. | Human | Weak, modest |
| 72 h | "Deep autophagy" | Human muscle: mTOR signalling halved and LC3B-II rose 30%, but p62 also rose, so flux is uncertain [fst-vendelbo2014]. | Human | Ambiguous |
| up to 4 days | — | Human leukocytes: activated autophagy detectable only in neutrophils, with ex vivo protease inhibition [fst-pietrocola2017]. | Human | Weak |
| 6 months of intermittent fasting plus TRE | — | Blood-cell LC3B-II flux differed from standard care (post hoc, P = 0.04), partly because it *fell* in controls. CR alone was not significant [fst-bensalem2025]. | Human, n = 121 | Preliminary |

Reviews agree that fasting is a potent autophagy inducer in cells and animals, but human data are scarce [fst-bagherniya2018]. A 48-h mouse fast is metabolically far more severe than a 48-h human fast [fst-pietrocola2017]. **Honest summary:** fasting probably increases autophagy in some human tissues after a day or more, but there is no validated human dose-response, no evidence that a particular hour count matters, and no evidence that this translates into health benefits independent of energy deficit.

## 3. Oxidative stress during and after fasting

### 3.1 Acute changes

Popular claims that fasting causes a "hormetic ROS burst" rest on animal and cell work. We found no good human data showing a transient rise in validated oxidative damage markers during a fast. What exists:

- **Ramadan.** Urinary 15-F2t-isoprostane *rose* by week 3 of Ramadan in 50 healthy adults, but weight and body fat also rose, and the authors attributed the change to weight gain [fst-faris2012]. Another small cohort found a slight fall in erythrocyte MDA and no change in plasma protein carbonyls, glutathione, GPx or catalase [fst-ibrahim2008].
- **Multi-day fasts.** A 10-day Buchinger fast (n = 109) lowered plasma TBARS and raised total antioxidant capacity (TAC). Erythrocyte GSH, glutathione reductase, GPx and catalase did not change [fst-wilhelmidetoledo2020]. An 11-day water fast (n = 10) also lowered TBARS, but lowered alpha-tocopherol, raised uric acid and creatinine, and reduced GFR [fst-mojto2018]. Two cautions apply. Uric acid rises substantially during fasting [fst-grundler2024, fst-wilhelmidetoledo2020], and because urate is a major plasma antioxidant, TAC increases partly reflect it (our inference). TBARS is a non-specific assay. Neither study had a control group.

### 3.2 Adaptive responses (Nrf2, sirtuins, mitochondria)

- Ramadan raised blood-cell expression of Nrf2 (+412%), SOD2 (+54%) and TFAM (+90%), while SIRT3 *fell* 62% [fst-madkour2019]. These are mRNA changes without damage endpoints.
- Weight-stable alternate-day fasting for 3 weeks raised SIRT3 expression by 2.7% and changed no oxidative stress marker [fst-wegman2015].
- eTRE raised whole-blood SIRT1 expression [fst-jamshed2019].
- Mechanistically, BHB is an endogenous class I HDAC inhibitor. In mice it induces FOXO3A and metallothionein-2 and protects against oxidative stress [fst-shimazu2013]. The relevant BHB concentrations (above about 1 mmol/L) are reached only after about 2 days of fasting (section 1.1), not with daily TRE.
- Blood metabolomics in 4 people fasting 34-58 h showed rises in pentose-phosphate and TCA-cycle metabolites, which the authors read as greater NADPH and antioxidant demand [fst-teruya2019]. Reviews frame such adaptive stress responses as central to fasting's benefits [fst-decabo2019]. This is suggestive but indirect.

### 3.3 Biomarker results in human trials

| Study | Protocol | Calories matched? | Marker (method) | Result |

Pooled evidence: across 99 RCTs, every fasting strategy and continuous restriction beat ad libitum eating, and only ADF edged out continuous restriction (-1.29 kg, a trivial difference) [fst-semnaniazad2025]. An umbrella review rated most IF associations as low or very-low quality, with modified ADF for BMI the only high-quality signal, and linked IF to fat-free mass loss [fst-patikorn2021]. Even in people with metabolic syndrome, adding personalised 8-10 h TRE to standard care for 3 months lowered HbA1c by only 0.10% [fst-manoogian2024].

**What the comparator achieves.** Two years of CALERIE calorie restriction did more on every axis: LDL, blood pressure, CRP and insulin resistance [fst-kraus2019], urinary F2-isoprostanes [fst-ilyasova2018], and a small slowing of the DunedinPACE methylation pace-of-aging measure (other epigenetic clocks unchanged) [fst-waziry2023]. The fasting-mimicking diet's mechanistic case rests largely on mouse work, where bimonthly 4-day cycles extended lifespan and lowered IGF-1 [fst-brandhorst2015].

**Ramadan as a natural experiment.** It combines fasting with circadian disruption, nocturnal eating and about 1 h less sleep [fst-faris2020sleep]. Weight and fat losses are small and transient [fst-fernando2019]. Testosterone, LH, FSH and thyroid hormones did not change on average [fst-poursalehian2024]. Because it tests daytime abstinence plus night eating, not a health-optimised protocol, it says little about TRE.

## 5. The isocaloric question: does fasting or its timing add anything beyond calorie restriction?

**Short answer: for weight, cardiometabolic risk factors and oxidative stress markers, essentially no. For short-term insulin sensitivity, possibly a little with early eating windows, but the best recent calorie-matched trial says no.**

Evidence that fasting does **not** add anything when calories are equal:

- **Liu 2022 (NEJM).** 12 months, n = 139. Both groups had identical calorie targets; 8-h TRE plus CR vs CR alone gave -8.0 vs -6.3 kg (difference -1.8 kg, P = 0.11), with no differences in fat, lean mass, blood pressure, lipids or glucose [fst-liu2022].
- **Templeman 2021.** The cleanest mechanistic design. In lean adults, 24-h alternate-day fasts with a matched deficit lost *less* fat than daily 25% restriction (-0.74 vs -1.75 kg). Fasting without any deficit (200% intake on feed days) changed nothing, and there was no fasting-specific effect on postprandial metabolism or adipose gene expression [fst-templeman2021].
- **Trepanowski 2017.** Over 1 year, ADF matched daily CR for weight and cardioprotective markers, with more dropouts [fst-trepanowski2017].
- **5:2 trials** were equivalent to continuous restriction for weight and HbA1c [fst-harvie2011, fst-sundfor2018, fst-carter2018].
- **Peters 2025 (ChronoFast).** Intended-isocaloric early (8:00-16:00) vs late (13:00-21:00) TRE in 31 women: no change in insulin sensitivity, 24-h glucose, lipids, inflammation or oxidative stress, despite shifting circadian phase [fst-peters2025].
- **Wegman 2015.** Weight-stable ADF did not change oxidative stress markers [fst-wegman2015].
- **Cochrane 2021:** no clinically meaningful difference between IF and continuous restriction [fst-allaf2021].

Evidence that timing **might** add something:

- **Sutton 2018.** 6-h eTRF improved insulin sensitivity, blood pressure and 8-isoprostane without weight loss, but n = 8 [fst-sutton2018].
- **Jones 2020.** 2 weeks of eTRF improved whole-body insulin sensitivity and muscle glucose uptake vs a control matched for the same deficit and weight loss (n = 16) [fst-jones2020].
- **Xie 2022.** Early TRE beat mid-day TRE for insulin sensitivity, but calories were not fixed [fst-xie2022].
- **Jamshed 2022.** eTRE plus energy restriction lost 2.3 kg more than energy restriction with a 12-h-plus window, which the authors equate to about 214 kcal/day extra restriction. That is best read as an adherence or appetite effect, not metabolic magic [fst-jamshed2022]. Supporting this, eTRF lowers hunger and ghrelin without changing energy expenditure [fst-ravussin2019].

**Interpretation.** Fasting protocols are *behavioural tools for creating a deficit*. Some people find "don't eat after 3 pm" or "two hard days a week" easier than counting calories, and that is a legitimate reason to use them. If there is a timing-specific effect, it is small, early-window-specific, short-term, and not yet shown on oxidative stress with adequate samples.

## 6. Risks and who should not fast

**Lean and muscle mass.** 16:8 TRE without resistance training reduced appendicular lean mass more than three meals a day [fst-lowe2020]. 6-h TRE lost 1.5 kg of lean mass in 8 weeks [fst-cienfuegos2020]. Over a 10-day fast, about 60% of the 5.9 kg lost was lean soft tissue. Most of that was water and glycogen, but 42% was metabolically active tissue, and BMR fell 12% [fst-laurens2021]. Two years of CR reduced fat-free mass by 2.2 kg and bone density at spine and hip [fst-villareal2016]. Resistance training with adequate protein prevents most of this during TRE [fst-moro2016, fst-tinsley2019].

**Refeeding syndrome.** Under NICE criteria, someone is high-risk with little or no intake for more than 10 days (alone), or more than 5 days plus another risk factor such as low BMI, recent weight loss, alcohol misuse, or insulin or diuretic use [fst-mehanna2008]. It occurs mostly within 72 h of restarting food [fst-friedli2017]. Supervised clinics refeed gradually over at least half the fast length [fst-finnell2018].

**Electrolytes and circulation.** Early fasting weight loss is largely sodium and water. Postural hypotension and arrhythmias are recognised complications [fst-kerndt1982]. In 768 supervised water-only fasts, most adverse events were mild (fatigue, insomnia, nausea, headache), but 212 visits had a grade 3 event, one 70-year-old developed grade 4 hyponatraemia on day 9, and another person needed IV fluids for dehydration [fst-finnell2018]. A Buchinger cohort reported adverse effects in under 1%, but the authors are clinic-affiliated and there was no control group [fst-wilhelmidetoledo2019].

**Uric acid and gout.** Uric acid rose by about 100-200 umol/L in Buchinger fasts, more with stronger ketosis [fst-grundler2024]. Water-only fasting caused hyperuricaemia and a small, reversible fall in GFR [fst-mojto2018]. Gout and urate stones are classic complications of prolonged fasting [fst-kerndt1982], although one 10-day cohort reported no gout attacks [fst-wilhelmidetoledo2020].

**Gallstones.** In a 10-year cohort of 4,730 women, longer overnight fasting periods and dieting both predicted gallstone hospitalisation [fst-sichieri1991]. Rapid weight loss on 520 kcal/day produced gallstones in 10.9% within 16 weeks [fst-yang1992].

**Hypoglycaemia with medication.** Two low-calorie days a week roughly doubled hypoglycaemia (RR 2.05) in people with type 2 diabetes on glucose-lowering drugs, despite dose reductions [fst-corley2018]. Medication protocols exist for this [fst-carter2018, fst-hassanein2017]. Insulin and sulfonylurea users should fast only with a prescriber's plan.

**Eating disorders.** Almost half of Canadian young people reported trying IF in the past year, and it was associated with eating-disorder behaviours and psychopathology, most consistently in women [fst-ganson2022]. Anyone with a history of restrictive eating should avoid fasting protocols.

**The 2024 AHA abstract on 8-h TRE and cardiovascular death (published 2025).** In NHANES (n = 19,831), eating within less than 8 h on two 24-h dietary recalls was associated with higher cardiovascular mortality (HR 2.35, 95% CI 1.39-3.98) vs 12-14 h, but not with all-cause or cancer mortality. The all-cause result did not survive sensitivity analyses [fst-chen2025]. Limits, from the full paper [fst-chen2025]:

- Eating duration came from only two days of dietary recall.
- The under-8 h group was about 2% of the sample, with roughly 33 cardiovascular deaths in the main analysis.
- That group was younger, more often male, a current smoker and food-insecure, had lower income, energy intake and diet quality, and 38% always skipped breakfast. In other words, this was mostly real-world meal-skipping, not deliberate TRE.
- When the under-8 h window was required on *both* recall days, the HR fell to 1.78 (0.85-3.74, not significant; 8 events).
- The group also had 2.7 kg less lean mass on DXA.
- The authors themselves flag residual confounding. RCTs of TRE show neutral-to-favourable effects on blood pressure and lipids [fst-liu2022, fst-jamshed2022]. **Grade: very low certainty of harm.** It does not justify alarm, but it is a reason not to claim cardiovascular *benefit* either.

**Who should not fast (beyond an ordinary overnight fast) without medical supervision:** pregnant or breastfeeding people; those with a current or past eating disorder; underweight people (BMI under 18.5); type 1 diabetes; insulin or sulfonylurea users; people with gout, kidney stones, or significant kidney or heart disease; frail older adults with sarcopenia; and children and adolescents. Multi-day fasts should be supervised for anyone.

## 7. Fertility

### 7.1 Men and sperm

- **Acute fasting suppresses the axis.** 48 h of fasting cut mean LH from 2.94 to 1.07 IU/L, halved LH pulse frequency, and lowered FSH and testosterone in 8 healthy men [fst-cameron1991].
- **TRE in trained men.** 8 weeks of isocaloric 16:8 lowered testosterone, IGF-1 and T3 while preserving muscle and strength [fst-moro2016]. A review of human trials concluded IF lowers testosterone in lean, active young men but not SHBG [fst-cienfuegos2022].
- **Ramadan.** A meta-analysis of 35 studies found no average change in testosterone, LH or FSH [fst-poursalehian2024]. Semen data are thin. A 97-man retrospective IVF cohort found lower post-gradient progressive motility during Ramadan (30% vs 38%) and lower volume [fst-engel2023]. A 1986 study of 21 men found no harm, with mixed changes [fst-abbas1986].
- **Fasting-mimicking diet.** Three 5-day cycles in 18 men with poor semen quality showed no clear difference from controls [fst-may2024].
- **Grade:** limited. **No human evidence that fasting improves sperm.** Multi-day fasts plausibly harm it transiently, through LH and testosterone suppression. For men with obesity, weight loss by any route helps; see [weight-loss-sperm].

### 7.2 Women: ovulation, cycles and hypothalamic suppression

- **Energy availability threshold.** In regularly menstruating women, LH pulsatility was intact at 30 kcal/kg lean mass/day but disrupted below it [fst-loucks2003]. This is the physiology behind Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport [fst-mountjoy2023].
- **72-h fasts.** In normal-weight women, a mid-follicular 3-day fast reduced LH pulses but did not affect follicle growth, ovulation or cycle length [fst-olson1995]. In lean women (20% body fat or less), the same fast made 2 of 7 cycles anovulatory and lengthened follicular phases [fst-alvero1998]. **Leanness predicts vulnerability.**
- **TRE and IF in women with obesity** lower testosterone and free androgen index and raise SHBG, without changing oestrogen or gonadotropins [fst-cienfuegos2022].

### 7.3 PCOS

- A single-arm 5-week 8-h TRE study (n = 15 completers) lowered testosterone, free androgen index, insulin and hsCRP, raised SHBG, and improved menstrual regularity in 11 of 15 women [fst-li2021].
- The only RCT comparing early 14:10 TRE with daily calorie restriction (n = 90) found **no difference** in weight, menstrual, androgen or metabolic outcomes [fst-talebi2024].
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 5 studies found IF lowered weight (-4.25 kg), HOMA-IR, free androgen index and DHEA-S and raised SHBG; total testosterone and AMH were unchanged [fst-ranneh2025].
- **Grade:** limited. TRE is a reasonable way to lose weight with PCOS (see [pcos]), with benefits that track weight and insulin, not fasting per se.

### 7.4 Trying to conceive and pregnancy

- **Pregnancy physiology.** Late pregnancy shows "accelerated starvation": extending the overnight fast from 12 to 18 h ("skipped breakfast") raised fatty acids and BHB and lowered glucose, while non-pregnant women barely changed [fst-metzger1982]. Higher third-trimester maternal BHB correlated with lower offspring IQ at ages 2-5 (r about -0.2, observational) [fst-rizzo1991].
- **Ramadan in pregnancy.** A meta-analysis of 22 studies (31,374 pregnancies) found no effect on birth weight or preterm delivery, with lower placental weight driven by one study [fst-glazier2018]. An umbrella review judged the evidence low quality and inconclusive [fst-altaiar2025].
- **Trying to conceive.** No trials. By inference from the energy-availability and lean-woman data [fst-loucks2003, fst-alvero1998], deliberate energy deficit and multi-day fasting are unwise for lean women trying to conceive. TRE that maintains energy intake is unlikely to matter. For women with obesity or PCOS, modest weight loss by any method is supported (see [egg-weight-loss]).
- **Grade:** pregnancy, limited and observational: avoid fasting beyond the overnight period. Preconception, mechanistic or limited.

## 8. Interactions

**Fasting and exercise.**

- Pre-exercise feeding improves prolonged endurance performance but not shorter efforts, while fasted exercise increases fat mobilisation and muscle signalling linked to mitochondrial adaptation [fst-aird2018].
- In men with overweight, 6 weeks of cycling before rather than after carbohydrate improved insulin sensitivity [fst-edinburgh2020].
- Resistance training preserves lean mass during TRE in men and women [fst-moro2016, fst-tinsley2019]. It is the single best countermeasure to the lean-loss risk.
- Exercise during prolonged fasting accelerates ketosis [fst-grundler2024].
- Heavy training plus fasting in lean women risks low energy availability [fst-loucks2003, fst-mountjoy2023]; see [vigorous-exercise-low-energy].
- Combining fasting with [aerobic-exercise-moderate] is reasonable.

**Fasting and Nrf2 activators (sulforaphane, other phytochemicals). Inference only.** There are no human trials combining fasting with sulforaphane or other Nrf2 activators. Fasting raised Nrf2 mRNA in one Ramadan cohort [fst-madkour2019], and sulforaphane activates Nrf2 directly (see [broccoli-sprouts], [sulforaphane-supplements]). Pathway saturation makes large synergy unlikely, but there is no reason to expect antagonism either. Taking broccoli sprouts within an eating window is fine. Unlike exercise adaptation, there is no evidence that antioxidant vitamins blunt fasting effects: in the weight-stable ADF trial, neither the fasting arm nor the vitamin C plus E arm changed oxidative stress markers [fst-wegman2015]. High-dose antioxidant cocktails remain unnecessary (see [high-dose-antioxidant-cocktail], [antioxidant-megadoses-during-training]).

**Fasting and sleep.**

- Ramadan reduced total sleep time by about 1 h/night (7.2 to 6.4 h) [fst-faris2020sleep]. This is a confound in every Ramadan biomarker study; see [short-sleep].
- Daytime 8-h TRE, whether early, late or self-selected, did not change sleep, mood or quality of life over 12 weeks [fst-claverojimeno2025].
- Late TRE delayed sleep midpoint by about 15 minutes relative to early TRE [fst-peters2025].
- Practical point: windows that end in the afternoon or early evening fit circadian physiology best and avoid late-night eating. This matters most for shift workers ([shift-work-circadian]).

## 9. Practical guide

1. **Start with the deficit, not the clock.** If you need to lose fat, choose the pattern you can sustain: 8-10 h TRE, 5:2, ADF, or plain calorie restriction. They work about equally [fst-semnaniazad2025, fst-allaf2021]. If you are not carrying excess fat, there is little evidence any fasting pattern lowers oxidative stress.
2. **If you use TRE,** an early window is the best-supported variant (the trials ended eating at 14:00-16:00; a window ending in the early evening is a practical compromise) [fst-sutton2018, fst-xie2022, fst-jamshed2022]. Expect ketones of only about 0.1-0.15 mmol/L, not "ketosis" [fst-halberg2005, fst-jamshed2019].
3. **Protect muscle:** resistance train 2-3 times a week and get about 1.6 g protein per kg body weight per day, as in the trials that preserved lean mass [fst-tinsley2019, fst-moro2016]. Avoid windows under 6 h [fst-cienfuegos2020].
4. **24-36 h fasts** (ADF or occasional) are safe for healthy adults not on glucose-lowering drugs, but offer no proven benefit over the same weekly deficit spread out [fst-templeman2021, fst-trepanowski2017].
5. **Multi-day fasts (3+ days):** treat them as a medical procedure. Get supervision, especially beyond 5 days, monitor electrolytes, refeed gradually, and avoid them with gout, diabetes medication, low BMI, pregnancy or an eating-disorder history [fst-mehanna2008, fst-finnell2018, fst-grundler2024].
6. **Fertility:** men preparing semen samples or trying to conceive should skip multi-day fasts for about 3 months [fst-cameron1991]. Lean women trying to conceive should avoid energy deficits [fst-alvero1998, fst-loucks2003]. Do not fast beyond the overnight period during pregnancy [fst-metzger1982].
7. **For oxidative stress,** the evidence-backed hierarchy is unchanged: lose excess fat ([weight-loss]), keep blood sugar controlled ([glycemic-control]), eat a [mediterranean-diet]-style pattern, exercise, and sleep. Fasting is one optional route to the first.

## 10. What we don't know

- Whether **any** fasting-specific effect on oxidative damage exists in humans once calories are matched. Only one tiny positive trial exists [fst-sutton2018], against two nulls [fst-wegman2015, fst-peters2025]. No trial has used mass-spectrometry F2-isoprostanes with calorie-matched fasting arms.
- The **human autophagy dose-response**: validated flux assays are only now emerging [fst-bensalem2025, fst-pietrocola2017].
- Whether periodic multi-day fasting or FMD cycles have **lasting** effects. Buchinger data are uncontrolled [fst-wilhelmidetoledo2019], and FMD trials are small and commercially linked [fst-wei2017, fst-brandhorst2024].
- **Hard outcomes**: no RCT of any fasting regimen reports mortality or cardiovascular events [fst-allaf2021]. The observational TRE-mortality signal is unresolved [fst-chen2025].
- **Fertility**: no adequately powered trial of fasting on semen quality, ovulation, time to pregnancy or IVF outcomes.
- **Lean-mass quality** over years of TRE in older adults, and the long-term bone effects of combining fasting with CR [fst-villareal2016].
