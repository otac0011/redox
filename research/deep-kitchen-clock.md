# Cooking chemistry, light & meal timing

This page covers two kinds of everyday "timing and technique" choices that shape oxidative and metabolic stress: **how you cook** (what heat does to meat, starch, oils and vegetables) and **when you are exposed to light and food** relative to your body clock. The diet page already gives short notes on high-temperature cooking and frying oils; this deep dive replaces those entries with fuller versions and adds new ones.

## Key takeaways

1. **Charring matters more than cooking per se.** Grilled and pan-fried meat cooked until well done forms heterocyclic amines (HCAs) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). People who eat the most of them have roughly 15-20% higher odds of colorectal adenoma in meta-analyses [kcc-martinezgongora2019, kcc-chiavarini2017], but a large prospective cohort found nothing [kcc-ollberding2012]. Simple fixes cut HCAs several-fold in the pan: lower heat with frequent turning [kcc-salmon2000], microwave pre-cooking [kcc-felton1994] and marinades [kcc-smith2008, kcc-salmon1997].
2. **Acrylamide is a regulatory concern but an epidemiological shrug.** EFSA judged that current intakes raise a concern for cancer, based on animal data [kcc-efsa2015, kcc-efsa2022]. Human studies are mostly null [kcc-pelucchi2015, kcc-filippini2022], with small signals for endometrial and ovarian cancer in never-smokers [kcc-hogervorst2007, kcc-adani2020].
3. **Dietary AGEs are a live controversy.** One research group reports big benefits from low-AGE diets [kcc-vlassara2016, kcc-uribarri2011]. Independent, more rigorous trials mostly found no effect [kcc-semba2014, kcc-linkens2022].
4. **"Seed oils are toxic" is not supported.** Higher linoleic acid intake and blood levels are linked to *lower* cardiovascular disease and mortality [kcc-li2020, kcc-marklund2019]. The real cooking issue is narrower: polyunsaturated oils oxidize faster under repeated high heat [kcc-casal2010, kcc-guillen2009], so choose stable oils for frying and don't reuse oil until it is degraded.
5. **Light is a drug for the clock.** Ordinary room light before bed delayed melatonin onset in 99% of people [kcc-gooley2011]. Half-maximal suppression happens at only ~25 lux [kcc-phillips2019]. Bright days and dark nights are linked to lower diabetes and mortality risk [kcc-windred2024a, kcc-windred2024b].
6. **Late meals meet a less glucose-tolerant body.** Eating near bedtime, when melatonin is rising, raises glucose more [kcc-garaulet2022, kcc-gu2020]. Misaligning sleep and meals by 12 hours raises glucose, blood pressure and inflammation within days [kcc-scheer2009, kcc-morris2016].
7. **Direct evidence that light or meal timing changes oxidative-stress markers in humans is thin.** It is mostly cross-sectional data from shift workers [kcc-teixeira2019, kcc-bhatti2017]. The metabolic evidence is much stronger than the redox evidence.

# Part 1: Cooking chemistry

## 1. Heterocyclic amines and PAHs: doneness and char

**Chemistry.** Muscle contains creatine, free amino acids and sugars. Above about 150 °C these react to form HCAs such as PhIP and MeIQx, especially on the surface of well-done meat. PAHs such as benzo(a)pyrene form mainly when fat drips onto flames or coals and the smoke coats the food [kcc-cross2004, kcc-duedahlolesen2022]. Both need metabolic activation, by CYP1A2 for HCAs and CYP1A1/1B1 for PAHs, before they damage DNA. PAH quinones also redox-cycle and generate reactive oxygen species. IARC's classification of processed and red meat noted that high-temperature cooking produces these compounds [kcc-bouvard2015].

**Human evidence.**

| Study | Design | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Sinha 1999 [kcc-sinha1999] | Case-control, 374 people | +29% adenoma odds per 10 g/day of well-done red meat; rare/medium +10% (NS) |
| Martínez Góngora 2019 [kcc-martinezgongora2019] | Meta-analysis, 12 studies | Top vs bottom PhIP: adenoma OR 1.20; MeIQx 1.20; benzo(a)pyrene 1.15 |
| Chiavarini 2017 [kcc-chiavarini2017] | Meta-analysis, 39 studies | Adenoma and cancer ORs 1.10-1.20; high heterogeneity |
| Cross 2010, NIH-AARP [kcc-cross2010] | Cohort, 300,948 | MeIQx HR 1.19, DiMeIQx 1.17 for colorectal cancer |
| Ollberding 2012, Multiethnic Cohort [kcc-ollberding2012] | Cohort, 165,717 | No association for HCAs (RR 0.90) or meat |

Most of the positive data come from case-control studies, which are prone to recall bias. Exposure is estimated from questions about doneness and cooking method. Sugimura, who discovered many of these compounds, noted that HCA levels in ordinary meals are too low to explain human cancer on their own [kcc-sugimura2004].

## 2. What you can do in the pan

| Technique | Effect on HCAs/PAHs | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Turn burgers every minute at lower pan heat | Much lower HCAs, still reaching the 70 °C that kills *E. coli*, with little extra time | [kcc-salmon2000] |
| Microwave patties 1-3 min first, discard juice | Precursors down up to 30%; HCAs down 3- to 9-fold | [kcc-felton1994] |
| Herb/spice marinade, 1 h (steak, 204 °C) | Total HCAs down 57-88% | [kcc-smith2008] |
| Vinegar-lemon-garlic-mustard marinade (chicken) | PhIP down 92-99%, but MeIQx up >10-fold at 30-40 min | [kcc-salmon1997] |
| Garlic and onion in oil marinade (patties) | MeIQx down up to ~70%; lemon juice had little effect | [kcc-gibis2007] |
| Black-beer marinade (charcoal-grilled pork) | EFSA PAH8 down 53% | [kcc-viegas2014] |
| Remove char, lean cuts, less fat dripping | Lower PAHs | [kcc-duedahlolesen2022] |

The marinade results come from food chemistry. No trial has shown that people who marinate have less cancer or lower oxidative damage. The sugary chicken marinade is a reminder that recipes matter: sugar feeds Maillard chemistry over long cooking times [kcc-salmon1997]. See [marinating-meat] and [high-temperature-cooking].

## 3. Acrylamide

**What it is.** Acrylamide forms when the amino acid asparagine reacts with sugars in dry, starchy foods heated above roughly 120 °C. The main sources are fries, crisps, coffee, biscuits, crispbread and toast. The body converts it to glycidamide, which binds DNA. Acrylamide also depletes glutathione and generates ROS [kcc-efsa2022].

**The regulators' view.** EFSA estimated average adult exposure at 0.4-0.9 µg/kg body weight per day (95th percentile 0.6-2.0). Because the margins of exposure to animal tumour doses are small for a genotoxic carcinogen, EFSA concluded that acrylamide is a concern for cancer [kcc-efsa2015]. A 2022 statement confirmed acrylamide's genotoxicity, including glycidamide DNA adducts in humans, and left those conclusions unchanged [kcc-efsa2022].

**The epidemiology.**

- Pooled studies: no association with most cancers. Kidney cancer was borderline (RR 1.20). Endometrial (1.23) and ovarian (1.39) risks were raised only in never-smokers, with confidence intervals touching 1 [kcc-pelucchi2015]. A 2022 dose-response analysis found no association with any non-gynaecological cancer [kcc-filippini2022].
- The Netherlands Cohort Study found ovarian cancer HR 1.78 overall. Among never-smokers it found endometrial HR 1.99 and ovarian HR 2.22 [kcc-hogervorst2007]. A 2020 meta-analysis backed small gynaecological risks [kcc-adani2020].
- Biomarker studies give mixed results. Blood adducts were linked to ER-positive breast cancer in Denmark [kcc-olesen2008] but not to ovarian cancer in EPIC [kcc-obonsantacana2016].
- Measurement is a big problem. Questionnaire estimates correlate with blood adducts at only r ≈ 0.3 [kcc-wilson2009], which can hide real effects.
- Pregnancy: higher cord-blood adducts were linked to 132 g lower birth weight [kcc-pedersen2012].

**Reducing it.** Cook starchy foods to golden rather than brown, and soak or blanch cut potatoes before frying. In one study, pretreatment soaks cut acrylamide 80-90% in deep-fried potatoes, and air-frying cut it about 90% compared with deep-frying [kcc-sansano2015]. Air-fried roots and tubers still carry measurable acrylamide [kcc-yoon2024]. See [acrylamide].

## 4. Advanced glycation end products (AGEs)

AGEs such as carboxymethyl-lysine (CML) form in food during dry, high-heat cooking and also inside the body from blood sugar. The widely used ELISA-based food database showed that dry heat raises food AGEs 10-100-fold, and that moist heat, acid and shorter cooking times reduce them [kcc-uribarri2010]. A mass-spectrometry database ranked crusty grain and nut products and canned meats highest, and fruit, vegetables and coffee lowest [kcc-scheijen2016].

| Trial | People | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Negrean 2007 [kcc-negrean2007] | 20, type 2 diabetes | High-AGE meal: FMD down 36% vs 21% |
| Uribarri 2011 [kcc-uribarri2011] | 36 | 4 months: lower insulin, TNF-alpha, 8-isoprostanes |
| Vlassara 2016 [kcc-vlassara2016] | 138, metabolic syndrome | 1 year: HOMA-IR 3.1 → 1.9 |
| de Courten 2016 [kcc-decourten2016] | 20 overweight, double-blind | 2 weeks: insulin sensitivity better on low-AGE |
| Semba 2014 [kcc-semba2014] | 24 older adults | 6 weeks: no effect on endothelial function or inflammation |
| Baye 2017 [kcc-baye2017] | 20 overweight | 2 weeks: no effect on BP, lipids or inflammation |
| Linkens 2022 [kcc-linkens2022] | 73 abdominally obese | 4 weeks, 2.7-5.3-fold AGE difference: no effect on glucose, vessels or inflammation |

Reviews call the trial evidence low quality [kcc-kellow2013, kcc-clarke2016]. In the Maastricht Study, habitual intake measured by mass spectrometry was not linked to microvascular function or diabetes [kcc-linkens2022b, kcc-linkens2023]. A fair reading: dietary AGEs are partly absorbed, but in people without kidney disease the case that they drive systemic oxidative stress is unproven. Your own blood sugar ([glycemic-control]) is the larger AGE source.

## 5. Cooking oils: oxidative stability vs smoke point

**Smoke point is the wrong yardstick.** Smoke point mainly reflects free fatty acids and refining. What matters for health is how fast an oil's fats oxidize into hydroperoxides and then aldehydes such as 4-hydroxynonenal. Polyunsaturated fats oxidize far faster than monounsaturated oleic acid.

- **Olive oil holds up under frying.** In domestic deep-frying, olive oils, extra-virgin included, lasted 24-27 h before reaching the 25% polar-compound legal limit. A vitamin-E-rich vegetable oil blend lasted 15 h [kcc-casal2010]. Heating EVOO at 180 °C for 36 h degraded phenols and tocopherols but left oleic acid, sterols and squalene intact [kcc-allouche2007]. Cooking vegetables in EVOO even transferred olive phenols into them [kcc-ramirezanaya2015].
- **PUFA-rich oils form more aldehydes.** Sunflower oil at 190 °C produced genotoxic 4-hydroxy-alkenals early, well before reaching the polar-compound limit [kcc-guillen2009]. PUFA-rich oils were the most heat-sensitive and coconut oil the least in NMR studies [kcc-ampem2023]. Some of these aldehydes survive digestion [kcc-goicoechea2008]. One reviewer argues that PUFA-rich oils should not be recommended for high-heat frying at all [kcc-grootveld2022].
- **A note on a popular study.** A widely shared 2018 industry-linked comparison of oils under heating ("De Alzaa 2018") appeared in a journal not indexed in PubMed. We do not use it as evidence. Its headline, that EVOO is stable under heat, agrees with the indexed studies above.
- **Does it matter clinically?** The Spanish EPIC cohort found no difference in heart disease whether people fried in olive or sunflower oil [kcc-guallarcastillon2012].

See [cooking-oil-choice].

## 6. Seed oils and linoleic acid

Popular claims say that seed oils (soybean, corn, sunflower, canola) are "toxic", inflammatory or a cause of chronic disease. The evidence:

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| 44 cohorts, 811,069 people [kcc-li2020] | Highest vs lowest LA intake: total mortality RR 0.87, CVD mortality 0.87, cancer mortality 0.89 |
| 30 biomarker cohorts [kcc-marklund2019] | Higher blood LA: CVD mortality HR 0.78 per interquintile range |
| 13 cohorts [kcc-farvid2014] | High LA: 15% fewer CHD events, 21% fewer CHD deaths |
| 8 RCTs [kcc-mozaffarian2010] | PUFA replacing saturated fat: CHD RR 0.81 |
| 15 RCTs in healthy people [kcc-johnson2012] | Adding LA did not raise inflammatory markers |
| Cochrane omega-6 [kcc-hooper2018] | Little or no effect on mortality (RR 1.00) or CVD events; low-quality evidence |
| Cochrane saturated fat [kcc-hooper2020] | Cutting saturated fat: CVD events RR 0.83 |
| Sydney Diet Heart, recovered data [kcc-ramsden2013] | Safflower-oil group: more deaths (HR 1.62) |
| Minnesota Coronary Experiment, recovered data [kcc-ramsden2016] | Lower cholesterol, no mortality benefit |

**Mechanistic worry.** LDL from linoleate-rich diets oxidizes more readily in a test tube than LDL from oleate-rich diets [kcc-reaven1993]. Cutting dietary LA lowers circulating oxidized LA metabolites [kcc-ramsden2012]. Neither has been shown to predict disease. The "oxidized linoleic acid hypothesis" has been argued in an opinion piece whose authors disclose commercial interests [kcc-dinicolantonio2018].

**Bottom line.** As a dietary fat replacing butter or lard, seed oils look neutral to beneficial. The legitimate concern is repeated high-heat use of the most polyunsaturated oils (section 7). See [seed-oils-linoleic-acid].

## 7. Deep-frying, oil reuse and fried food

Frying oil degrades by hydrolysis, oxidation and polymerisation. Higher temperatures, more frying cycles, more free fatty acids, trace metals and more unsaturation all speed this up [kcc-choe2007]. The legal discard limit used in a Portuguese frying study was 25% total polar compounds [kcc-casal2010]. Animals fed repeatedly heated oil develop higher blood pressure and vascular inflammation [kcc-ng2014].

In people, a meta-analysis found 28% more major cardiovascular events and 22% more coronary heart disease with the highest fried-food intake, with no rise in mortality and high heterogeneity [kcc-qin2021]. In older US women, one or more servings a day was linked to 8% higher all-cause mortality; fried chicken and fried fish drove most of it [kcc-sun2019]. In US nurses and health professionals, eating fried food 7 or more times a week was linked to more diabetes (RR 1.55) and coronary disease (RR 1.21), but body weight, blood pressure and cholesterol explained most of it [kcc-cahill2014]. Spain, where people fry at home in olive or sunflower oil, showed no association [kcc-guallarcastillon2012]. That contrast suggests that heavily reused fast-food oil and the foods fried in it matter more than frying itself. See [oxidized-frying-oils].

**Air fryers.** Air fryers use little oil and, in one study, cut acrylamide in potatoes by about 90% compared with deep-frying [kcc-sansano2015]. They do not eliminate acrylamide, HCAs or PAHs [kcc-yoon2024]. We found no human outcome data on air-fryer use.

## 8. Vegetables: boiling, steaming, frying

| Nutrient | Best methods | Worst | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glucosinolates (broccoli, cabbage) | Steaming, microwaving (one study also stir-frying) | Boiling (~90% of losses leach into water) | [kcc-song2007, kcc-yuan2009] |
| Sulforaphane yield | Raw or very lightly cooked (myrosinase intact) | Fully cooked: ~3x lower | [kcc-conaway2000] |
| Vitamin C | Microwaving, steaming | Boiling | [kcc-lee2018, kcc-yuan2009] |
| Carotenoids, lycopene | Cooking, often with oil, *raises* availability | | [kcc-miglio2008, kcc-dewanto2002, kcc-palermo2014] |
| Phenols | Steaming; sautéing in EVOO adds olive phenols | Boiling | [kcc-palermo2014, kcc-ramirezanaya2015] |

The two broccoli studies disagree about stir-frying. One found no significant glucosinolate loss [kcc-song2007]; the other found the biggest losses with stir-frying [kcc-yuan2009]. This probably reflects differences in time, temperature and added water. For sulforaphane tricks such as adding mustard powder, see [broccoli-mature] and [mustard-seed-myrosinase]. Cooked tomato with oil is covered under [tomatoes-lycopene].

## 9. Cookware (brief)

- **Cast iron** adds iron to food. In low- and middle-income countries, iron pots or ingots raised haemoglobin in about half of studies, mostly in children [kcc-alves2019]. This helps people with iron deficiency. It is not useful, and possibly unwanted, for people with high iron stores; see [iron-excess].
- **Non-stick (PTFE)** pans release fumes when overheated, and older coatings were made with PFOA. Replacement chemicals such as GenX are also under suspicion, and human data are limited [kcc-sajid2017]. Don't preheat empty non-stick pans on high heat. PFAS exposure overall is covered under [pfas].

## 10. Storing oils and fish-oil supplements

**Culinary oils.** Under simulated household use (opened, light/dark cycle, 25 °C, 40 days), EVOO in green PET bottles lost 44% of its vitamin E and 26% of its phenols. It also exceeded the legal peroxide limit after 30 days. In bag-in-box or tin, losses were 1-5% and 11-12% [kcc-sordini2026]. Over 18 months in the dark, phenols fell markedly after 6 months at room temperature but much less at 4 °C [kcc-crupi2025]. Buy sizes you will finish within a few months, keep oil closed, dark and away from the stove, and prefer tin, dark glass or bag-in-box.

**Fish-oil capsules.** EPA and DHA are among the most oxidizable fats in the diet.

- Independent New Zealand testing found 83% of products over the peroxide limit and most below their label EPA/DHA content [kcc-albert2015].
- A Canadian survey found 50% over limits, with flavoured, bulk and children's products the worst [kcc-jackowski2015]. Most of its authors were affiliated with an omega-3 company.
- A US analysis of three top sellers found excess oxidation and lost in-vitro antioxidant activity [kcc-mason2017].
- An industry-association re-test of 47 New Zealand products found 72-86% compliant [kcc-bannenberg2017].

Health effects are milder than the "rancid oil" fear suggests. In a 7-week RCT, 1.6 g/day EPA+DHA from heavily oxidized oil did not raise urinary isoprostanes, HNE or CRP [kcc-ottestad2012]. However, oxidized oil lost the LDL-particle benefit seen with fresh oil [kcc-rundblad2017], and in another trial it worsened cholesterol [kcc-garciahernandez2013]. See [fish-oil-oxidation] and [omega3-fish].

# Part 2: Light, sleep timing and meal timing

## 11. Morning and daytime light

Light reaching melanopsin cells in the retina is the main signal that sets the body clock. Light early in the biological day moves the clock earlier and strengthens its rhythm. A week of camping with only natural light moved participants' melatonin onset toward sunset, and late chronotypes shifted the most [kcc-wright2013]. A summer camping weekend achieved about 69% of that shift [kcc-stothard2017]. Office workers who got more morning light fell asleep faster and slept better, especially in winter [kcc-figueiro2017]. Light therapy improves sleep problems modestly (insomnia g = 0.47), with smaller effects in RCTs and signs of publication bias [kcc-vanmaanen2016].

In 88,905 UK Biobank participants who wore light sensors for a week, the brightest days were associated with 17-34% lower all-cause mortality over 8 years [kcc-windred2024b]. This is observational: healthier, more mobile people go outside more.

An international expert consensus recommends at least **250 lux melanopic EDI at the eye during the day** [kcc-brown2022]. Typical indoor lighting often falls short of this, while outdoor daylight easily exceeds it. See [morning-light].

## 12. Evening and night-time light

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Gooley 2011, 116 adults [kcc-gooley2011] | Room light (<200 lux) before bed delayed melatonin onset in 99% and cut melatonin duration by ~90 min |
| Phillips 2019, 55 adults [kcc-phillips2019] | 50% melatonin suppression at ~25 lux; sensitivity varied more than 10-fold between people |
| Chang 2015, 12 adults [kcc-chang2015] | E-reader vs paper book for ~4 h before bed: later sleep onset, less melatonin, delayed clock, groggier mornings |
| Schöllhorn 2023, 72 men [kcc-schollhorn2023] | At equal brightness, low-melanopic screen light reduced the melatonin and sleep effects |
| Mason 2022 [kcc-mason2022] | One night sleeping at 100 lux raised heart rate and next-morning insulin resistance |
| Park 2019, 43,722 women [kcc-park2019] | TV or light on while sleeping: incident obesity RR 1.33 |
| Obayashi 2013, 528 older adults [kcc-obayashi2013] | Bedroom ≥3 lux: obesity OR 1.89, dyslipidaemia OR 1.72 |
| Windred 2024, 84,790 adults [kcc-windred2024a] | Brightest nights: type 2 diabetes HR up to 1.53 |

**What helps and what doesn't.** The consensus targets are ≤10 lux melanopic EDI in the 3 hours before bed and ≤1 lux in the bedroom [kcc-brown2022]. Brightness and spectrum together matter. iPhone Night Shift alone did not improve sleep [kcc-duraccio2021], and Cochrane found the effects of blue-blocking glasses on sleep indeterminate [kcc-singh2023]. Melatonin itself is a radical scavenger and supports DNA repair. Supplements are covered under [melatonin]. See [evening-light-exposure].

## 13. Sleep timing and regularity

Going to bed and getting up at consistent times may matter as much as total sleep. In 60,977 UK Biobank participants, the most regular sleepers had 20-48% lower all-cause mortality than the least regular, and regularity predicted death better than duration [kcc-windred2024c]. "Social jetlag", the gap between workday and free-day sleep timing, was linked to insulin resistance, triglycerides and adiposity [kcc-wong2015]. Definite evening types had 10% higher mortality than morning types [kcc-knutson2018]. All of this is observational. Sleep duration itself is covered under [short-sleep].

## 14. Meal timing (chrononutrition)

The body handles the same meal worse at night. A meta-analysis of identical meals found much higher glucose responses at night than in the day [kcc-leung2020]. In forced-desynchrony experiments, postprandial glucose was 17% higher in the biological evening than the morning, independent of sleep and fasting [kcc-morris2015].

**Late dinners.**

- In 845 Spanish adults, a glucose drink taken 1 h before bedtime produced 3.5-fold higher melatonin, 8.3% higher glucose and 6.7% lower insulin than the same drink 4 h before bedtime. The effect was worse in carriers of the MTNR1B melatonin-receptor risk variant [kcc-garaulet2022, kcc-lopezminguez2018].
- Dinner at 22:00 vs 18:00 raised night glucose and reduced fat burning, especially in habitual early sleepers [kcc-gu2020].
- Under tightly controlled conditions, isocaloric late eating raised hunger and lowered daytime energy expenditure [kcc-vujovic2022].

**Weight.**

- Observational: late lunch eaters (after 15:00) lost less weight in a Spanish clinic [kcc-garaulet2013], and late eaters lost ~80 g/week less in a larger cohort [kcc-dashti2021].
- Front-loading trials: a big-breakfast/small-dinner pattern beat the reverse in one trial [kcc-jakubowicz2013], and a 2023 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs found 1.2 kg more weight loss with earlier intake [kcc-young2023].
- Null results: a carefully controlled 4-week crossover found no difference in energy expenditure or weight [kcc-ruddickcollins2022], and an earlier meta-analysis found no weight difference between small and large dinners [kcc-fong2017].

**Oxidative stress.** Early time-restricted feeding lowered 8-isoprostane in men with prediabetes [kcc-sutton2018], but it also shortened the eating window. A 2025 crossover of early vs late isocaloric 8-hour windows found no change in oxidative-stress or insulin-sensitivity markers [kcc-peters2025]. Eating-window length is covered under [time-restricted-eating] and [intermittent-fasting]. See [late-eating].

## 15. Circadian misalignment and oxidative stress

Forced-desynchrony studies show what misalignment does within days:

- Sleeping and eating ~12 h out of phase lowered leptin 17%, raised glucose 6% despite 22% more insulin, and reversed the cortisol rhythm. Three of eight participants reached prediabetic glucose levels [kcc-scheer2009].
- Misalignment alone raised postprandial glucose 6% by reducing insulin sensitivity [kcc-morris2015].
- It raised 24-h blood pressure by 3/1.5 mmHg and IL-6, CRP and TNF-alpha by 3-29% [kcc-morris2016].
- Three weeks of short sleep on 28-hour days lowered metabolic rate and impaired insulin secretion [kcc-buxton2012].

These studies did not report direct oxidative-stress markers. The oxidative evidence comes from other sources:

- Night workers had higher TBARS and hydrogen peroxide and lower catalase, SOD and total antioxidant capacity than day workers [kcc-teixeira2019].
- Urinary 8-OH-dG was lower during night work than night sleep [kcc-bhatti2016, kcc-bhatti2017], which the authors read as impaired DNA repair.
- Melatonin before day sleep gave a borderline (p = 0.06) increase in 8-OH-dG excretion [kcc-zanif2025].
- In mice, simulated night shifts caused mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress, with matching cross-sectional signals in human workers [kcc-jiang2026].

## 16. Shift work

Shift work is associated with:

- 17% more CVD events and 26% more coronary disease. Risk rises about 7% for every 5 years beyond the first 5 [kcc-torquati2018].
- About 9-10% more type 2 diabetes [kcc-gan2015, kcc-gao2020].
- A small absolute increase in coronary risk among US nurses, which faded after they stopped [kcc-vetter2016].

IARC classifies night shift work as "probably carcinogenic" (2A) [kcc-iarc2019]. However, 10 prospective studies found no link with breast cancer even after 30 years [kcc-travis2016]. See [shift-work-circadian].

## Sperm and egg relevance

- **Sperm.** In 3,425 men planning pregnancy, rotating shift workers had ~11% lower sperm concentration and 16% lower chance of pregnancy within a year. Fixed night workers did not differ from day workers [kcc-wang2026]. Night-shift men at a fertility clinic had higher oxidative stress (d-ROMs) [kcc-boeri2025]. Evening screen use correlated with poorer motility in 116 men [kcc-green2020], and outdoor light at night was linked to lower motility in sperm donors [kcc-tian2024]. These are preliminary observational findings, so we did not create a separate sperm factor; they inform the sperm scores of [shift-work-circadian] and [evening-light-exposure].
- **Eggs.** A meta-analysis linked shift work to menstrual disruption and infertility, and night shifts to early pregnancy loss [kcc-stocker2014]. A cohort of 1,739 nurses found no effect on time to pregnancy [kcc-gaskins2015]. At IVF, women on evening, night or rotating shifts had 2.3 fewer mature oocytes [kcc-minguezalarcon2017]. Follicular fluid melatonin protects oocytes from oxidative stress in laboratory studies [kcc-reiter2014]. Follicular and serum AGEs tracked poorer IVF outcomes [kcc-jinno2011], and in women with PCOS a high-AGE diet raised testosterone and oxidative stress [kcc-tantalaki2014]. See [pcos].
- **Pregnancy.** Higher acrylamide exposure was linked to lower birth weight [kcc-pedersen2012].

## Where the evidence is contested

1. **Seed oils.** On the pro side are cohort and biomarker meta-analyses [kcc-li2020, kcc-marklund2019, kcc-farvid2014], an RCT meta-analysis [kcc-mozaffarian2010], no rise in inflammation [kcc-johnson2012] and the AHA advisory [kcc-sacks2017]. On the sceptical side are recovered-data re-analyses showing harm or no benefit [kcc-ramsden2013, kcc-ramsden2016], a restricted meta-analysis [kcc-hamley2017], Cochrane's low-quality, near-null omega-6 verdict [kcc-hooper2018] and the oxidized-LA hypothesis [kcc-dinicolantonio2018]. The AHA counters that the Sydney trial's margarine contained trans fat and the Minnesota trial was short with high turnover [kcc-sacks2017]. The disagreement is mostly about which old trials are valid, not about new data showing seed-oil harm.
2. **Acrylamide.** EFSA's animal-based margin-of-exposure concern [kcc-efsa2015, kcc-efsa2022] contrasts with mostly null epidemiology [kcc-pelucchi2015, kcc-filippini2022, kcc-mucci2003]. The exceptions are gynaecological cancers in never-smokers [kcc-hogervorst2007, kcc-adani2020] and inconsistent biomarker studies [kcc-olesen2008, kcc-obonsantacana2016]. Defenders of the concern point to poor exposure measurement [kcc-wilson2009].
3. **Dietary AGEs.** Positive trials, mostly from one group using an ELISA database [kcc-vlassara2016, kcc-uribarri2011, kcc-uribarri2010], stand against null independent trials and cohorts using LC-MS/MS [kcc-semba2014, kcc-baye2017, kcc-linkens2022, kcc-linkens2023]. One independent blinded trial was positive [kcc-decourten2016].
4. **HCAs and cancer.** Case-control-heavy meta-analyses [kcc-martinezgongora2019, kcc-chiavarini2017] disagree with a large null cohort [kcc-ollberding2012], and the doses involved are low [kcc-sugimura2004].
5. **Marinades.** They usually help, but a sugary marinade raised MeIQx at long cooking times [kcc-salmon1997].
6. **Frying.** US cohorts and a meta-analysis link fried food to CVD [kcc-qin2021, kcc-sun2019]. Spain shows no link [kcc-guallarcastillon2012], and adiposity explained much of the US association [kcc-cahill2014].
7. **Fish-oil oxidation.** Independent surveys [kcc-albert2015, kcc-jackowski2015] disagree with an industry survey [kcc-bannenberg2017], and conflicts of interest exist on both sides. Oxidized oil did not raise oxidative markers [kcc-ottestad2012] but blunted lipid benefits [kcc-rundblad2017, kcc-garciahernandez2013].
8. **Screens and evening light.** The lab data are strong [kcc-chang2015, kcc-gooley2011], but a letter questioned their real-world relevance and the authors replied [kcc-zeitzer2015, kcc-changczeisler2015]. Night-mode filters and blue blockers show little benefit [kcc-duraccio2021, kcc-singh2023]. Light-pattern cohort authors disclose lighting-industry funding [kcc-windred2024a, kcc-windred2024b].
9. **Meal timing and weight.** Positive trials [kcc-jakubowicz2013, kcc-young2023] contrast with null controlled trials [kcc-ruddickcollins2022, kcc-fong2017, kcc-peters2025].
10. **Shift work.** IARC's 2A classification [kcc-iarc2019] contrasts with null prospective breast-cancer data [kcc-travis2016]. Reading low 8-OH-dG as "reduced repair" [kcc-bhatti2017] is an assumption. Fertility findings conflict [kcc-stocker2014, kcc-gaskins2015].
11. **Vegetable cooking.** The broccoli studies disagree about stir-frying [kcc-song2007, kcc-yuan2009].

## Practical bottom line

**In the kitchen**

1. Don't char meat. Use moderate heat, turn often, cut off blackened bits, marinate (herbs, garlic, onion, acid; go easy on sugar) and microwave burgers briefly first. Use stewing, braising and poaching for much of your meat.
2. Cook potatoes, bread and other starches to golden, not brown. Soak cut potatoes before frying, and use an air fryer or oven instead of a deep fryer.
3. For frying and roasting use olive oil (extra-virgin is fine), high-oleic oils or rapeseed. Use seed oils freely as a dietary fat, but not for repeated deep-frying. Don't reuse frying oil until it smokes, darkens or smells acrid.
4. Steam or microwave vegetables rather than boiling them, or keep the cooking water. Cook tomatoes and carrots with a little oil.
5. Store oils closed, cool and dark, and finish them within months. Pick plain fish-oil capsules from brands that publish oxidation tests, keep them cool, and discard any that smell rancid.

**Around the clock**

6. Get bright outdoor light in the morning and plenty of daylight during the day.
7. Dim the lights in the 2-3 hours before bed, turn screens down or off, and sleep in a dark room.
8. Keep sleep and wake times regular, including at weekends.
9. Eat the main meal earlier and finish eating 3-4 hours before bed.
10. If you work nights, protect day sleep, avoid big meals in the small hours and limit years of rotating nights where you can.

## What we don't know

- Whether any cooking change (marinating, avoiding char, low-AGE cooking, air frying) lowers cancer, cardiovascular disease or in-vivo oxidative damage in a randomized trial. None has been tested on hard outcomes.
- Whether dietary AGEs matter for people with normal kidney function once body weight and blood sugar are accounted for.
- Whether acrylamide at dietary doses causes human cancer. Better exposure biomarkers in larger cohorts are needed.
- Whether oxidized linoleic acid metabolites (OXLAMs) predict disease. Long, well-controlled trials of LA intake are lacking.
- Whether light-pattern associations with diabetes and mortality are causal. No randomized trial of daylight or bedroom darkness has measured oxidative stress or hard outcomes.
- Whether meal timing changes oxidative-stress markers independently of the eating window and weight.
- Whether shift work damages sperm DNA or egg quality, and whether melatonin or schedule design prevents it.
