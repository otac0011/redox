# Foods, drinks and dietary patterns vs. systemic oxidative stress

Area id: `diet`. Structured data: `data/diet.json` (43 factors). Every claim below is tied to a reference in the list at the end. All PubMed IDs were checked against the NCBI record (title, DOI and year) on 2026-09-30/10-01. Polyphenol contents come from the Phenol-Explorer website [phenolexplorer, rothwell2013]; vitamin and carotenoid contents come from USDA FoodData Central [usdafdc].

## Bottom line

1. **Diet changes oxidative stress mostly by what you avoid.** Chronic overfeeding raises urinary F2-isoprostanes within weeks [samochabonet2012] and systemic oxidative stress within days at extreme intakes [boden2015]. BMI is one of the strongest population correlates of urinary isoprostanes [keaney2003]. Calorie restriction is the only dietary intervention with a long, well-run RCT showing lower isoprostanes (13-27% over 2 years) [ilyasova2018]. Large glucose or fat loads acutely activate leukocyte NADPH oxidase [mohanty2000, mohanty2002]. High-heat dry cooking multiplies AGEs 10-100-fold [uribarri2010], and alcohol raises isoprostanes in a dose-dependent way [meagher1999].
2. **A Mediterranean-style pattern built on extra-virgin olive oil has the strongest whole-diet evidence.** It cut major cardiovascular events by about 30% in PREDIMED [estruch2018]. Sub-studies showed less oxidative DNA damage [mitjavila2013] and LDL that resisted oxidation better [hernaez2017].
3. **Among single foods, the best human evidence is for:**
   - high-phenolic EVOO [covas2006];
   - cruciferous foods that actually deliver sulforaphane, which depends on how they are prepared [egner2014, chen2019, vermeulen2008, okunade2018];
   - filtered coffee [bakuradze2015, hoelzl2010];
   - blueberries [curtis2019];
   - cocoa flavanols [hooper2012, efsa2012];
   - brewed green tea [hakim2003].
4. **"More antioxidants" is the wrong mental model.** Polyphenol milligrams, Folin scores and ORAC do not predict benefit in the body [forman2014, halliwell2007, manach2005]. High-dose antioxidant pills either do nothing or cause harm [bjelakovic2012, omenn1996]. They can even block the benefits of exercise [ristow2009].

## How to read the evidence

- **Markers matter.** A validation study found that plasma and urinary F2-isoprostanes and MDA measured by GC-MS, plus urinary 8-OHdG, tracked real oxidative injury. Most other markers did not [kadiiska2005]. Even 8-iso-PGF2α can come from enzymatic COX activity during inflammation [vanterve2015]. Many positive food trials rely on weaker measures: TBARS-type MDA, "total antioxidant capacity", or ELISA kits.
- **The same oil gives different answers depending on the marker.** Fish oil raised TBARS but not F2-isoprostanes or GC-MS MDA [higdon2000].
- **Vascular function is the most reproducible outcome for polyphenol foods.** This is flow-mediated dilation (FMD) [hooper2012, fairliejones2017]. It is related to oxidative stress, but it is not the same thing. One blueberry trial did tie its FMD gain to oxidative stress directly: the gain shrank when ascorbate was infused [woolf2023].
- **Healthy people may already be at a ceiling.** In one trial, 600 g/day of fruit and vegetables for 24 days did not change oxidative DNA damage in healthy non-smokers [moller2003]. Positive results cluster in people with high baseline stress: smokers [hakim2003], metabolic syndrome [basu2010, nair2017], type 2 diabetes [bahadoran2011], or obesity [lopes2003].

## Why milligrams of polyphenols (and ORAC) do not translate into benefit in the body

1. **Assays disagree with each other.** The Folin-Ciocalteu "total phenolics" assay also reacts with protein, sugars and other reducing substances. Phenol-Explorer's Folin values run consistently higher than the sum of compounds measured by chromatography [perezjimenez2010]. The gap is sometimes absurd. Lentils score 3697 mg/100 g by Folin but about 10 mg/100 g by chromatography. Black beans score 1391 vs 54 mg/100 g [phenolexplorer]. Even within ORAC, total phenolics correlated only weakly with hydrophilic ORAC [wu2004]. FRAP "antioxidant content" varies several-thousand-fold between foods [carlsen2010], and none of these rankings has been validated against outcomes in people. (USDA withdrew its ORAC database in 2012.)
2. **Most of the compound is not absorbed intact.** Eating 50 mg of a polyphenol yields 0-4 µmol/L of total metabolites in plasma. Anthocyanins, galloylated catechins and proanthocyanidins are among the least absorbed [manach2005]. A ¹³C-labelled anthocyanin had about 12% relative bioavailability including all its metabolites, which circulated for up to 48 h as phenolic and hippuric acids [czank2013]. Resveratrol is well absorbed, but almost none survives unconjugated [walle2004]. Curcumin is barely detectable without piperine [shoba1998, anand2007].
3. **Plasma concentrations are too low to scavenge radicals.** Kinetic arguments show that low-micromolar polyphenols cannot out-compete the body's own enzymes for radicals [forman2014].
4. **The active molecules are often made by your gut bacteria, and people differ.** Gut bacteria turn polyphenols into phenolic acids, valerolactones and urolithins. Some people are "high" or "low excretors", and some are "producers" or "non-producers" of a given metabolite [favari2024]. The same food therefore does different things in different people.
5. **Dose-response curves have thresholds and plateaus.** For example:
   - Blueberry FMD rose with dose up to about 766 mg polyphenols, then flattened [rodriguezmateos2013].
   - 75 g/day of blueberries did nothing; 150 g/day worked [curtis2019].
   - Cocoa improved FMD regardless of dose [hooper2012], and the gain plateaued after about 2 weeks [sansone2015].
   - Plasma vitamin C stops rising much above about 200 mg/day [levine1996].
   - Sulforaphane only worked at the highest of three doses [chen2019].
   - Olive oil phenolics are the exception, with a roughly linear dose-response across the range tested [covas2006].
6. **Compounds work through different mechanisms, so the milligrams are not interchangeable.** See the next section.

So the `key_numbers` in the JSON are there to show what a food contains, not to rank benefit. Coffee delivers the most polyphenols per serving of anything below, while EVOO delivers among the least, yet EVOO has some of the best outcome evidence.

## Different foods, different mechanisms

| Mechanism | Main dietary examples | What the human data show |
|---|---|---|
| **Electrophile signalling through Nrf2/KEAP1.** An "indirect antioxidant": the compound switches on glutathione synthesis, GST, NQO1 and HO-1 [dinkovakostova2008, cuadrado2019]. | Sulforaphane (broccoli sprouts, seeds, raw broccoli). Weaker: curcumin, garlic organosulfur compounds, coffee roast compounds [houghton2016]. | Phase II enzymes induced in airway cells in a dose-dependent way [riedl2009]. Detoxication of benzene and acrolein raised [egner2014]. No effect in COPD lungs [wise2016]. |
| **Gut-microbial metabolites acting on endothelium and NADPH oxidase.** | Anthocyanins (berries), flavan-3-ols (cocoa, tea), chlorogenic acids (coffee, eggplant), ellagitannins (walnuts, pomegranate, blackberries). | FMD gains [curtis2019, hooper2012]. Lower neutrophil NADPH oxidase [rodriguezmateos2013]. Lower monocyte ROS [nair2017]. Fewer DNA strand breaks with coffee [bakuradze2015]. |
| **Protecting LDL and membranes inside lipid particles.** | Olive hydroxytyrosol, lycopene, other carotenoids, vitamin E. | Oxidized LDL falls with olive phenolic dose [covas2006]. Less postprandial LDL oxidation with tomato [burtonfreeman2012]. |
| **Antioxidant chemistry in the food and gut lumen.** Concentrations are high here, so direct scavenging is plausible. | Herbs and spices in meat, polyphenols eaten with oxidizable fats. | Spice blend: 71% less MDA in the burger and 49% less in urine [li2010]. Marinades cut HCAs by 57-88% [smith2008]. In rats, red-wine polyphenols blocked stomach lipid peroxidation [gorelik2008, kanner2007]. |
| **Nitric oxide supply.** | Nitrate in leafy greens and beetroot. | Systolic BP about -4.4 mmHg [siervo2013]. Additive with cocoa flavanols [rodriguezmateos2015]. |
| **Classic chain-breaking antioxidant nutrients.** | Vitamin C, carotenoids. | Useful up to saturation (about 200 mg/day vitamin C) [levine1996]. Pills add nothing or harm [bjelakovic2012, omenn1996]. |

Because these routes differ, eating sulforaphane foods and polyphenol foods together is **complementary rather than redundant**. Foods that share a route (two strong sulforaphane sources, or two anthocyanin sources taken past the plateau) are partly redundant.

## The saturation question: if broccoli seed powder already activates Nrf2, does eating broccoli add anything?

No human trial has tested stacking a sulforaphane supplement with dietary broccoli. The relevant human data are these:

- **There is a threshold.** In Qidong, only the full-dose broccoli sprout drink raised benzene detoxication (+63%). It produced about 25 µmol of urinary sulforaphane metabolites per day. Half and one-fifth doses did nothing [chen2019]. A small extra dose on top of nothing may therefore fall below the threshold, and the same dose added to an already effective dose lands on an unknown part of the curve.
- **No ceiling has been mapped.** In the upper airway, phase II enzyme induction rose with dose up to 200 g of sprouts and did not plateau within that range [riedl2009]. Absorption is linear from 25 to 200 µmol [shapiro2001], so the gut is not the bottleneck. A major review states that no maximally effective dose has been established and that the shape of the curve is unknown [yagishita2019].
- **There is no tolerance with daily use.** Over 12 weeks of daily dosing, sulforaphane bioavailability did not decline, and the detox effect appeared quickly and was sustained [egner2014].
- **Some markers do not move at all.** Twice-daily vs once-daily dosing, and sprouts vs extract, did not change HO-1, HDAC activity or p21 in blood cells [atwell2015]. In COPD, 25 or 150 µmol/day did not induce Nrf2 targets in the lung [wise2016].
- **The pharmacokinetics are pulsatile.** Sulforaphane's half-life is about 2.5 h [vermeulen2008]. Seed powder in the morning and broccoli at dinner give two separate peaks rather than one doubled peak. Glucoraphanin taken without myrosinase is released more slowly and more steadily [egner2011].
- **Cell biology sets a ceiling of its own.** Nrf2 induction is bounded: KEAP1 sensing and enzyme synthesis saturate, and strong chronic activation raises unresolved safety questions [cuadrado2019].

**Practical answer.** If you already take an effective dose (seed powder with active myrosinase, or a fresh sprout serving), the extra **Nrf2-specific** benefit from broccoli at another meal is probably smaller than it would be on its own. Diminishing returns are likely, though not proven. **But broccoli is not just a sulforaphane pill.** It adds about 89 mg vitamin C per 100 g [usdafdc], plus lutein, folate and fiber. Mature heads also contain indole glucosinolates that sprouts largely lack [fahey1997]. None of that is redundant. Polyphenol foods (coffee, berries, cocoa, olive oil) act through different routes and are not made redundant by Nrf2 activation.

## Food-by-food notes

### Cruciferous vegetables and sulforaphane

- **Chemistry.** Broccoli contains glucoraphanin, which is inert on its own. Myrosinase, an enzyme released when plant tissue is crushed, converts it to sulforaphane. If cooking has destroyed the myrosinase, gut bacteria take over, with conversion ranging from about 1% to more than 40% between people. Each person's rate is fairly stable over time [fahey2012].
- **Sprouts.** Three-day sprouts hold 10-100 times more glucoraphanin than mature heads [fahey1997]. Fresh sprouts gave about 3x more absorbed sulforaphane than a myrosinase-treated extract at the same nominal dose [atwell2015]. Chewing thoroughly raises absorption [shapiro2001].
- **Seeds.** Seeds are the richest source [yagishita2019]. Eaten whole with their own active myrosinase, sprouts or seeds were 3-4x more bioavailable than glucoraphanin given without myrosinase [fahey2015].
- **Mature broccoli.** Supermarket heads averaged 0.38 µmol/g glucoraphanin, ranging from under 0.005 to 1.13 [yagishita2019]. Cooking is the main problem:
  - Raw broccoli: 32-37% of sulforaphane reaches the urine. Cooked: 3.4-10% [conaway2000, vermeulen2008].
  - Mild heating to about 60 °C actually *increases* sulforaphane, because it kills a protein that diverts glucoraphanin to an inactive nitrile. At 70 °C and above, myrosinase in florets is destroyed [matusheski2004].
  - Adding 1 g of mustard powder to 200 g of cooked broccoli raised sulforaphane output about 4.5-fold [okunade2018].
  - High-glucoraphanin varieties gave 3-5x more circulating sulforaphane even from soup [sivapalan2018].
- **Supplements.** Glucoraphanin-only products recovered about 5% of the dose in urine in Qidong and about 8-13% in Baltimore. Sulforaphane-rich drinks recovered about 70% [egner2011, fahey2015]. A myrosinase-free pill gave dramatically lower isothiocyanate levels than 40 g of fresh sprouts [clarke2011]. Labels are not always accurate: one diabetes trial took its sulforaphane dose from manufacturer claims, which the authors of a later review found did not hold up when assayed [yagishita2019].
- **Outcome evidence.**
  - Detox: daily dosing for 12 weeks raised excretion of benzene (+61%) and acrolein (+23%) conjugates [egner2014]. An earlier 2-week trial showed no overall difference between arms, but people who absorbed more sulforaphane formed fewer aflatoxin-DNA adducts [kensler2005].
  - Oxidative markers: broccoli sprout powder lowered MDA and oxidized LDL in type 2 diabetes [bahadoran2011, bahadoran2012]. Glucoraphanin capsules lowered urinary 8-OHdG and liver enzymes in fatty liver [kikuchi2015]. In an uncontrolled study, 30 g/day of sprouts lowered IL-6 and CRP [lopezchillon2019].
  - Null result: no effect in COPD [wise2016].
  - Safety: thyroid function stayed normal over 12 weeks [chartoumpekis2019], and a 7-day phase I study found no toxicity [shapiro2006].

### Berries

- **Blueberries have the most trials.** A 6-month RCT found that 150 g/day improved FMD by 1.45 percentage points and lowered arterial stiffness, while 75 g/day did nothing [curtis2019]. Other blueberry trials found:
  - better endothelial function [stull2015, woolf2023];
  - lower monocyte superoxide and ROS [nair2017];
  - 28% lower oxidized LDL [basu2010];
  - fewer oxidized DNA bases [riso2013];
  - lower blood pressure in postmenopausal women [johnson2015].

  A systematic review of 45 studies rates vascular benefits as consistent and oxidative-stress results as conflicting [martini2023].
- **Blackberries** have similar polyphenol density: about 257 mg/100 g by chromatography and 147 mg/100 g anthocyanins [phenolexplorer]. However, the only controlled feeding study measured fat oxidation and insulin sensitivity, not oxidative stress [solverson2018].
- **Across berries,** pooled RCTs show small improvements in LDL, systolic BP, glucose and TNF-α [huang2016]. Anthocyanin foods improve FMD [fairliejones2017]. In a cohort of young women, high anthocyanin intake was linked to fewer heart attacks [cassidy2013].

### Eggplant

- **The evidence is in test tubes.** Nasunin, the anthocyanin in eggplant skin, scavenges superoxide and blocks hydroxyl-radical formation by chelating iron, in vitro [noda2000]. The flesh's main phenolic is chlorogenic acid, at more than 65% of phenolic acids, with large differences between cultivars [whitaker2003, kaushik2017]. Phenol-Explorer lists only a Folin value for whole purple eggplant (61 mg/100 g) [phenolexplorer].
- **The one human RCT was not about antioxidants.** Eggplant powder lowered blood pressure, and the authors attributed this to acetylcholine [nishimura2019].
- **Compared with coffee:** one cup of filtered coffee supplies about 168 mg of chlorogenic acid (5-CQA) [phenolexplorer] and has human trial data. Eggplant does not.

### Coffee

- **Main compounds.** Coffee is the world's main source of chlorogenic acids, and much of it is metabolised by colonic bacteria [clifford2017]. In PREDIMED it was the second-largest contributor to urinary polyphenols, after fruit and vegetables [medinaremon2011].
- **Trials.** 750 mL/day for 4 weeks produced a 27% difference in DNA strand breaks vs water [bakuradze2015]. The effect appeared within 2 h of the first cup [bakuradze2016]. 800 mL/day of high-chlorogenic-acid coffee lowered urinary 8-iso-PGF2α by 15% in 5 days [hoelzl2010]. The overall picture: coffee raises glutathione and protects DNA, while effects on lipid and protein oxidation are unclear [martini2016].
- **Cohorts.** The lowest risk is at 3-4 cups/day: all-cause mortality RR 0.83 [poole2017]. Decaf shows the same association [ding2015].
- **Filtered vs unfiltered.** Paper filters remove the cafestol and kahweol that raise LDL [urgert1997]. About 10 mg of cafestol is in 2-3 cups of French-press coffee [urgert1997b].
- **Caffeine and pregnancy or fertility** are covered by the fertility agents. Note only that pregnancy is where coffee's harm signals cluster [poole2017].

### Tea

- **Green tea.** In heavy smokers, 4 cups/day of decaffeinated green tea for 4 months cut urinary 8-OHdG by 31%. Black tea did nothing in the same trial [hakim2003]. In metabolic syndrome, 4 cups/day lowered MDA and HNE [basu2010tea]. EGCG peaks about 1.5 h after drinking, with a half-life of about 3.4 h [lee2002].
- **Extracts.** EFSA found that supplements supplying 800 mg/day of EGCG or more raise liver transaminases. Brewed tea at usual intakes (90-300 mg EGCG/day) is generally safe [efsa2018]. Rare severe liver injury has been reported, mostly with multi-ingredient products [mazzanti2015].

### Cocoa and dark chocolate

- **Endothelial function.** EFSA accepted cause and effect for endothelial function at 200 mg of cocoa flavanols per day. That is about 2.5 g of high-flavanol cocoa powder or 10 g of high-flavanol dark chocolate [efsa2012]. A meta-analysis shows FMD gains with cocoa [hooper2012].
- **Healthy adults.** 900 mg/day improved FMD, blood pressure and lipids [sansone2015].
- **COSMOS** (500 mg/day for 3.6 years, 21,442 people): total cardiovascular events were not significantly reduced (HR 0.90). Cardiovascular death fell (HR 0.73), but that was a secondary outcome [sesso2022].

### Extra-virgin olive oil

- **EUROLIVE** (200 men, 25 mL/day): oxidized LDL and other oxidative markers fell linearly with the oil's phenolic content (2.7, 164 and 366 mg/kg) [covas2006].
- **The EU claim** follows an EFSA opinion [efsa2011]. Under Regulation 432/2012, oils with at least 5 mg of hydroxytyrosol and derivatives per 20 g may carry an LDL-protection claim [tsimidou2019].
- **What a typical oil supplies.** Phenol-Explorer lists about 37 mg/100 g of hydroxytyrosol-containing secoiridoids for an average EVOO (sum of compounds) [phenolexplorer]. Twenty grams is in the right range, but oils vary widely.
- **PREDIMED.** Adding EVOO to a Mediterranean diet cut events (HR 0.69) [estruch2018] and made LDL less oxidizable [hernaez2017].

### Nuts, legumes and whole grains

- **Walnuts and nuts.** Walnuts lower LDL and apoB [guaschferre2018] and improved endothelial function. Oxidation markers did not change in the endothelial trial [ros2004], and the PREDIMED nut arm did not improve LDL oxidation [hernaez2017]. Overall evidence on nuts and oxidation is mixed [lopezuriarte2009].
- **Legumes, whole grains and fiber** act mainly indirectly. They lower glycemic load and feed gut fermentation [okeefe2008, reynolds2019]. Whole grains: about 19% lower coronary heart disease risk per 90 g/day in cohorts [aune2016].

### Tomatoes, carotenoids, leafy greens and vitamin C foods

- **Tomatoes.** Lycopene is 2.5-3.8x more bioavailable from paste than from fresh tomatoes [gartner1997]. Carotenoids need fat to be absorbed: with fat-free dressing, absorption was essentially nil [brown2004]. Adding avocado raised absorption 2.6-15-fold [unlu2005]. Tomato eaten with a high-fat meal blunted LDL oxidation [burtonfreeman2012]. Tomato interventions improved LDL, IL-6 and FMD [cheng2017].
- **Beta-carotene pills** increased lung cancer in smokers [omenn1996], and beta-carotene and vitamin E supplements raised mortality in the Cochrane review [bjelakovic2012]. Get carotenoids from food.
- **Leafy greens.** Nitrate lowers blood pressure [siervo2013], and greens and cruciferous vegetables are among the produce most strongly linked to lower mortality [aune2017].
- **Vitamin C.** Plasma saturates around 200 mg/day, an amount easily reached from food [levine1996]. Kiwifruit protected lymphocyte DNA from H2O2 ex vivo [collins2001].

### Garlic, turmeric, pomegranate, red wine, herbs and spices

- **Garlic** supplements lower blood pressure in people with hypertension [ried2016]. A meta-analysis reports oxidative-marker effects, but it relies on weak markers and small trials [askari2021].
- **Turmeric:** curcumin is barely absorbed without piperine [shoba1998, anand2007]. A teaspoon of dried turmeric contains roughly 65 mg of curcumin, far below trial doses [phenolexplorer].
- **Pomegranate:** the dramatic 10-patient study [aviram2004] was not confirmed. An 18-month RCT in 289 people found no effect on carotid thickening [davidson2009], and a meta-analysis found no effect on lipids [sahebkar2016].
- **Red wine and resveratrol: the numbers do not work.** Red wine contains about 0.18 mg/100 mL of trans-resveratrol [phenolexplorer], so a glass holds about 0.3 mg. A 25 mg oral dose leaves under 5 ng/mL of free resveratrol in plasma [walle2004]. Dietary resveratrol levels did not predict mortality [semba2014]. Ethanol itself raises isoprostanes [meagher1999].
- **Herbs and spices** work best in the pan. They prevent oxidation of the meat itself and reduce the oxidized products that reach your body [li2010, smith2008]. They also blunt postprandial insulin and triglycerides [skulasray2011].

### Harmful dietary factors

- **Processed and red meat.** IARC classifies processed meat as Group 1 and red meat as Group 2A carcinogens [bouvard2015]. Heme iron drives lipid peroxidation and N-nitrosation in the gut [bastide2011]. Fecal N-nitroso compounds rise with each extra gram of red meat; white meat has no such effect [bingham2002]. High-heat cooking adds HCAs and PAHs [cross2004].
- **AGEs from dry cooking.** An identical meal cooked to be high in AGEs cut FMD by 36%, vs 21% for the low-AGE version [negrean2007]. One year on a low-AGE diet improved insulin resistance and oxidative markers [vlassara2016]. A systematic review agrees but notes limited trial quality [clarke2016].
- **Oxidized frying oils** supply absorbed lipid-oxidation end-products [kanner2007, ng2014]. Human outcome data are thin.
- **Trans fats** carry strong cardiovascular harm evidence [mozaffarian2006].
- **Postprandial spikes and overfeeding** — see the Bottom line [mohanty2000, mohanty2002, okeefe2008, boden2015, samochabonet2012].
- **Alcohol:** brief note only; the lifestyle agent covers it in depth [meagher1999].
- **Iron supplements without deficiency** raised the free-radical-generating capacity of feces by about 40% [lund1999].

### Dietary patterns

- **Mediterranean diet.** PREDIMED's event reduction is described above [estruch2018]. In sub-studies, urinary 8-oxo-dG fell more than on the low-fat control diet, while F2-isoprostanes showed only a borderline difference [mitjavila2013]. LDL oxidation improved in the EVOO arm [hernaez2017].
- **DASH.** In 24 adults, the diet prevented the F2-isoprostane rise during an experimental lipid infusion and lowered blood pressure in obese people with hypertension [lopes2003].
- **High fruit and vegetable intake.** Results conflict. Doubling produce to 12 servings/day lowered 8-OHdG and isoprostanes in an uncontrolled study [thompson1999]. 600 g/day did nothing in a controlled trial [moller2003]. Cohorts show benefits up to about 800 g/day [aune2017].
- **Calorie restriction:** isoprostanes fell 13-27% over 2 years in CALERIE [ilyasova2018], and DNA damage fell at 6 months [heilbronn2006].
- **Intermittent fasting.** Early time-restricted feeding lowered plasma 8-isoprostane by 14% in 8 men. The difference came mainly from a worsening on the control schedule [sutton2018]. An uncontrolled alternate-day trial reported large falls in oxidative markers alongside 8% weight loss [johnson2007]. For a mechanistic review, see [decabo2019].

## How to compare foods

The ranking below is by **strength of human evidence × likely size of effect on systemic oxidative stress** (not on general health). Ranks 3-7 are close, and a different reading of the same papers could reorder them.

| Rank | Food | Why it ranks here | Main uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Extra-virgin olive oil (high-phenolic)** | Large RCT with a linear dose-response on oxidized LDL [covas2006]. EU-authorised claim [efsa2011, tsimidou2019]. Outcome RCT plus LDL-oxidation sub-study [estruch2018, hernaez2017]. | Phenolic content varies widely and is seldom labelled. |
| 2 | **Cruciferous foods delivered with active myrosinase** (fresh sprouts, seed powder, raw or mustard-dressed broccoli) | Strongest mechanism and clear pharmacodynamics in humans [egner2014, chen2019, riedl2009]. Oxidative markers improved in at-risk groups [bahadoran2011, kikuchi2015]. | Threshold dose. COPD null [wise2016]. No hard outcomes. Preparation decides whether you get any sulforaphane. |
| 3 | **Filtered coffee, 3-4 cups** | Several RCTs on DNA strand breaks, isoprostanes and glutathione [bakuradze2015, hoelzl2010, martini2016]. Huge cohort base [poole2017]. | Some trials industry-funded. Lipid and protein oxidation markers inconsistent. |
| 4 | **Blueberries, about 150 g/day** | 6-month RCT [curtis2019] plus multiple oxidative and vascular RCTs [basu2010, nair2017, riso2013, woolf2023]. | Small trials. Threshold (75 g failed). Oxidative markers mixed [martini2023]. |
| 5 | **Cocoa flavanols, ≥200 mg/day** | Robust FMD meta-analysis and EFSA claim [hooper2012, efsa2012]. Large outcome RCT [sesso2022]. | Primary COSMOS endpoint null. Oxidative damage itself rarely measured. |
| 6 | **Brewed green tea, about 4 cups** | Large 8-OHdG fall in smokers [hakim2003]. Lower MDA in metabolic syndrome [basu2010tea]. | Little data in healthy non-smokers. Extracts add liver risk [efsa2018]. |
| 7 | **Tomato products cooked with oil** | Lower postprandial oxidized LDL [burtonfreeman2012]. Meta-analysis on LDL and FMD [cheng2017]. | Short trials. Oxidized LDL not significant in the meta-analysis. |
| 8 | **Nuts (walnuts), about 30 g** | Lipid-lowering meta-analysis [guaschferre2018]. Outcome RCT [estruch2018]. | Direct oxidation markers mostly unchanged [ros2004, hernaez2017]. |
| 9 | **Leafy greens and vitamin C-rich fruit and vegetables** | Nitrate BP meta-analysis [siervo2013]. Vitamin C saturation is easy from food [levine1996]. Cohort data [aune2017]. | Benefit concentrated in people with low intake. |
| 10 | **Herbs and spices cooked into meat and fatty dishes** | Large reductions in MDA and HCAs in the food and in urinary MDA [li2010, smith2008]. | Tiny meal studies. Benefit depends on what you are cooking. |

**Lower down:** blackberries and other berries (plausible by analogy, little direct data) [solverson2018, huang2016]; mature cooked broccoli without mustard [vermeulen2008]; whole grains and legumes (indirect) [aune2016, reynolds2019]; garlic [askari2021]; fish (neutral for oxidation, modest cardiovascular benefit) [higdon2000, abdelhamid2020]; turmeric [shoba1998]; pomegranate [davidson2009]; eggplant (in vitro only) [noda2000]. Red wine as an "antioxidant" is not supported [walle2004, meagher1999].

**The answer to the user's examples.**
- Per serving, blackberries supply about as many polyphenols as blueberries [phenolexplorer], but blueberries have the trials.
- Eggplant skin's nasunin looks impressive in vitro but has no human oxidative-stress data [noda2000].
- Coffee supplies more polyphenol per day than either and has DNA-damage RCTs [bakuradze2015].
- Broccoli sprouts work through a different mechanism (Nrf2), with the clearest human pharmacodynamics. They are complementary to all three rather than competing with them.

For comparison, the levers that sit above every food on this list are: not overeating [ilyasova2018, samochabonet2012], cutting alcohol [meagher1999], cutting processed meat and charred or high-AGE cooking [bouvard2015, negrean2007], and adopting a Mediterranean pattern [estruch2018].

## Practical servings guide (amounts used in positive human trials)

| Food | Amount that worked in trials | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Broccoli sprouts | 30-40 g fresh per day [lopezchillon2019, clarke2011]. Detox beverages: 600 µmol glucoraphanin + 40 µmol sulforaphane per day [egner2014]. | Chew well. Threshold of about 25 µmol/day urinary sulforaphane metabolites [chen2019]. |
| Broccoli seed powder | Doses supplying about 50-230 µmol glucoraphanin, with active myrosinase [fahey2015] | Mix into cool food. Do not cook. |
| Mature broccoli | 200 g raw or lightly steamed [vermeulen2008, conaway2000] | If cooked, add about 1 g mustard powder afterwards [okunade2018]. |
| Blueberries | 150 g fresh per day (1 cup) [curtis2019], or 22-25 g freeze-dried [johnson2015, woolf2023, riso2013] | 75 g/day was ineffective. |
| Blackberries | 600 g/day in the only feeding trial [solverson2018]. About 150 g is a sensible serving by analogy. | No oxidative-stress RCT. |

## What the evidence does not show

- There is no trial of combining several Nrf2 activators, and no long-term trial of sulforaphane on clinical outcomes [yagishita2019].
- Few food trials use mass-spectrometry isoprostanes or 8-oxo-dG. Many rely on TBARS or TAC, which are non-specific [kadiiska2005].
- Most berry, cocoa and coffee trials are small, short and often industry-funded. Heterogeneity is high [huang2016, fairliejones2017, martini2016].
- People differ widely in how they metabolise polyphenols [favari2024] and glucoraphanin [fahey2012], so average effects hide responders and non-responders.
- Eggplant, pomegranate, turmeric and garlic lack convincing human oxidative-stress data.
