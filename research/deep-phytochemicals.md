# Isothiocyanates & polyphenols deep dive

Scope: isothiocyanates other than sulforaphane, the main polyphenol families, and related "xenohormetic" compounds (spermidine, astaxanthin). Sulforaphane/broccoli, curcumin, fasting and ketosis are covered elsewhere. Structured data: `patch-phyto.json` (new diet, sperm and egg factors plus replacement entries for existing diet factors). Every PubMed ID cited here was checked against the NCBI record (title, DOI, year) and screened for retraction or expressions of concern on 2026-10-01; none were flagged. Leads came partly from Rhonda Patrick's FoundMyFitness topic pages, episode pages and posts on X; those are used only to pick what to research and are never cited as evidence.

## Bottom line

1. **These compounds are signals, not sponges.** Plasma levels of polyphenol metabolites are low-micromolar at best [phy-manach2005], too low to out-compete the body's own enzymes for radicals [phy-forman2014]. The plausible mechanisms are mild electrophilic or hormetic signalling (Nrf2 and related pathways) [phy-dinkovakostova2008, phy-son2008] plus local effects on blood vessels and the gut.
2. **The most reproducible human effects are vascular, not "antioxidant".** Flavan-3-ols (cocoa, tea, apples, grapes) lower office blood pressure by about 2.8/2.0 mmHg and raise flow-mediated dilation by about 2 percentage points across 145 RCTs, with bigger effects in hypertension [phy-lagou2025]. Quercetin at 500 mg/day or more [phy-serban2016], hesperidin [phy-rizza2011, phy-morand2011] and milled flaxseed [phy-rodriguezleyva2013] show the same kind of effect.
3. **Direct oxidative-damage endpoints are rarer, but exist for some foods.** Watercress (85 g/day) cut lymphocyte DNA damage by 17% and oxidative purine damage by 24% [phy-gill2007]. High-flavanol cocoa prevented an F2-isoprostane rise [phy-wiswedel2004]. Astaxanthin lowered MDA in pooled small trials [phy-ma2022].
4. **The fashionable longevity supplements are mostly ahead of the evidence.** Fisetin has no reported human senolytic RCT and failed to extend lifespan in the rigorous NIA mouse program [phy-harrison2024]. Spermidine's 12-month trial was null [phy-schwarz2022]. Resveratrol's metabolic effects are trivial in meta-analyses [phy-zeraattalab2021], and it blunted training gains in older men [phy-gliemann2013]. Urolithin A is the best of this group, with modest muscle-endurance gains in manufacturer-funded RCTs whose primary endpoints were mostly missed [phy-liu2022, phy-singh2022].
5. **Your gut bacteria decide much of the outcome.** Up to a quarter of people make no urolithins from ellagitannins [phy-tomasbarberan2014], and only 25-60% can make equol from soy [phy-frankenfeld2014].
6. **Food patterns beat isolated pills on the evidence we have.** Higher food polyphenol intake [phy-tresserrarimbau2014] and higher flavonoid diversity [phy-parmenter2025] are associated with lower mortality, and a polyphenol-rich diet improved blood pressure and gut markers in a controlled trial [phy-delbo2021]. High-dose isolated compounds bring new risks: liver injury from green tea extract [phy-yu2017], higher LDL with pterostilbene [phy-riche2014], and lower tamoxifen metabolites with DIM [phy-thomson2017].

## How these compounds act in the body: signalling vs scavenging

- **Direct scavenging requires concentration.** A typical 50 mg dose of a polyphenol gives 0-4 µmol/L of total metabolites in plasma, mostly conjugates rather than the parent compound [phy-manach2005]. Reaction-rate arguments show such levels cannot meaningfully trap radicals against enzymes such as peroxiredoxins and glutathione peroxidases [phy-forman2014].
- **Indirect antioxidants work catalytically.** Electrophilic compounds (isothiocyanates, some polyphenol oxidation products) modify KEAP1 cysteines, freeing Nrf2 to induce glutathione synthesis, NQO1, HO-1 and other long-lived enzymes. These are not consumed, so a brief pulse can protect for days [phy-dinkovakostova2008]. Forman and colleagues call this maintenance of "nucleophilic tone" or para-hormesis [phy-forman2014].
- **Hormesis and xenohormesis.** Many phytochemicals are mild toxins at the doses humans eat and act by triggering stress-response pathways (Nrf2, sirtuin-FOXO, NF-kB) [phy-son2008]. The xenohormesis hypothesis adds an evolutionary twist: animals may have evolved to read plant stress molecules as an early warning of hard times [phy-howitz2008]. It is an interesting idea, not a tested human result.
- **Where scavenging is plausible.** Inside the gut lumen and in food, concentrations are high. Lipid-soluble molecules that partition into membranes (astaxanthin, olive phenolics bound to LDL) also have a better case. Even so, the human data for astaxanthin rest on soft markers [phy-ma2022, phy-park2010].
- **Not every effect is redox.** Some headline effects run through other targets: estrogen metabolism for indole-3-carbinol [phy-michnovicz1997], mitophagy for urolithin A [phy-ryu2016], autophagy for spermidine [phy-eisenberg2016], and cyclooxygenase inhibition for oleocanthal [phy-beauchamp2005].

## Bioavailability and the microbiome

- **Absorption varies by compound and food matrix.** Quercetin glucosides from onions were 52% absorbed in ileostomy volunteers, against 24% for the pure aglycone used in most supplements and 17% for rutin [phy-hollman1995]. Isoflavones and gallic acid are among the best-absorbed polyphenols; anthocyanins, proanthocyanidins and galloylated catechins (EGCG) are among the worst [phy-manach2005]. Pure (-)-epicatechin was ~82% bioavailable in the one study that measured it, while flavan-3-ols from tea, cocoa, apples and grapes average ~25%, largely as microbial valerolactones [phy-dipede2023].
- **Gut bacteria make many of the active molecules.** Most polyphenols reach the colon, where microbes turn them into smaller phenolics that are often better absorbed and more active [phy-espin2017].
- **Metabotypes split people into responders and non-responders.**
  - *Urolithins.* After eating ellagitannins (pomegranate, walnuts, strawberries, raspberries), 25-80% of people make only urolithin A (metabotype A), 10-50% also make isourolithin A/urolithin B (B) and 5-25% make none (0). These proportions held regardless of food source [phy-tomasbarberan2014]. In 839 people aged 5-90, age was the main determinant of metabotype [phy-cortesmartin2018].
  - *Equol.* Only some adults convert soy daidzein to equol [phy-setchell2002]; estimates are 25-60%, against 80-95% for the alternative metabolite O-DMA [phy-frankenfeld2014]. Setchell proposed that failing to separate producers from non-producers explains much of the inconsistency in soy trials [phy-setchell2002].
  - *Isothiocyanates.* Sulforaphane and erucin interconvert in the body at a rate that is consistent within a person but varies between people [phy-clarke2011].
  - *Lignans.* Plant lignans become enterolactone only through gut fermentation; milled flaxseed raised enterolignans 2-50-fold [phy-rodriguezleyva2013].
- **Practical consequence.** Trials that don't stratify by metabotype dilute real effects in producers. Buying the downstream metabolite (urolithin A, and in principle equol) is one way around it; urolithin A capsules were bioavailable in plasma at all doses tested [phy-andreux2019].
- **Polyphenols also change the microbiome.** In the MaPLE crossover trial in 51 older adults, raising polyphenol intake from 812 to 1391 mg/day through polyphenol-rich foods for 8 weeks lowered serum zonulin and diastolic blood pressure and increased butyrate-producing bacteria [phy-delbo2021]. Zonulin is an indirect marker of gut permeability.

## Isothiocyanates beyond sulforaphane

Isothiocyanates (ITCs) form only when the plant enzyme myrosinase meets its glucosinolate substrate in crushed or chewed tissue. Cooking destroys myrosinase. Lightly microwaved cabbage (2 minutes) yielded more allyl isothiocyanate than cabbage cooked for 5.5 minutes, and boosting gut bifidobacteria with inulin did not make up the difference [phy-fuller2007].

### Watercress and PEITC

- **DNA damage.** In 60 adults (half smokers), 85 g/day of raw watercress for 8 weeks lowered basal lymphocyte DNA damage by 17% and basal-plus-oxidative purine damage by 24%; benefits were larger in smokers. Plasma lutein doubled and beta-carotene rose by a third [phy-gill2007], so carotenoids are part of the story.
- **Tobacco carcinogen metabolism.** Eleven smokers ate 57 g of watercress at each meal for 3 days (at least 19-38 mg PEITC/day). Urinary detoxified metabolites of the lung carcinogen NNK rose by 33.5%, in proportion to PEITC intake [phy-hecht1995]. A later crossover RCT in 82 smokers gave pure PEITC (10 mg four times daily). It lowered the NNK activation ratio by only 7.7% [phy-yuan2016]: real, but small.
- **Exercise.** In 10 young men, acute and 8-week watercress supplementation blunted the rise in DNA damage and lipid peroxidation after exhaustive exercise [phy-fogarty2013].
- **Verdict.** This is the best human evidence for any non-sulforaphane ITC food, but it comes from small trials.

### Moringa (moringin)

- **Chemistry.** Moringa leaves contain glucomoringin. Extracting fresh leaves in water lets the leaf's own myrosinase form rhamnose-bearing benzyl isothiocyanates, at 1.66% of the concentrate. One of them kept 80% stability after 30 days at 37 °C, which is much more stable than sulforaphane, and they were anti-inflammatory in macrophages [phy-waterman2014]. In high-fat-fed mice the concentrate improved glucose tolerance and suppressed hepatic gluconeogenesis [phy-waterman2015].
- **Human glucose data.**
  - A 4 g dose of leaf powder raised insulin secretion by 74% without changing glucose in 10 healthy volunteers. This was an unrandomised dose-escalation study [phy-anthanont2016].
  - Adding 20 g of leaf powder to a meal lowered the post-meal glucose curve in 17 diabetic Saharawi adults but not in 10 healthy ones; the dose was poorly tolerated [phy-leone2018].
  - A 2025 meta-analysis of 9 RCTs (649 participants) found no consistent effect on glucose, lipids or body measures. A small diastolic BP reduction was not robust, and certainty was very low for every outcome [phy-crisan2025].
- **What is missing.** No published human pharmacokinetic or Nrf2-target data exist for moringin.

### Mustard, horseradish and wasabi (allyl isothiocyanate, 6-MSITC)

- **Absorption.** Allyl isothiocyanate (AITC, the pungent principle of mustard, horseradish and wasabi) is about 90% absorbed in animal studies. It is excreted as mercapturic acids, with urinary levels at least 10 times plasma levels, which concentrates exposure in the bladder [phy-zhang2010]. In humans, the main evidence is the cabbage-cooking study above [phy-fuller2007].
- **Memory.** Wasabi's 6-methylsulfinylhexyl isothiocyanate (6-MSITC) improved working and episodic memory, but no other cognitive domain, in a 12-week RCT of 72 adults aged 60+ [phy-nouchi2023].
- **Practical role.** Mustard's main proven use is restoring myrosinase to cooked broccoli; see `diet.md` and the [mustard-seed-myrosinase] factor.

### Rocket, radish and garden cress (erucin, sulforaphene, benzyl ITC)

Erucin (rocket) is the reduced twin of sulforaphane; the two interconvert in the body [phy-clarke2011]. We found no human outcome trials for erucin, radish sulforaphene or garden-cress benzyl isothiocyanate: the literature is cell and animal work. These foods are best treated as part of [cruciferous-other].

### Indole-3-carbinol (I3C) and DIM

- **Pharmacokinetics.** I3C comes from glucobrassicin, the indole glucosinolate in cabbage, broccoli and Brussels sprouts. It is never detected in plasma. In stomach acid it condenses to DIM and other products, and DIM is the only one found in blood. Plasma DIM plateaued above about 1000 mg I3C (Cmax ~607 ng/mL), with large differences between people [phy-reed2006].
- **Estrogen metabolism: consistent.**
  - 6-7 mg/kg/day I3C raised urinary 2-hydroxylated estrogens and lowered estradiol, estrone, estriol and 16α-hydroxyestrone in men and women [phy-michnovicz1997].
  - 400 mg/day raised the 2-hydroxyestrone:estriol ratio for 3 months, though 3 of 20 women did not respond [phy-bradlow1994].
  - From food, each extra 10 g/day of Brassica raised the urinary 2:16 ratio by only 0.08 [phy-fowke2000].
- **Clinical outcomes: thin and mixed.**
  - In 30 women with cervical precancer (CIN II-III), 200 or 400 mg/day I3C for 12 weeks gave complete regression in 8 of 17, against 0 of 10 on placebo [phy-bell2000]. This was a small trial and, as far as we found, has not been replicated.
  - In 130 women on tamoxifen, DIM (150 mg twice daily for 12 months) raised the 2/16 ratio and SHBG but lowered plasma tamoxifen metabolites, including endoxifen. Breast density did not change [phy-thomson2017]. That is a potential harm.
  - US National Toxicology Program two-year studies found clear evidence of carcinogenicity (liver tumours) in male mice and some evidence (uterine adenocarcinoma) in female rats given I3C by gavage [phy-ntp2017].
- **Verdict.** The "estrogen detox" marketing rests on a surrogate ratio. Supplements are a drug-like intervention with safety questions; eating crucifers is not.

## Polyphenols and related compounds

### Quercetin

- **Blood pressure: holds up.** 730 mg/day for 28 days lowered BP by 7/5 mmHg in stage 1 hypertension but not in prehypertension. Plasma and urinary oxidative-stress markers did not change [phy-edwards2007]. A meta-analysis of 7 RCTs found -3.0/-2.6 mmHg overall, confined to doses of 500 mg/day or more (-4.5/-3.0) [phy-serban2016].
- **Respiratory infections: null overall.** In 1002 adults, 500 or 1000 mg/day for 12 weeks did not reduce infection rates or severity. Only a post-hoc subgroup of fit adults aged 40+ on 1000 mg had fewer sick days [phy-heinz2010].
- **Endurance: trivial.** A meta-analysis of 11 studies found a ~2% improvement (effect size 0.15) [phy-kressler2011]. In untrained men, 1000 mg/day for 2 weeks improved a 12-minute time trial by ~3%, with non-significant trends in mitochondrial markers [phy-nieman2010].
- **Senolytic use.** Quercetin's senolytic data come from combination with dasatinib, a leukaemia drug:
  - Three days of D+Q reduced senescent-cell markers in fat and skin of 9 patients with diabetic kidney disease, in an open-label study [phy-hickson2019].
  - An open-label pilot in pulmonary fibrosis improved walking measures but not lung function [phy-justice2019].
  - Quercetin was not detected in cerebrospinal fluid in a 5-patient Alzheimer's pilot [phy-gonzales2023].
  - The one phase 2 RCT, in 60 postmenopausal women, missed its primary bone endpoint [phy-farr2024].
- **Safety.** Supplements up to 1000 mg/day have few reported adverse effects, but long-term safety data at high doses are missing, and drug interactions and kidney concerns exist [phy-andres2018].
- **Food vs supplement.** Total flavonol plus flavone intake in Japanese women averaged 16.7 mg/day, mostly from onions [phy-arai2000]. The effective BP dose is 30 or more times that.

### Fisetin and the senolytic story

- **Mouse data.** Fisetin was the most potent senolytic among 10 flavonoids. Late-life dosing reduced senescence markers and extended median and maximum lifespan in one set of mouse experiments [phy-yousefzadeh2018].
- **Mouse data that disagree.** The NIA Interventions Testing Program, which tests compounds in genetically mixed mice at three sites, found that fisetin did not extend lifespan in either sex [phy-harrison2024].
- **Human trial status.** The Mayo AFFIRM-LITE trial gives 20 mg/kg/day for 2 consecutive days (about 1.4 g for a 70 kg adult). It began in 2018 and was still listed as enrolling, with primary completion estimated for November 2026, when we checked [phy-nct03675724]. The only published fisetin RCTs we found are:
  - a 60-man trial of continuous 200 mg/day fisetin plus training that measured adipokines [phy-alipour2026];
  - a stroke trial of fisetin added to thrombolysis [phy-wang2019].

  Neither tested senolysis.
- **Caution from the field.** Leading senolytic researchers wrote that it is too early to use senolytics outside clinical trials [phy-kirkland2020].
- **Food.** Strawberries are often promoted as a fisetin source, but all dietary flavonols and flavones together come to tens of milligrams a day [phy-arai2000], about 1% of a single trial dose.

### Green tea catechins (EGCG)

- **Brewed tea.** The diet chapter already covers the main oxidative-stress results: 31% lower urinary 8-OHdG in smokers drinking 4 cups/day. More broadly, flavan-3-ol interventions, tea included, lower BP [phy-lagou2025]. In the UK Biobank, drinking 2 or more cups a day of mostly black tea was associated with 9-13% lower all-cause mortality [phy-inouechoi2022].
- **Extracts are a different exposure.**
  - 800 mg/day EGCG for 4 weeks was tolerated in 40 healthy adults, but free EGCG exposure rose by more than 60% with once-daily bolus dosing [phy-chow2003].
  - In a 12-month RCT in 1021 women, high-dose green tea extract raised ALT and AST, and 5.1% developed moderate or worse liver abnormalities (OR 7.0), with positive rechallenge [phy-yu2017].
- **Exercise.** In sprinters, 980 mg/day of green tea polyphenols prevented the post-sprint MDA rise. It did not affect performance or appear to blunt antioxidant-enzyme adaptation [phy-jowko2015].

### Cocoa flavanols

- **Which compound matters.** Epicatechin, not the more abundant procyanidins, drives the vascular effect. A cocoa extract with 130 mg epicatechin improved FMD, BP and pulse wave velocity; a procyanidin-rich extract with only 20 mg epicatechin did not [phy-rodriguezmateos2018].
- **Oxidative stress.** A high-flavanol drink prevented the rise in GC-MS-measured F2-isoprostanes seen after a low-flavanol drink, most clearly with exercise [phy-wiswedel2004].
- **Acute stress.** 150 mg epicatechin taken 1.5 h before a mental stress task prevented the stress-induced fall in FMD [phy-baynham2021].
- **Peripheral artery disease.** In 44 patients, 15 g cocoa (75 mg epicatechin) daily for 6 months improved 6-minute walk by 42.6 m when measured 2.5 h after a dose. At 24 h the gain was 18 m and not significant. Calf mitochondrial activity and capillary density also improved [phy-mcdermott2020].
- **Cognition: mostly null.**
  - COSMOS-Web (3562 older adults, 500 mg flavanols/day) missed its primary memory endpoint at 1 year. Memory did improve in people with the lowest habitual flavanol intake or diet quality [phy-brickman2023].
  - COSMOS-Clinic (573 participants, in-person testing) found no effect on global cognition, memory or executive function over 2 years [phy-vyas2024].
- **Interpretation.** Cocoa flavanols look like a "repletion" nutrient for vascular and memory function in people who eat few of them, not an enhancer for everyone.

### Anthocyanins and berries

- **Bioavailability.** Anthocyanins are among the least-absorbed polyphenols [phy-manach2005], and their benefits track microbial metabolites (see `diet.md` for the blueberry FMD trials).
- **Cognition.**
  - In 35 adults aged 40-65, a single 1-cup-equivalent wild blueberry drink kept memory and executive function from declining over an 8-hour test day and lowered post-meal glucose and insulin [phy-whyte2021].
  - In 86 older adults with mild cognitive decline, 6 months of wild blueberry powder improved processing speed [phy-cheatham2023].
- **Caveat.** Both trials are small, and the cognitive tests are many.

### Olive oil phenolics (hydroxytyrosol, oleocanthal)

- **Strongest evidence.** The strong evidence for high-phenolic EVOO (EUROLIVE oxidized LDL, PREDIMED events) is in `diet.md`.
- **A newer trial was weaker.** In the 50-person OLIVAUS crossover, 60 mL/day of high- (320 mg/kg) vs low-polyphenol (86 mg/kg) oil for 3 weeks produced **no significant between-treatment differences**. Oxidized LDL fell and antioxidant capacity rose only within the high-polyphenol arm, more so in people with abdominal obesity [phy-sarapis2022].
- **Oleocanthal.** This compound gives fresh EVOO its throat sting and inhibits cyclooxygenase like ibuprofen in vitro [phy-beauchamp2005]. Whether culinary amounts have a drug-like anti-inflammatory effect is untested.

### Resveratrol and pterostilbene

- **Positive small trials.**
  - 150 mg/day for 30 days produced calorie-restriction-like metabolic changes in 11 obese men [phy-timmers2011].
  - 10 mg/day lowered HOMA-IR and urinary ortho-tyrosine in 19 people with diabetes [phy-brasnyo2011].
  - 200 mg/day for 26 weeks improved word retention and lowered HbA1c in older adults [phy-witte2014].
- **Null or negative trials.**
  - High-dose resveratrol for 4 weeks had no effect on insulin sensitivity, BP or inflammation in 24 obese men [phy-poulsen2013].
  - In the 52-week Alzheimer's trial (up to 1 g twice daily), brain-volume loss was greater on resveratrol [phy-turner2015].
- **Meta-analyses.** No overall BP effect, with reductions only at 300 mg/day or more and in diabetes [phy-fogacci2019]. An umbrella review graded nearly all metabolic effects as trivial or of very low certainty and concluded that the evidence does not support supplementation [phy-zeraattalab2021].
- **Pterostilbene.** 125 mg twice daily lowered BP by 7.8/7.3 mmHg but **raised LDL by 17 mg/dL** in people with high cholesterol [phy-riche2014]. Safety markers were otherwise clean up to 250 mg/day [phy-riche2013].

### Ellagitannins and urolithin A

- **Preclinical.** Urolithin A induces mitophagy, extended lifespan in worms, and improved exercise capacity in aged rodents [phy-ryu2016].
- **Human trials.** All were manufacturer-supported, testing the Mitopure product.
  - *Phase 1, older adults.* Up to 1000 mg/day for 4 weeks was safe, bioavailable and induced muscle mitochondrial gene expression [phy-andreux2019].
  - *Older adults, 4 months (n=66).* 1000 mg/day improved hand and leg muscle endurance and lowered CRP, ceramides and acylcarnitines. The primary endpoints, 6-minute walk and maximal ATP production, were **not** significant [phy-liu2022].
  - *Middle-aged adults, 4 months.* 500-1000 mg/day raised muscle strength by ~12% and improved VO2peak and 6-minute walk, but not the primary endpoint, peak power [phy-singh2022].
  - *Trained runners, 4 weeks (n=42).* 1000 mg/day lowered creatine kinase and perceived exertion, but time-trial performance and VO2max did not differ from placebo [phy-whitfield2025].
  - *Immune markers, 4 weeks (n=50).* 1000 mg/day expanded naive-like CD8+ T cells and their fatty-acid oxidation capacity [phy-denk2025].
- **Verdict.** A consistent pattern of modest benefit on secondary endpoints. It is promising but not yet established, and no trial has measured oxidative damage.

### Lignans (flaxseed)

- **Blood pressure.** In 110 patients with peripheral artery disease, 30 g/day of milled flaxseed for 6 months lowered systolic BP by about 10 mmHg and diastolic by 7 mmHg. In those with hypertension at baseline, systolic fell by 15 mmHg. Plasma ALA tracked both pressures; lignans tracked diastolic [phy-rodriguezleyva2013].
- **Mortality.** In PREDIMED, lignan intake was one of the polyphenol subclasses most strongly associated with lower mortality (HR 0.60) [phy-tresserrarimbau2014].

### Isoflavones and equol

- **Hormones.** Soy protein and isoflavones do not change testosterone, free testosterone, estradiol, estrone or SHBG in men, across 41 studies, regardless of dose or duration [phy-reed2021].
- **Responder status.** The equol-producer split above is the key unknown when interpreting soy trials [phy-setchell2002, phy-frankenfeld2014].

### Hesperidin (citrus)

- **Vascular trials.** 500 mL/day of orange juice, or a control drink with the same hesperidin, lowered diastolic BP over 4 weeks and improved post-meal microvascular reactivity in 24 overweight men [phy-morand2011]. 500 mg/day hesperidin for 3 weeks raised FMD (10.3% vs 7.8%) and lowered CRP in metabolic syndrome [phy-rizza2011].
- **Meta-analysis.** Modest reductions in fasting glucose, lipids, systolic BP and TNF-α, clearer above 500 mg/day and beyond 12 weeks [phy-heidari2025].

### Chlorogenic acids

Coffee is the dominant source and is covered in `diet.md`, so no new data are added here.

### Spermidine

- **Animal data.** Spermidine extended mouse lifespan and protected the ageing heart. The protection depended on autophagy and vanished in mice lacking Atg5 in heart cells [phy-eisenberg2016]. Reviews frame it as a caloric-restriction mimetic [phy-madeo2018].
- **Cohort data.** In an Austrian cohort of 829 adults followed for 20 years, each 1-SD higher dietary spermidine intake was associated with 24% lower mortality (HR 0.76), replicated in a second cohort [phy-kiechl2018]. Spermidine-rich diets are also rich in whole grains, legumes and vegetables, so confounding is likely.
- **Trials.**
  - A 3-month pilot (30 adults) suggested better mnemonic discrimination, but the confidence interval touched zero [phy-wirth2018].
  - The 12-month phase 2b trial (100 adults, 0.9 mg/day from wheat-germ extract) found no effect on memory or biomarkers [phy-schwarz2022].
- **Dose context.** Total polyamine intake from ordinary food is around 316-541 µmol/day [phy-ali2011], which dwarfs the supplement dose.

### Astaxanthin

- **Biomarker trials.** A meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (380 participants) found lower blood MDA (SMD -0.95), and lower IL-6 in type 2 diabetes, but no change in CRP or TNF-α [phy-ma2022]. In 42 young women, 2-8 mg/day for 8 weeks lowered a DNA-damage marker and CRP but not lipid peroxidation [phy-park2010]. In young Finnish men, 8 mg/day lowered hydroxy fatty acids within the treated group [phy-karppi2007].
- **Mouse lifespan.** In the NIA Interventions Testing Program, astaxanthin extended median lifespan in male mice by 12% (not in females) [phy-harrison2024]. That is a rare positive ITP result, but it is not human evidence.

## Hormesis, xenohormesis and whether Nrf2 activators add up

- **Concept.** Xenohormesis [phy-howitz2008] and phytochemical hormesis [phy-son2008] give a coherent reason why bitter, pungent, "defensive" plant compounds might help: they are mild stressors.
- **Human proof is missing.** We found no trial showing that plants grown under stress, or "more bitter" foods, produce better health outcomes.
- **Additivity of Nrf2 activators.**
  - *Cell data only.* In liver cells, I3C or DIM combined with PEITC or sulforaphane induced Nrf2-ARE activity synergistically or additively [phy-saw2011].
  - *No human data.* No human study has tested whether moringa plus watercress plus broccoli sprouts gives more Nrf2 target induction than one of them.
  - *Likely limits.* Nrf2 signalling saturates, and strong chronic activation raises unresolved safety questions [phy-cuadrado2019].
- **Practical stance.** Compounds that share a mechanism (several ITC sources) are partly redundant; compounds with different mechanisms (an ITC food, epicatechin, hesperidin, flaxseed lignans) are complementary. This matches the saturation analysis in `diet.md`.

## Exercise interactions

- **Vitamins C and E.** Vitamin C 1000 mg plus vitamin E 400 IU blocked exercise-induced gains in insulin sensitivity and in PGC-1α and antioxidant-enzyme expression [phy-ristow2009]. A review found blunting most consistent with high-intensity and resistance training, and no evidence that antioxidant supplements enhance adaptation [phy-merry2016].
- **Resveratrol: the clearest polyphenol example of blunting.**
  - In 27 men aged ~65, 250 mg/day during 8 weeks of high-intensity training cut the VO2max gain by 45%. It also abolished training improvements in blood pressure, LDL and triglycerides [phy-gliemann2013].
  - In the same programme, resveratrol blunted training-induced falls in muscle protein carbonylation and TNF-α [phy-olesen2014].
  - In young men, 150 mg/day during HIIT blunted peak-power gains and PGC-1α/SIRT1/SOD2 induction [phy-scribbans2014].
- **But not always.** In 65-80-year-olds, 500 mg/day enhanced the muscle response to 12 weeks of combined training (mitochondrial density, torque, fibre area) [phy-alway2017]. A pilot in functionally limited older adults was supportive [phy-harper2021]. Age, dose, training type and baseline function seem to matter.
- **Other polyphenols: no blunting signal.**
  - *Quercetin.* 1000 mg/day for 2 weeks showed a small performance gain and non-significant increases in mitochondrial-biogenesis markers [phy-nieman2010].
  - *Green tea.* Polyphenols did not appear to blunt enzyme adaptation [phy-jowko2015].
  - *Watercress* [phy-fogarty2013], *cocoa* [phy-wiswedel2004] and *urolithin A* [phy-whitfield2025] lowered exercise-induced damage markers.

  None of these was a long training study powered for adaptation.
- **Practical stance.** Avoid resveratrol (and high-dose C/E) around training if adaptation is the goal. Food polyphenols show no evidence of harm. The common advice to separate supplements from training by a few hours is reasonable but untested.

## Fertility (sperm and egg)

Most fertility claims for these compounds rest on animal work. The human data:

**Egg / IVF**

- **Astaxanthin: three small RCTs with improved embryology but no proven births.**
  - *PCOS.* 8 mg/day for 40 days raised serum antioxidant capacity and granulosa-cell Nrf2 targets, and increased MII-oocyte and top-quality embryo rates. Pregnancy rates did not differ [phy-gharaei2022].
  - *Poor responders.* 12 mg/day for 8 weeks increased oocytes, MII oocytes and high-quality embryos [phy-shafie2024].
  - *Endometriosis.* 6 mg/day for 12 weeks gave similar embryology improvements [phy-rostami2023].
  - *Grade:* limited; embryology endpoints are surrogates.
- **Soy isoflavones: observational only.** In a fertility-clinic cohort of 315 women, higher dietary isoflavone intake was associated with higher ART live-birth rates (OR about 1.8) [phy-vanegas2015]. Grade: limited.
- **Resveratrol: no outcome benefit and a signal of harm.** A resveratrol-based multivitamin for 3 months before ICSI increased oocytes, fertilisation and embryos, but not pregnancy or live birth [phy-gerli2022]. The existing [egg-resveratrol] entry documents lower pregnancy and more miscarriage with resveratrol taken during embryo-transfer cycles, so this is not a recommendation.
- **Spermidine: mice only.** It restored oocyte quality and fertility in aged mice by enhancing mitophagy [phy-zhang2023]. There are no human data.
- **Fisetin, quercetin, urolithin A, moringa:** we found no human egg-quality trials.

**Sperm**

- **Pomegranate extract.** In one 70-man RCT, pomegranate extract plus galangal for 3 months raised total motile sperm by 62%, against 20% on placebo [phy-fedder2014]. Grade: limited (single trial, combined product, no pregnancy data).
- **Resveratrol.** An uncontrolled 20-man pilot of a multivitamin with 150 mg/day resveratrol reported motility rising from 48% to 59% [phy-illiano2020]. Without a placebo group, this is essentially uninterpretable.
- **Soy.** It does not lower male reproductive hormones [phy-reed2021]; see the existing [soy-sperm] factor.
- **Astaxanthin.** See the existing [astaxanthin-sperm] factor.
- **Quercetin, fisetin, moringa, urolithin A, spermidine:** animal data only, as far as we found.

## Leads from Rhonda Patrick's content and how they held up

Patrick's FoundMyFitness pages are generally careful to label preclinical work, and several of her summaries match the primary papers well. Where they overreach, it is usually by quoting a secondary endpoint or a subgroup as the headline, or by giving a mechanism more weight than the human outcome data support.

| Claim / lead (from her topic pages, episodes or posts) | What the primary evidence shows | Grade | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urolithin A (500-1000 mg/day) raises leg strength ~10-12% and VO2peak in middle-aged adults and improves endurance in older adults | True for secondary endpoints. Primary endpoints were missed in both main trials [phy-singh2022, phy-liu2022]. No performance gain in trained runners [phy-whitfield2025]. All trials were manufacturer-funded | limited | Partly holds: the effect sizes are accurate but come from secondary outcomes |
| Urolithin A "rejuvenates" immune cells (naive CD8+ T cells, NK cells, monocytes) | 4-week RCT in 50 adults showed these phenotype shifts as primary or secondary outcomes [phy-denk2025]. No clinical (infection) endpoints | limited | Holds as stated; clinical meaning unknown |
| Only a minority of people make much urolithin A from food | 5-25% make none and 10-50% make a mix, while 25-80% make only urolithin A [phy-tomasbarberan2014]. Metabotype shifts with age [phy-cortesmartin2018] | moderate (descriptive) | Roughly right; "minority" depends on the trial population |
| Fisetin is a senolytic (strawberries richest source) | One mouse lifespan study [phy-yousefzadeh2018] was not reproduced by the NIA ITP [phy-harrison2024]. No human senolytic RCT results [phy-nct03675724]. Dietary amounts are milligrams [phy-arai2000]. Her senescence page notes the lack of human trials, which is fair | mechanistic | Overreach whenever it is implied to be actionable, especially via strawberries |
| Dasatinib + quercetin clears senescent cells; extends mouse lifespan | Senescent cells fell in a 9-patient open-label study [phy-hickson2019]. The only RCT missed its primary endpoint [phy-farr2024]. Says nothing about quercetin alone | limited | Holds for the drug combination in pilots only |

| Quercetin lowers BP, especially above 500 mg/day | Confirmed by meta-analysis (-4.5/-3.0 mmHg at 500 mg/day or more) [phy-serban2016, phy-edwards2007] | limited-moderate | Holds |
| Quercetin raises endurance by only ~3% (trivial) | Meta-analysis: ~2%, trivial-to-small [phy-kressler2011] | moderate | Holds (fairly framed) |
| Quercetin bioavailability is ~1% | Absorption is 24-52% depending on form [phy-hollman1995]. What is low is the unconjugated parent compound in plasma | n/a | Misleading figure, though the practical point (low plasma levels) stands |
| Spermidine improves cognition via autophagy; spermidine-rich foods linked to better brain function | 3-month pilot borderline [phy-wirth2018]; 12-month trial null [phy-schwarz2022]. | Moringa is a viable alternative to broccoli sprouts; moringin as potent as sulforaphane | Moringa ITCs are unusually stable [phy-waterman2014], but potency comparisons are cell-based and there are no human Nrf2 or PK data | mechanistic | Unproven |

## Practical summary: ranking by human evidence x effect

Ranking considers the quality of human evidence, the size of the effect on a meaningful outcome (vascular function, BP, validated oxidative-damage markers), and safety.

| Rank | Compound / food | Best human evidence | Typical effect | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cocoa flavanols (high-flavanol cocoa or extract, ~200-500 mg flavanols) | 145-RCT flavan-3-ol meta-analysis; COSMOS [phy-lagou2025, phy-brickman2023] | BP -2.8/-2.0 mmHg; FMD +1.7-2 points; no CVD or cognition benefit overall | strong |
| 2 | Tea (green or black), 2-4 cups | Flavan-3-ol meta-analysis; UK Biobank [phy-lagou2025, phy-inouechoi2022] | Small BP drop; 9-13% lower mortality (observational) | moderate |
| 3 | High-phenolic EVOO | EUROLIVE/PREDIMED (`diet.md`); OLIVAUS weaker [phy-sarapis2022] | Lower oxidized LDL; CVD events -30% in pattern | strong (diet.md) |
| 4 | Berries / anthocyanins (blueberries ~150 g/day) | `diet.md` FMD trials; small cognitive RCTs [phy-whyte2021, phy-cheatham2023] | FMD +1.45 points; small cognitive gains | moderate |
| 5 | Milled flaxseed 30 g/day | One 110-patient 6-month RCT [phy-rodriguezleyva2013] | SBP -10 mmHg | limited (large effect) |
| 6 | Watercress ~85 g/day raw | 60-person RCT [phy-gill2007]; smoker studies [phy-hecht1995, phy-yuan2016] | DNA damage -17 to -24% | limited |
| 7 | Quercetin 500-1000 mg/day (supplement) | Meta of 7 RCTs [phy-serban2016] | BP -4.5/-3.0 mmHg | limited |
| 8 | Citrus hesperidin (oranges; 500 mg/day) | Small crossover RCTs, meta [phy-rizza2011, phy-heidari2025] | FMD +2.5 points; DBP down | limited |
| 9 | Urolithin A 500-1000 mg/day (supplement) | 4 manufacturer RCTs [phy-liu2022, phy-singh2022, phy-denk2025, phy-whitfield2025] | Muscle endurance/strength ~10-12% (secondary endpoints) | limited |
| 10 | Astaxanthin 2-12 mg/day (supplement) | Meta of 12 small RCTs [phy-ma2022] | MDA down; CRP unchanged | limited |
| Low | Moringa leaf powder | Null meta-analysis [phy-crisan2025] | None consistent | limited |
| Low | Spermidine supplements | Null 12-month RCT [phy-schwarz2022] | None | limited |
| Avoid / caution | Resveratrol, pterostilbene | Trivial effects [phy-zeraattalab2021]; training blunting [phy-gliemann2013]; LDL rise [phy-riche2014] | Net null or harmful for trainees | moderate (negative) |
| Avoid / caution | I3C/DIM supplements | Surrogate shifts; tamoxifen interaction; NTP tumours [phy-thomson2017, phy-ntp2017] | Unknown benefit, plausible harm | limited |
| Not yet | Fisetin | No human senolytic results [phy-nct03675724, phy-harrison2024] | Unknown | mechanistic |

**Simple daily pattern consistent with this evidence:** tea or coffee, a serving of berries, an orange, a tablespoon or two of high-phenolic EVOO, a large handful of watercress (85 g in the trial) or other raw crucifers, and a couple of tablespoons of milled flaxseed. Add a high-flavanol cocoa drink if you want the vascular effect (the peripheral artery disease trial used 15 g cocoa a day [phy-mcdermott2020]). This delivers several distinct mechanisms with no supplement risks, and it matches the cohort finding that variety of flavonoid sources matters independently of quantity [phy-parmenter2025].

## What we don't know

- **Metabotype-stratified effects.** Whether urolithin, equol or enterolactone producers get the benefits that non-producers miss has rarely been tested prospectively [phy-espin2017, phy-setchell2002].
- **Nrf2 additivity in humans.** Do stacked ITC sources (watercress, moringa, mustard, sprouts) add up, plateau, or overshoot? Only cell data exist [phy-saw2011, phy-cuadrado2019].
- **Fisetin in humans.** AFFIRM-LITE and similar trials have not reported [phy-nct03675724].
- **Long-term safety of high-dose isolated compounds** (quercetin >12 weeks [phy-andres2018], DIM [phy-thomson2017], EGCG [phy-yu2017]).
- **Independent replication of urolithin A trials** and any hard outcome (falls, frailty, hospitalisation).
- **Polyphenols and training adaptation over months**, beyond resveratrol [phy-merry2016].
- **Fertility outcomes.** Live-birth data for astaxanthin, and any controlled human sperm trial for resveratrol, urolithin A or spermidine.
- **Moringin pharmacokinetics** and Nrf2 induction in humans.
